The memory trade is running straight into Nvidia's report

Micron and Marvell are back at the highs on a DRAM spike, Nvidia limps into earnings down six straight days, and fuel cells and healthcare carry the non-AI bid.

SemisAi-InfraEnergyHealthcareConsumerEtfs

Yesterday the theme was the consumer cracking and the semis going quiet. Today the semis answered: memory is the loudest trade in the market, DRAM pricing is at records, and the whole complex is leaning into Nvidia's report next week. The broad tape slipped on the lingering Walmart drag, but the AI rotation into the enablers is on, and two non-AI pockets, fuel cells and healthcare, are carrying real bids of their own.

Market context. Per the Kalshi list, the S&P fell about 0.9 percent with the Dow about 1.3 percent as the Walmart read kept dragging consumer names. Per the Polymarket list, Nvidia fell a sixth straight day, its worst losing streak in four years, into its August 26 report. The memory names went the other way: Micron reclaimed its 50-day and Marvell kept its bid, Per the People list while Merck touched an all-time high and Moderna stayed near its squeeze. Gold and Bitcoin running is the macro wind at the AI trade's back.

The scoreboard. The CoreWeave put-selling thesis hit its August 21 expiry with no confirmation from today's sources: no $80 print, and CoreWeave stayed the most-battered name in the AI-infra complex into its report. I am marking the short-put entry expired rather than claimed, because nothing in today's tape settled the level. The call's substance, that the AI-infra names got oversold into the financing unwind, is the same thesis the Citadel clearance supports, so the idea lives on even though the dated put is dead.

1. Memory is the momentum story and it is now a pricing story. Per the Stocks list, DRAM contract pricing is up 700 percent year over year and spot NAND is running roughly 7x its prior high, with the memory makers passing through record take-or-pay contracts that lock in revenue through 2030. Per the Stocks list, Micron reclaimed its 50-day at 963 and cleared the 972 resistance, and Per the Stocks list the next levels are 1022 then 1037 to 1051. Per the Micron video, HBM supply is secured for all of 2026, and Per the People list JPMorgan sees DRAM and NAND revenue near $1.8 trillion in 2028. The buybacks are the tell: SK Hynix authorized a roughly $28.7 billion repurchase that cancels shares, Samsung a package near $72 billion, and SanDisk added $14 billion. When the memory makers themselves are buying back hard into a pricing supercycle, the trade has a floor under it.

2. Marvell is the custom-silicon survivor and it just guided up. Per the Marvell video, Marvell guided second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue to about $2.7 billion, up 35 percent year over year, and raised its outlook on AI bookings, trading at roughly 75 times earnings against a Broadcom at 71 times, with the analysts still carrying upside in their price targets. Its edge is the memory-pooling and custom-silicon portfolio it handed to Google and others, the same CXL and photonic-fabric demand that is lifting the memory complex. At this valuation it is a momentum hold, not a value buy, but the guide is the proof the AI custom-chip demand is real.

3. Power and fuel cells are the next bottleneck and Bloom is sold out. Per the Daniel Koss piece, data-center moratoriums are spreading, 269 since January 2023, and Bloom Energy is reportedly sold out into 2029 to 2030, leaving FuelCell Energy as the next name. FCEL trades near $18 with a market cap just over $1.6 billion and a bull case to roughly $80 on 500 megawatts of capacity by mid-2028. Per the power video, the bigger theme is Nvidia moving to an 800-volt architecture where Infineon is the top pick, down about 40 percent from its June high, and Onsemi's per-rack content rising from $15,000 to $115,000. Power is the trade that shows up once the AI capex numbers are real, and this run's sources keep pointing at the same few names.

4. The Citadel-Situational Awareness unwind cleared the AI overhang. Per the People list, Ken Griffin sold about 80 percent of the risk his firm absorbed from the collapse of Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund, roughly $4 billion in block trades, and the holdings were the same high-beta AI names the drawdown punished: SanDisk at 28 percent of the book, Micron at 27.5 percent, then Bloom, TSMC and Nebius. The overhang that suppressed these names is clearing, a structural reason the AI-infra complex is finding a bid beyond the memory story.

5. Healthcare is the non-AI bid and it is real. Per the People list, Morgan Stanley upgraded Merck to Overweight with a $179 price target, about 54 percent above its prior level, after the mRNA cancer-vaccine read, and Merck touched an all-time high, up 15.8 percent on the week. Moderna held most of its historic squeeze, Per the People list and Cramer called Celsius the winner over Coke while the healthcare complex kept its bid. This is the clearest rotation pocket outside the AI trade, and the breadth the edition needs to carry.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $MU. Back above its 50-day at 963, next resistance 1022 then 1037 to 1051. Memory pricing at records with the buybacks as a floor.
  • $MRVL. Guided revenue up 35 percent and raised the outlook. Expensive, but the custom-silicon demand is proven.
  • $NVDA. Down six straight, its worst streak in four years, into the August 26 report. The whole semis tape trades off the guide.
  • $FCEL. The next fuel-cell name after Bloom sold out. Bull case to roughly $80 on 500 megawatts by mid-2028.
  • $BE. Sold out into 2029 to 2030 on the data-center power shortage. The leader of the fuel-cell shortage trade.
  • $MRK. Upgraded to $179, an all-time high after the vaccine read. The healthcare bid that balances the AI-heavy tape.

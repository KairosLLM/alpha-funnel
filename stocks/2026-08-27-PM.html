Nvidia printed $96.2 billion and guided $108 billion

Revenue was $96.2 billion versus $92.2 billion expected, up 106 percent, the Q3 guide is $108.0 billion plus or minus 2 percent, and the stock went minus 2 percent after hours then plus 6 percent at the open.

SemisAi-InfraSoftware

This is the print. Wednesday was the estimate. Per the call, fiscal second-quarter revenue came in at $96.2 billion versus $92.2 billion expected, up 106 percent year over year against $46 billion a year ago. Adjusted EPS $2.22 versus $2.10, up 120 percent.

I do not have an 8-K. These figures are Najarro's call plus the recap.

Market context. First print after hours, the stock dropped roughly 2 percent on the gross-margin guide. Najarro later has it up roughly 4.7 percent. 0xResearch has the chip leader gapping 6 percent into Thursday's open, Nasdaq 100 up 1 percent.

1. Data center did the work. Memory takes the guide. Per the Stocks AM recap, data center $89.0 billion versus $85.8 billion expected, up 117 percent year over year and 18 percent on the quarter. Najarro splits that as hyperscalers about $49 billion and ACE (neocloud, industrial, and enterprise, the non-hyperscaler bucket) $40 billion, with ACE up 25 percent on the quarter and 138 percent year over year.

Adjusted gross margin 75.0 percent, in line, up 250 basis points year over year. Q3 gross margin guide 74.0 percent plus or minus 50 basis points. Najarro's memory tape is prices up 80 percent and 60 percent, Micron up 60 percent and 70 percent on the quarter, and a margin hit of only 2 percentage points. Q3 revenue guide $108.0 billion plus or minus 2 percent versus $104.2 billion on the street. Buy-side was around $109 billion.

Adjusted operating income $64.0 billion, adjusted net income $54.0 billion, free cash flow $21.3 billion versus $13 billion a year ago. Returned about $26.0 billion in the quarter, about $99.0 billion left on the authorization. Outlook: no Data Center compute revenue from China.

2. Fiscal 2028 is the number the street did not have. Najarro: revenue grows approximately 70 percent next year, supply constrained. If this year is about $400 billion, 70 percent is about $680 billion versus consensus around $570 to $580 billion, a beat of over $100 billion on what the market was expecting. Vera CPU in full production, CPU revenue more than doubling in fiscal 2028 against roughly $20 billion of total server CPU demand.

Production shipments of Vera Rubin began earlier this month. Neocloud partners exit the year with 8 gigawatts, up from roughly 3 gigawatts at the end of 2025. Cloud industry backlog now exceeds $2 trillion. Top-five hyperscaler capex nearly $800 billion in 2026 and $1.3 trillion in 2027.

They invested nearly $50 billion in frontier AI labs, and AI-lab demand supported by the balance sheet is about 25 percent of the business next year. That $50 billion is money into labs. Commitments went from $119 billion last quarter to $279 billion, primarily memory, $366 billion total at Nvidia plus $56 billion in other AI cloud agreements, and OpenAI land and power about $108 billion. The Information, via the recap, also has Nvidia agreeing to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion. Treat that as a rumor.

3. The banker stack is leases. Per the WSJ writeup, Nvidia backstops some $230 billion in lease obligations and residual-value deals, including a $105 billion backstop for the OpenAI Ohio lease and potentially up to $125 billion of residual-value support. CoreWeave has a $6.3 billion deal to buy unsold capacity. The byline snapshot of Nvidia minus 2.91 percent is the pre-print tape.

4. CrowdStrike and Okta reported after the bell. Per Hogue, CrowdStrike aftermarket plus 11 percent, already up 14 percent on the day. The recap is cleaner: CrowdStrike fiscal Q2 revenue $1.47 billion versus $1.44 billion, up 26 percent. Adjusted EPS $0.31 versus $0.29, up 35 percent. Free cash flow $377.4 million versus $353 million, up 33 percent.

Net new ARR (the new annualized subscription dollars) a record $333 million, up 51 percent. Total ARR $5.84 billion, up 25 percent. Full-year guide $5.99 to $6.01 billion of revenue, $1.25 to $1.26 adjusted EPS, ARR $6.60 to $6.61 billion. CrowdStrike up 10 percent after hours. Okta shares surged 20 percent in extended trading. Recap has the beat at 105 versus 96 cents on EPS.

5. Nebius printed real contracts. IREN got Microsoft to accept Horizon 1. Per Rick Orford, Nebius has four major AI cloud contracts, average total contract value more than $1 billion each, priced at $20 to $25 million per megawatt. AI cloud revenue up 514 percent to $575 million, 454 percent year over year, annualized run rate $3 billion. AI cloud adjusted EBITDA margin 50 percent in Q2. Inference more than tripled. Use $575 million and $3 billion. Ignore the ASR "half a trillion" slip in the same paragraph.

Orford on IREN: Microsoft accepted Horizon 1 inside a $9.7 billion, 5-year contract. Horizon 1 is the first of four 50-megawatt sites in 2026, 200 megawatts total. Four facilities around $1.9 billion in average annual revenue once fully on. Nvidia gave them exemplary cloud status. 52-week range from $17.76, the stock is up 103 percent, 25 percent over the past month.

Prior $2.8 billion of new AI cloud contracts, guidance $3.7 billion to more than $4 billion. Market cap around $16 billion, more than $17 for every dollar of sales. ARR target a top $4 billion by end of 2026, 85 percent already under contract. The title's 180 percent upside is not in the spoken numbers. The body has an analyst range of 10 and 150 percent.

Washington owns twelve public companies and just told you which ones

Thirty government equity stakes in a year, Redwire's defence revenue up 380 percent, and the memory complex sitting 115 percent above a mean it has never failed to revert through.

IndustrialsEnergySemisAi-Infra

This morning's memo said to let the SpaceX calendar work. The more interesting list this weekend is the one where the government is already on the register, because twelve of those names are public and most of them have run.

The week ahead. Friday closed at the S&P's 26th record high of 2026, up over 14 percent for the year after being down 7 percent on March 30. Macro has Wednesday's CPI tonight.

The counterweight is positioning, not fundamentals. Per the Financial Markets list, Heisenberg has this on pace for one of the best Augusts in 41 years, and the only two comparables both broke: September 1986 fell 10 percent peak to trough and September 2020 fell 8.4. Per the Financial Markets morning list, Barchart has Bank of America's Bull and Bear indicator at one of the strongest stock market sell signals this century. Benjamin Cowen expects a correction to begin by late September.

The scoreboard.

  • Due Monday. Grab reports. The July 10 call was the January $3.50/$4.50 call spread with options pricing almost no move into the print.
  • Due Tuesday, and it has already run. Supermicro. Per Joseph Hogue, $SMCI is up 31 percent last week and 19 percent on the month into a print carrying a $60 billion-plus backlog, gross margin guided as high as 17 percent against roughly half that on the Street, and FY revenue near $40 billion on 80 percent growth. The criminal probe and the underwater $27.50 raise I flagged on July 28 have been swamped by the numbers.
  • Under pressure for a second day. My 60/40 that memory multiples expand before they compress. Section three is the case against me.

The forward hook is the part nobody is trading. Washington wants domestic refining capacity back and is looking at reopening a refinery closed in 2021 that was built for Venezuelan heavy sour crude. The names under that are $VLO, the largest pure-play refiner and up 123 percent on the year, and $MPC, larger on capacity but diluted by MPLX.

This is the cleanest non-AI thesis available right now, and its edge is that the buyer has already announced itself.

The contracts are named: over $20 million of Marine Corps orders, a $15 million Army follow-on that is the third in eight months, and a multi-year high-eight-figure Penguin award to a NATO country. It was also selected onto the MDA SHIELD vehicle tied to Golden Dome, though that is multi-award and guarantees no revenue.

The honest gaps. Net loss was $76.5 million, over $44 million of it non-recurring including a $42.5 million Edge Autonomy stock-comp charge. Adjusted EBITDA is minus $9.2 million, free cash flow is negative, and the share count is rising to fund the transformation. No price, no multiple and no target, so this is a thesis rather than an entry.

I am 60/40 that the defence segment passes the space segment in revenue before year end. A 1.92 book-to-bill does not reverse in two quarters.

3. The memory bull case is now arguing with the chart, and the chart has history on its side. The year-to-date table is the bull case in one column. Per Evan of StockMKTNewz, fifteen S&P names have more than doubled this year and the storage and memory complex owns the top of it: $SNDK up 410.7 percent, $DELL up 260.5, $MU up 207.5, $STX up 195.1 and $WDC up 152.1.

Then per the Stocks list, the mean-reversion case. Every time the semiconductor index or $SMH has traded more than 100 percent above its 200-week average, it has reverted through the mean rather than stalling above it. Three completed instances: the index at 272 percent above in 2000, 104 percent in 2021, and $SMH at 113 percent in 2021. $SMH peaked near 180 percent above in June and still sits near 115 percent after the pullback.

The rest of the tape agrees with the chart more than with me. Korean semis fell 40 percent in 40 days. Jan van Eck's read is that the memory names "weren't growing volume, they just upped prices." FinX called the top outright. Jordi Visser has already sold Micron and rotated into silver, Bitcoin and Eli Lilly. And per the Polymarket list, Apple has started testing CXMT memory chips. That is the demand side going shopping in China for the first time.

Against all of that sits the only argument that has held: Elon Musk's line that memory supply grows about 20 percent a year against demand growing about 200. Roundhill's chief executive still calls SanDisk a $2,200 stock.

I am holding 60/40 for a third day and I am now the minority view in my own edition. What would break it is a CXMT qualification at Apple, because that converts a pricing story into a share story.

4. Visser says the crash is over and pointedly did not raise his target. Per Jordi Visser, the July drawdown was a positioning event and "the bottom is in as far as I'm concerned on a probability basis."

The confirmation is broad rather than narrow. The S&P rose 3.6 percent on the week and the Nasdaq proxy 5, with new all-time highs on the equal-weight S&P, the Russell, the NYSE Composite, the DAX and the FTSE in the same week. Earnings growth is running near 50 percent on 15 percent revenue growth, with margins at 17 percent against 13 a year ago.

His single best datapoint is on Nvidia, via Gavin Baker: it trades at its lowest forward earnings multiple in ten years, while B200 rental went from the mid $2 per GPU hour seven months ago to just under $4. Rising rental rates against a falling multiple is the shape of a market that has stopped believing the revenue.

What he did not do is raise the target. Last week he called for many AI names at new all-time highs by year end. This week he repeated the bottom call and attached no new number to it. Treat that as a call carried forward rather than reaffirmed.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $SMCI. Reports Tuesday after a 31 percent week, with a $60 billion-plus backlog and gross margin guided near 17 percent against a Street at roughly half. The setup has already moved, so the risk here is the reaction rather than the number.
  • $NBIS. Reports this week. Goldman raised its 2030 revenue estimate to $35.5 billion from $22.1, a 61 percent lift, and models about $30 billion of EBITDA by 2030 on contracted power above 4 gigawatts. It rose 14 percent Friday into Michael Burry's short.
  • $SNDK. Up 410 percent this year and the cleanest expression of whichever side of section three is right. It beat on Friday and closed red, the first time the tape has ignored a good memory print.
  • $USAR. The one fully specified position on the board. Per Rick Orford, called at $13.30, near $20 now, stop raised to $18, target zone $19.75 to $21.75. He flags that his own daily signal has not crossed yet, so he is holding rather than adding.
  • $RDW. Defence revenue up 380 percent year over year with a 1.92 book-to-bill and a record $500 million backlog, still burning cash and still diluting. Own the trajectory or wait for free cash flow, but do not call it a space stock.
  • $VLO. The refining leg of the same government-capital thesis that has already repriced $MP and $INTC, up 123 percent over the past year and the largest pure-play in the group. Nobody is front-running this one yet.

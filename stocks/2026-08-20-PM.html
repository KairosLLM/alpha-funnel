Walmart cracked and the AI trade went quiet into Nvidia

The consumer bellwether fell 9 percent on its worst sales growth in six years while Moderna held its squeeze and the semis waited on earnings.

ConsumerHealthcareSemisAi-InfraEtfs

Yesterday the theme was rotation and Moderna. Today the rotation's cleanest expression failed: Walmart posted its worst US sales growth in six years and fell the most since 2022, and the market is now asking whether the consumer side of the trade can hold. Moderna kept most of its historic gain, and the AI complex went quiet into Nvidia earnings next week. The lead is the crack in the rotation.

Market context. The S&P held near 7,720 after a soft open, with the Nasdaq up modestly while Per the Stocks list the Q's sat on five straight red days at the 50-day and the VIX rose about 7 percent intraday against a barely-down SPY, a divergence that sets up a heavy earnings week. Per the People list, Walmart fell 9.2 percent to about $103.70, its worst single day since May 2022. The AI tape was mixed: Moderna and Marvell up big, Applied Digital and Nebius down, and the semis holding a floor off Monday's lows. Walmart, Target and Lowe's retail reads land this week as the consumer test.

The scoreboard. The CoreWeave put-selling thesis is due tomorrow at $80. CoreWeave has been the most-battered name in the AI-infra complex through the drawdown, so the next two days decide it. Nothing else on the book resolves this window.

1. Walmart posted its worst sales growth in six years and the consumer call has a data point. Per the People list, US same-store sales rose 2.6 percent against 3.8 percent expected, the slowest growth in six years, and the stock fell 9.2 percent to about $103.70, its worst single day since May 2022. The full print was mixed: revenue beat at $187.94 billion against $187 billion, adjusted EPS beat at $0.81 against $0.74, and the company raised full-year guidance to $2.80 to $2.87. But the comp miss and a light Q3 guide were the tell the market traded. The read across the tape is that the higher-income customer is holding while the base softens, and that is the same split the rotation has been betting on.

2. Moderna kept most of its historic move and the healthcare complex repriced. Per the People list, Moderna is now the second-best S&P 500 performer this year, up 491 percent, behind SanDisk, after the first positive Phase 3 result for a personalized cancer vaccine. It doubled again at the open and settled well above where it started, and the 3x Moderna ETP surged 532 percent in a day. Merck fell slightly after a downgrade but the oncology complex held its bid. This is the second session of a catalyst that repriced an entire sector, and the pullback risk is now the squeeze ending, with analysts still pegging the stock far below its spike.

3. The semis went quiet into Nvidia and the setup is a divergence. Per the NVIDIA video, Nvidia has guided to 91 billion in revenue for the August 26 report, give or take 2 percent, and the next guide is the whole tape's question. The Q's are down five straight days at the 50-day while the VIX is up about 7 percent, a tape that has made the memory and AI names the most oversold they have been since the spring. Per the Stocks list, Marvell handed Google a warrant for nearly 7 percent of the company against custom-silicon revenue, and JPMorgan flagged Broadcom as an aggressive buy at current levels. The semis are holding a floor, but the direction is now a bet on the Nvidia number.

4. Applied Digital is the neocloud story the market has not fully priced. Per the Applied Digital video, the company now holds $36 billion of contracted lease revenue, up to $86 billion with renewal options, after winning three new leases worth about $20 billion from one hyperscaler that has now picked it three times in a row. Quarterly revenue topped $250 million, up more than 400 percent year over year. The stock has pulled back about 7 percent in the past month even as the backlog grew, and that gap is the setup to watch in the AI-infra complex.

5. SpaceX's second lockup is live and the float math is the story. Per the People list, up to 319 million shares, about 7 percent of the company, became eligible to trade today, the second insider lockup expiration, and the stock fell about 6 percent to near $140. History says the first unlock marked the bottom rather than the top, so the read is that this second one is more of a float-absorption event than a thesis-breaker, with the AI-factory and Starlink compounder thesis intact.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $WMT. Down 9.2 percent on the weakest US comps in six years, worst day since 2022. The consumer tape's first hard data point.
  • $MRNA. Second-best S&P performer this year after the cancer-vaccine win. Squeeze-led, so watch the pullback risk.
  • $NVDA. Has guided to 91 billion for the August 26 report. The next guide is the whole semis tape's catalyst.
  • $APLD. $36 billion contracted, up to $86 billion with renewals, down 7 percent in a month. The neocloud the market has not priced.
  • $MRVL. Handed Google a warrant for nearly 7 percent of the company against custom-silicon revenue through 2033. The survivor of the custom-chip trade.
  • $SPCX. Down 6 percent on the second lockup, but the first unlock marked a bottom. Float absorption, not a broken thesis.

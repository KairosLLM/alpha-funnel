Memory held the line while the rest of AI sold off

Micron ran another 5 percent back above $1,000 with UBS at $1,625, Nvidia caught a buy-the-dip, and the rotation into retail and dividends kept running.

SemisAi-InfraConsumerFinancialsEnergy

Monday was the memory tape with everything green behind it. Tuesday is the divergence: Micron held and led while the rest of the AI complex sold off hard, and the money that left AI did not leave the market, it rotated. This is the read on a session that separated the memory trade from the AI trade.

Market context. The S&P is down about half a percent with the Nasdaq off more, and the weakness is concentrated in the AI names: QQQ down near 2 percent, SOXL down almost 20 percent, SNDK down 8 percent after a 90 percent run, Nvidia flat to down near $219. Micron went the other way, up 5 percent and back above $1,000. Energy was the only green sector Monday, and the rotation this morning is out of AI into dividend payers and retail. Retail earnings kick off Tuesday with Home Depot.

1. Memory is the one AI pocket that held, and the Street is still raising targets. Micron ran another 5 percent to sit back above $1,000, with BofA modeling $188.6 billion of free cash flow by 2030 against $55.7 billion this year, JPMorgan at $1,540 on $154 of 2028 EPS, and options targeting $1,050 to $1,075. UBS goes further, at $1,625, arguing the market is treating Micron like a normal memory cycle while AI makes memory far more critical. The stock trades about six times forward earnings.

The supply story underneath is unchanged and getting tighter: Micron can serve only 50 to 66 percent of data-center demand, UBS models HBM prices up 79 percent year over year, and the CEO says 2027 constraints are worse than 2026. SanDisk is the mirror image, down 8 percent after a 90 percent move off the $970s, and the divergence is the cleanest read on who is chasing versus who is already in. The memory trade is now the AI pocket with a supply floor under it.

2. Nvidia caught a buy-the-dip and BofA made the case. BofA rates Nvidia a Buy with a $350 target against a price near $219 and calls the stock up to 50 percent undervalued. The nuance matters: the "despec" is not Nvidia wanting less memory, it is not being able to source it, and the roadmap still scales toward 500 gigabytes to a terabyte of HBM. That is a swing factor for Micron and SK Hynix rather than a demand problem.

The capex engine is not slowing: the big-five hyperscalers have $3.2 trillion locked into AI commitments, and BofA raised its AI infrastructure estimate 17 percent in one month to $3.6 trillion through 2028. Nvidia reports August 26, and the buy-the-dip consensus is now the positioning into it. The counter is the leveraged-ETF flows: flows into the Micron and SanDisk products show the dip got bought, not capitulated, with one-week volatility below one-month saying the hedging is complacent at the highs.

3. The rotation is real and it is out of AI into everything else. The tape is a clean rotation, profit taking out of the AI names into less loved areas, with Meta down 3.8 percent to $547.50 and now trading below 10 times this year's operating cash flow. Meta has been one of the cheapest megacap AI names, and it is getting sold on a legal overhang, the Oakland trial and 29 state attorneys general, while the macro tape punishes the group. Google is down 12 of the last 15 weeks and Amazon 9 of the last 11 days.

The house read is that this is a rates rotation, not an earnings story: the selloff driver is the 10-year over 4.7 percent and the 30-year at 5.3 percent, the same backdrop that last took the S&P down 30 percent. Rate-sensitive, cheaper, cash-returning names are the beneficiaries, and that is where the marginal money is going.

4. The 13F books are confirming the same rotation. The filings added detail Tuesday: Berkshire holds $28 billion of Google after adding 24 million shares, up 45 percent, and Druckenmiller closed his Intel and Micron positions and opened four Bitcoin miners, Bitdeer, Riot, Hut 8 and IREN. Ackman keeps Uber at 12 percent of his fund, Dalio's firm holds a billion of the SPY while selling the big semis, and the airlines and credit-card names stay the most bought. This is the consumer-and-fintech rotation the house has been calling the quarter's real theme, and it is now showing up in the books and in the tape at the same time.

5. Earnings week is the non-AI catalyst on the calendar. Home Depot reports Tuesday morning, Target and Lowe's Wednesday, Walmart and Deere and Alibaba Thursday, and Walmart is the broadest read on household spending, the first real consumer check in weeks. KKR agreed to buy UGI for $42.50 a share in a $9 billion deal, a cash buyout confirming that dividend infrastructure names still draw premium offers. The retail prints land into a rates tape where they could matter more than usual, because the market is rotating toward them.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $MU. Up 5 percent back above $1,000 with UBS at $1,625 and BofA modeling $188.6 billion of 2030 free cash flow. The one AI pocket with a supply floor.
  • $NVDA. BofA at $350 against $219, reading the memory despec as temporary. The buy-the-dip consensus into the August 26 print.
  • $SNDK. Down 8 percent after a 90 percent move off the $970s. The chase unwinding, and the divergence from Micron is the tell.
  • $CNDL. More than $8 million of insider buying on a $1.86 billion market cap in the last week after the stock fell from $96 to $34.50 and bounced to $41. A small-cap insider cluster.
  • $HD. The first big consumer print of the week Tuesday, into a tape that is rotating toward retail and dividend names.

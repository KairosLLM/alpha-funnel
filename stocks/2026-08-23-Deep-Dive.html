Nvidia's $500 billion MOUs meet a $3 trillion off-balance-sheet bill

Street is looking for about $92 billion of Nvidia revenue on Wednesday, nine top tech names carry some $3 trillion of off-balance-sheet commitments, and Wednesday's SOX drop of 5.0 percent was the midweek warning.

SemisAi-InfraEnergy

This channel's week was one question asked three ways. Who pays for the AI build. What $NVDA is allowed to print on Wednesday. And whether memory is still the crowded long after the SOX lost 5.0 percent in a day. The financing stack is the story. The earnings print is the referee. This memo is both sides of that stack, and what I would actually hold into August 26.

The through-line is that Wednesday priced the funding risk, and Thursday's macro bid papered over it. The funding risk did not leave.

1. The $500 billion is still a memorandum, and the $3 trillion is already a bill

Nate B Jones has Nvidia signing MOUs with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman, and KKR to mobilize more than $500 billion of third-party capital. He flags they are MOUs, subject to execution of the final agreements. Rick Orford ran the same stack the same week. I am not treating a memorandum as cash in the door.

The harder number is the one that showed up as a reprint, not as the paywalled original. The August 16 WSJ piece, via /u/Wild_Space, says nine top tech names had some $3 trillion of off-balance-sheet commitments. Traditional capex was about $600 billion over the past year. Uncommenced leases $1.2 trillion. Purchase obligations $1.9 trillion. Alphabet purchase commitments $811 billion as of June 30. Meta's Hyperion: Blue Owl majority, $27 billion in debt off Meta's balance sheet, Meta's initial lease about $12.3 billion, Meta uncommenced leases $347 billion as of June.

That is the financing story this channel has been circling all month. The house already has an open call that the AI rally is funded by hundreds of billions of purchase commitments against a thin sliver of direct equity. This week put a $3 trillion label on the same structure. Macro owns the "who funds the build" driver. The names that have to live with it sit here.

2. Nvidia is the referee, and the Street is clustered at $91 to $92 billion

There is no official 8-K in what I have. Earnings is still ahead. Stocks with Josh, on August 21, says earnings is Wednesday of next week, maps a swing from 212 to 227, and wants a Monday dip to 212.

Jose Najarro has Nvidia sitting at $214, up roughly 13.7 percent year to date, market cap roughly $5.2 trillion. Street revenue this quarter is roughly $92 billion. He would not be surprised by 94 or 95 billion. Watch Vera Rubin, the Q3 and Q4 ramp, margins, CPU TAM, and LPX.

Rick Orford is working off the last print. Data-center around $75.2 billion, up 92 percent. Networking almost 200 percent. EPS $1.87 against $1.77. Gross margin around 75 percent. Inventory around $26 billion. Supply commitments roughly $119 billion. China data-center compute zero, against $4.6 billion a year ago. Guide $91 billion, give or take 2 percent. Street $91.92 billion. Sequential growth implied about 11.5 percent against about 20 percent the prior two quarters. His read: sold-out, China, and a product transition, not a demand crack.

The open house call is the one I am carrying into Wednesday. Nvidia has guided to $91 billion. A next guide above $105 to $108 billion means no deceleration. Near $100 billion means a real slowdown. I am not rewriting that on a Saturday preview.

3. Memory is still the crowded long, and Wednesday showed you the crowding

Blockworks on August 19 is the midweek tape: SOX −5.0 percent, $MU −7.0 percent, SK Hynix ADR −9.2 percent. That is what a crowded memory long looks like on a 5.3 percent 30-year.

The fundamental tape underneath it did not break. Orford on Micron versus CXMT has Micron's 52-week range $113 to $1,255, data-center $11.5 billion of revenue in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 at an 87 percent gross margin. CXMT raised about $8.6 billion through July 2026 in its IPO, with Q1 profits about $3.7 billion. Milk Road on August 17 has JPMorgan raising the Micron target from $550 to $1,540, and BofA taking free cash flow from $55.7 billion this year to $188.6 billion by 2030. That last pair is a newsletter claim. I am leaving it labeled.

The house memory call is still 60/40 that multiples expand before they compress. Monday's ledger already scored the $991 confirmation as a hit when Micron reclaimed $1,000. The 1022, then 1037 to 1051, map is still the one I am using. Wednesday's −7.0 percent is what the crowding tax looks like, not a thesis change.

Applied Materials is the other side of the same week. Guided toward its first-ever quarter over $10 billion, year-over-year growth at the midpoint over 50 percent, annualized $40 billion-plus, Q3 FY26 revenue just over $9.1 billion. The stock sold off anyway. A $10 billion guide that the tape fades is the same funding-and-duration problem, not a demand miss.

4. Power is the bottleneck that showed up as politics

Daniel Koss has Nebius ($NBIS) forced off 32 natural-gas engines at 350 megawatts in Vineland, New Jersey, and onto fuel cells. The rumor is Bloom sold out into 2029 and 2030. The leftover name is $FCEL. That is local permitting doing what a 30-year cannot: rationing the build by the megawatt.

The same week's color around it, ranked lower: Applied Digital at $36 billion of contracted lease revenue and $86 billion of option upside, Q4 FY26 revenue just over $250 million, up over 400 percent, 1.4 gigawatts contracted. Nebius with Nvidia at $2 billion and 9.3 percent beneficial ownership, Goldman at 7.2 percent, the stock pulled back 13 percent after a 188 percent year, more than $40 billion of contracted revenue plus $15 billion potential, 24 percent short. Those are position facts. They are not the week's argument.

5. The bull case, the bear case, and the arbitration

The bull case is that the build is pre-sold and the referee is eight sessions from a print the Street already expects. $91 to $92 billion is not a surprise. A beat toward 94 or 95 billion, with Vera Rubin and the Q4 ramp intact, lets the $500 billion MOU stack get treated as real capacity rather than a press release. Memory already has 87 percent data-center gross margins at Micron and a JPMorgan target at $1,540. Power rationing is a volume constraint, not a demand one.

The bear case is that $3 trillion off the balance sheet is the bill coming due, and Wednesday already priced a piece of it. MOUs are not capital. China data-center compute at zero against $4.6 billion a year ago is a hole in the last print. Sequential growth slowing from about 20 percent to about 11.5 percent is the deceleration the house call is watching. A crowded $MU that loses 7.0 percent in a day is what happens when the 30-year and the funding question show up on the same tape.

I am 55/45 that Wednesday's Nvidia print holds the AI-infra bid, and I am saying that is a low-conviction number on purpose. The Street is already at $91.92 billion. The information is in the next guide, not in a beat of a number everyone already has.

What would change my mind: a guide that clusters near $100 billion, or a memory print that breaks the 87 percent margin. What keeps me at 55/45: the $3 trillion is real, the MOUs are not cash, and the buyback that bounced the SOX on Thursday has not spent a dollar yet.

What to do with it

Hold the structural memory and power names through Wednesday. Do not add $NVDA into $214 on a Saturday preview. Josh's 212 dip is the only entry on this tape I would take, and only as a swing into the print, not as a new core.

Own the funding risk as a size problem, not as a short. The $3 trillion is why I will not let this channel become a one-name Nvidia memo. $MU, $AMAT, $NBIS, $FCEL, and $APLD are how the same bill shows up in names you can actually trade.

Watchlist for the week ahead. $NVDA into August 26, $91 billion last guide, Street around $92 billion, house line on the next guide at $105 to $108 billion versus near $100 billion. $MU into 1022, then 1037 to 1051, with CXMT as the China comp. $AMAT on a $10 billion quarter the tape already faded. $NBIS and $FCEL on the Vineland-to-Bloom power ration. $APLD on $36 billion of contracted lease revenue the market has not paid up for.

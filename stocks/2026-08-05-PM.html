AMD beat and fell ten percent, and that is the whole market in one print

Bank of America's 7,741 target for the year is done, the S&P closed an entire candle outside its upper Bollinger band for the first time since March 2024, and SpaceX guided to a $100 billion run rate.

Ai-InfraSemisIndustrialsConsumerEtfs

Yesterday I said SpaceX reported into a 34 percent short interest and nobody had a model. It beat and guided to a $100 billion exit run rate. AMD is the more instructive print, because it beat too and got sold ten percent.

Market context. The Hormuz route agreement Macro covers tonight took the last of the war premium out and the tape went vertical. The Nasdaq rose more than 3 percent.

Per Walter Bloomberg on the Market list, Bank of America says the S&P has reached its 2026 target of 7,741, with technicals pointing to 8,000, then 8,234 and potentially 8,541. It sees 7,500 as key support and warns weak August-to-October seasonality, stretched momentum and Friday's payrolls could cap it.

Heisenberg has the stretched part measured. For the first time since March 2024, the entire S&P candlestick body closed outside the upper Bollinger band, by a wide margin. Sunday it was Tesla outside the lower band. Four sessions later the index is outside the upper one.

The scoreboard.

  • Resolved, and it beat. SpaceX reported $7.8 billion of June-quarter revenue against a $6.8 billion expectation and guided to a $100 billion exiting run rate. Per Gene Munster, that implies a December quarter near $22 billion, and December revenue estimates went up about 17 percent.
  • For, after being against. I marked Shay Boloor's Lag-7-becomes-Mag-7 call against on Monday when equal-weight led. A 3 percent Nasdaq day is the other side of that, and the call is live again.
  • Standing. My 60/40 that the July low holds into mid-August runs to August 15 and is not in doubt at a record high.
  • Due tonight. SanDisk reports with an implied move of roughly 15 percent either way.

1. AMD beat, and the market decided that was not enough. Per Con on the Stocks list, AMD produced good numbers and fell about 10 percent after hours having closed the regular session up 7 percent near its highs. It had nearly tripled over the prior year and was up 140 percent year to date going in.

Two things did it. The beat was modest against expectations that were not, and capex rose while free cash flow fell. For a name priced on operating leverage that is the wrong shape regardless of the earnings line.

The read-through is the point. Con concludes AI names now have to beat by a lot rather than merely beat, and he is selling his AI enabler positions on it, naming resistance at $NBIS $220 to $230 and $MU $900, with $AAOI, $NET, $MTSI and $ASYS reporting within days and Nvidia, Marvell and Micron behind them.

The other side is specific and worth holding. Per Shay Boloor on the Market list, Futurum reiterated a Buy with an $800 target, raising its 2027 forecast to $90 billion of revenue and $17.50 of EPS, including $29 billion from server CPUs and $39 billion from data centre GPUs on the Venice ramp, Helios shipments to Meta, Oracle and OpenAI, and up to 14 gigawatts of committed accelerator deployments. That last figure is contracted rather than modelled.

My lean is 60/40 that AMD trades above today's after-hours level in a month. A sell-the-news reaction to a beat in a name up 140 percent is positioning rather than thesis.

2. SpaceX guided to $100 billion and left a $350 billion funding gap. The print was good. The arithmetic behind the guidance is the story.

Per Gene Munster, guidance implies about $8.5 billion of monthly revenue in December, and the Street sits near $100 billion for 2027, up 122 percent. Munster thinks that is low, and at 5 percent month-over-month growth off December he gets $135 billion. His caveat is the honest one: the cloud contracts carry 90-day cancellation provisions, and he waves it away because inference is in the top of the first.

3. CXMT refused to cut prices for Apple, and that breaks the bear case on memory. Per Jukan on the Market list, South Korean media reports Apple negotiated with CXMT over mobile DRAM supply prices including LPDDR5X, to ease cost pressure on its next iPhone lineup, and CXMT rejected the request. It insisted on prices higher than or comparable to Samsung and SK Hynix.

If that holds it inverts the story I cut my memory call on. Monday's headline was CXMT doubling capacity past 600,000 wafers a month, and the market read new Chinese supply as a price war. A supposed price-breaker declining to undercut the incumbents on a marquee account is the opposite behaviour, and it says CXMT is optimising for margin rather than share.

The honest caveat is that The AI Investor, relaying the same report, called it "fake news to me, doesn't pass the smell test." He may be right and the sourcing is one media report.

So I am raising the memory call back to 60/40 that multiples expand before they compress, from 55/45 Monday, and doing it on a single-sourced report I have flagged as contested. SanDisk reports tonight into a 15 percent implied move and settles it faster than any of this.

4. The humanoid race got a supply chain this week. Per Wayne Liang on the Market list, four separate national efforts moved at once.

  • Mitsubishi is converting an idle engine plant to build roughly 1,000 humanoids a month
  • Unitree lists in China in a couple of weeks, holding 85 percent of 2025 shipments with more than $20 billion of subsidies behind it
  • Hyundai is targeting a few thousand a year through Boston Dynamics
  • Tesla is ramping Optimus

The tradeable part: downstream flow benefits $CCXI and Agility as the only US-listed pure-play, and he flags plainly that a SPAC merger is higher risk and higher reward.

Converting an engine plant is the detail. The constraint has moved from whether humanoids work to who has idle precision-manufacturing capacity. That is an industrials question, and Japan has more of it than anyone.

5. Gold outran everything and the leadership underneath is not what the index says. Gold rose more than 4 percent to above $4,300, with the tracking fund running from roughly $370 to almost $392 in two sessions. That is a record equity tape and a gold breakout in the same week.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $AMD. Beat and fell 10 percent after hours on capex up and free cash flow down. Futurum's $800 target rests on 14 gigawatts of committed accelerator deployments, contracted rather than hoped for. This is a positioning reset in a name that had tripled, not a thesis break.
  • $SNDK. Reports tonight with a roughly 15 percent implied move, and it has reacted positively to four of its first five prints since listing. This is the cleanest read on whether commodity DRAM pricing is actually cracking after the CXMT headlines.
  • $SPCX. Beat at $7.8 billion and guided to a $100 billion exit run rate, with the first big lockup opening tomorrow. The funding gap is roughly $350 billion and the answer is probably Nvidia's balance sheet, so own it as a compute business rather than a launch business.
  • $CCXI. The only US-listed pure-play on humanoid components into Mitsubishi at a thousand units a month, Unitree's listing and Optimus ramping. A SPAC structure means size it like the option it is.
  • $GLD. Up four percent above $4,300 while equities printed records and oil fell. If gold is a monetary signal it is the most important chart on the board. If McGlone is right that it is a sock puppet to the S&P, it is beta with better marketing.
  • $XHB. Homebuilders are on AskLivermore's buy list and the cleanest expression of a rate path where six desks disagree. They win on Citi's cut scenario and lose on Kashkari's.

Nebius ran 34 percent, Burry doubled down, and the S&P broke 7,800

Nebius posted 454 percent revenue growth on $582 million, raised its contract book to $46 billion, and the afternoon brought Burry with a fresh short at $247 plus a $94 billion memory thesis that nobody was carrying this morning.

Ai-InfraSemisFinancials

Yesterday I said the neocloud earnings were the real news and the depreciation argument had no purchase on a business that pre-sells capacity above cost. Today the market agreed and kept going.

Market context. Per the Market morning list, the S&P 500 broke above 7,800 for the first time ever in a session that opened green on the post-CPI momentum and kept running. The VIX touched 14.39, its lowest level of the year, on the lightest volume in thirteen sessions. The S&P has now printed 26 all-time highs this year. $NVDA closed at $224, its first close above 220 in a month. $META fell 3.3 percent and $MSFT 2.2, the rotation inside the rally that says semis are back and the software trade is taking a breather. Options volume leaders: NVDA 3.6 million contracts, SMCI 720,000, MU 1.3 million.

1. Nebius put up the numbers and Burry answered with a bigger short. Per the Crypto PM list and the Stocks PM list, Nebius did $582.3 million of revenue against $573 million expected, up 454 percent year over year. Adjusted EBITDA was $236 million against $173 million. AI Cloud revenue rose 514 percent. The company raised contracted capacity to 5 gigawatts, expects more than 1 gigawatt a year of deployment from 2027, and carried about $46 billion of multi-year AI cloud contracts including $27 billion with $META and $17 billion with $MSFT. Customer prepayments are expected to top $9 billion in 2026 alone, at 50 to 60 percent of capex.

Per the Stocks PM list, the pricing is the real weapon. Nebius signed its first deal at $40 to $50 million per megawatt, against a normal rate of about $20 to $25 million and a baseline of $9.8 million. Its first capacity auction cleared 15 percent above the highest price it had ever charged for Blackwell. It could sell its entire 2027 capacity today and is choosing not to. Per Shay Boloor, short-notice deals pay back in under two years with customers funding roughly half the capex upfront.

The stock ran from roughly $195 to $275 before fading slightly in the close, a move that puts Burry's $211.77 short from Tuesday underwater and his new $247 short from today barely flat. Per burrytracker, Burry added NBIS shorts at $247, MU short at $924, and ORCL short at $152 today, with a quote that Nebius is what the top of a boom looks like. Per the Jose Najarro stream, the question is how many of his 300,000 Substack followers are covering a 30 percent short float in a stock that is up ten times in eighteen months and answering on every quarter.

The after-hours earnings gave the longs a breather. NBIS faded about half a percent in sympathy with Cerebras, which fell 14 percent on operating expense of $500 million against $80 million expected at a $60 billion market cap. At that valuation on less than a billion of revenue, the credibility was inside the margin math.

2. SanDisk just put a match to the memory complex, and the size of the thesis is what matters. Per the Market PM list, SanDisk unveiled its HBF architecture, NAND repurposed as AI memory, and the numbers are not incremental. HBF matches high-bandwidth memory at 12.8 terabytes per second of bandwidth while using one-eighth the GPU resources at one-quarter the footprint. It achieves twice the GPU efficiency at one-eighth the capex, because one HBF GPU runs the same model that requires eight HBM GPUs.

Per Shay Boloor, SanDisk guided mid-to-high-teens revenue growth, roughly 80 percent non-GAAP gross margins, and about 50 percent adjusted free cash flow margins, with 100 percent of excess cash returned. Eight customers have signed contracts totalling roughly $94 billion in lifetime value. Key-value cache workloads are expected to drive 35 percent of AI data centre NAND workloads by 2030, and BiCS NAND delivers 54 percent more bits per wafer at 27 percent annual productivity improvement. Sell-side targets are running back to $3,000 from the current $1,404.

This is a thesis that would exist even if Nvidia stayed flat. The memory call the house has been fighting over for two weeks is now sitting on top of a named architecture with numbers, and the argument over whether multiples expand or compress just got a third term that neither side was pricing.

3. AMAT reported a beat and fell on it, and the month has a new earnings rhythm. Per the Reddit post and the Stocks AM list, Applied Materials beat earnings and raised guidance on its semiconductor systems, guided AGS revenue growth above 20 percent and advanced-packaging revenue growth above 70 percent for 2026, and fell 5 percent after hours because the expectations were higher than the beat. That is the same pattern Cerebras hit and the same one Coherent hit with a double beat that faded six percent: the bar for a clean after-hours reaction on earnings day inside a stock at an all-time high is not a beat, it is a blowout, and nothing short of a blowout that clears the valuation question gets a pass.

Per the Chip Stock Investor stream, DigitalOcean raised full-year guidance to 30 to 31 percent from 25 to 27, called for 50 percent growth by the end of fiscal 2027 on $400 million a quarter, and an analyst noted the cleanest balance sheet in the cloud infrastructure space with net cash and stock buybacks actively offsetting dilution. The stock faded after its own Q2 but the guidance keeps stacking.

Per the Financial Markets AM list, Coherent did $2.1 billion against $1.98 billion, guided Q1 to $2.3 billion against $2.1 billion, and has its revenue trajectory pointing above $3 billion a quarter by the end of fiscal 2027 on a backlog booked through calendar 2027. The after-hours faded anyway.

The Meta and Microsoft drops inside an index making all-time highs are the same signal in reverse. $META down 3.3 and $MSFT down 2.2 tells you this is a rotation within the rally, not a distribution event. The semis ran, the software gave back, Cisco beat on networking up 40 percent and faded in sympathy, and the after-hours trade painted its own picture.

4. Ackman rebuilt his portfolio and Peltz is coming for Wendy's. Per the Market AM list and Walter Bloomberg, Bill Ackman added six new holdings in his biggest portfolio revamp in years: $NFLX, $V, $MA, $ALC, $ICE and $SPGI. He bought $NFLX in 2022 at about $350 a share for $1.1 billion, sold at a roughly $400 million loss, and is now back in with the stock roughly half what he paid. His thesis is double-digit revenue compounding and about 20 percent annual earnings growth. Pershing Square USA is down 3.5 percent through July against the S&P up 13.

Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund Management is preparing a take-private of Wendy's at up to $8.80 a share with a consortium including BlueFive Capital and the Flynn Group. The stock has a 30 percent short float on 160 million shares and Heisenberg called the price today directly.

Per the Reddit post and Bloomberg, the California Public Utilities Commission was scheduled to vote today on Charter Communications' merger with Cox, the last major regulatory step before it becomes the largest broadband and cable provider in the United States at 38 million subscribers surpassing Comcast. The stock trades at $150 on a 3.9 times earnings multiple with $4.9 billion of net income and a 50 percent short float, and the SpaceX consumer mobile partnership talks reported by Bloomberg and Reuters in June sit on the other side of the close.

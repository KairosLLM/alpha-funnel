The S&P set a record, gold beat it, and memory did not move at all

Nebius reports Wednesday into Michael Burry's fresh short, Figure follows Thursday, and 745 on the tracking fund is the line that decides whether 800 is live.

The index closed at a record on Friday and the memory complex did not move. That gap is the week in one sentence, and next week has three prints to close it.

The week ahead. Friday finished at a record high on the S&P. Per the Kalshi list, $2.8 trillion was added to the US market across the week, with the Nasdaq tracking fund up 5.3 percent. Gold still beat it, finishing almost 8 percent higher per the Stocks list, more than the two big index funds combined.

Per Stocks with Josh, the level to hold is 745, and the path to 800 requires Wednesday's CPI to come in cool. His invalidation is unusually clean: "If at any time we cross back beneath this 745, then it means that this up move isn't likely going to happen and it means that this was the top." Set against a VIX near two-year lows and Kalshi at 67 percent for 8,000 this year, the asymmetry is not generous.

1. Nebius reports Wednesday into a short that just got bigger. Per Rick Orford, $NBIS has moved to a partner-funded model where partners fund, own and operate the physical build while Nebius supplies the design, the platform and the customers. The bricks are theirs, the brains are Nebius.

The guidance is the whole case: an annualised run rate of $7 billion to $9 billion by year end, from about $1.25 billion at the end of last year. Behind it sit a Meta agreement worth potentially $27 billion and 1.2 gigawatts of secured power and land in Pennsylvania. Nvidia holds a 9.3 percent stake after a $2 billion investment, and Goldman raised its own position from 7.2 to 10.5 percent this week.

The price is where it gets uncomfortable. 105.5 times sales, 7.7 times book, a 60-month beta of 4.1, and a 52-week range from about $50 to just under $300.

Michael Burry is short at $211.77 and spent this week adding to March semiconductor ETF puts and shorting more shares. This is the cleanest binary on the calendar: a 105 times sales name reporting into a famous short, with a beta of 4. I have no edge on the direction and I am not pretending to. What I would say is that a 4-beta name at that multiple is a position to size, not to be right about.

2. Mastercard grew 14 percent and the argument is entirely about the price. Per a breakdown on Reddit, revenue hit $9.28 billion, up 14 percent, with adjusted EPS of $5.04, up 21 percent, and operating margin expanding to 61.1 percent from 59.9. Expenses grew 11 percent against 14 percent revenue growth, and that gap is the EPS spread.

The mix is better than the headline. Value-added services grew 20 percent against 10 percent for the core payment network, and non-US switched volume grew 12 percent against 6 percent in the US. Cross-border volume rose 12 percent and was still running at 11 percent in July.

The poster's own conclusion is the honest one and it is not a buy: the difficulty with Mastercard is not the quality of the business, it is the valuation, and at a premium multiple even a great business produces mediocre returns if growth slows. I agree. It is still the cleanest non-AI compounder in the week's material, and the only one where the debate is price rather than durability.

4. Memory is sold out and the stocks went nowhere, and there is an arbitrage inside it. Per Milk Road AI, roughly 70 percent of hyperscaler capex is going to memory with a projected $761 billion next year, everything is sold out this year, and hyperscalers are signing three to five year contracts with price levels fixed. Elon Musk's version on the SpaceX call: memory demand is growing 200 percent a year and memory capacity is growing 20 percent.

Samsung is their pick as the laggard, with revenue up 130 percent, net profit up 52 percent, and it shipped HBM4 to Nvidia first, which retires the step-behind narrative. UBS sees it taking HBM share from SK Hynix next year.

Then the structural trade nobody will take. The NASDAQ wrapper for SK Hynix trades at a 22 percent premium, while SK Square, a holding company owning 20 percent of SK Hynix, trades at a 44 percent discount to its look-through value. Management KPIs are tied to closing that discount below 30 percent. Same asset, two listings, a 66 point spread and a paid incentive to compress it.

I am holding the memory call at 60/40 and acknowledging it has not worked for a week. Sold-out capacity with contracted pricing is a real floor. It is not a catalyst, and the tape has spent five sessions proving the difference.

SanDisk's investor day put a floor under the memory trade, and Micron ran at $1,000

SanDisk guided to roughly 80 percent gross margins through 2030 on $94 billion of locked contracts, JPMorgan set a $2,250 target, and Micron cleared $965 on its way at the four-figure line.

SemisAi-InfraFinancialsIndustrials

Yesterday the question was whether Nebius could hold its run against Burry. Today the memory complex answered a bigger one, and the S&P closed at another record on top of it.

Market context. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,798.99 after clearing 7,800 intraday, with the Nasdaq up 0.81 percent. Hedge funds bought $6.8 billion of US equities last week, the largest weekly buy in 18 years, while retail sold $4.1 billion and institutions sold $1.1 billion. The tape rotated into semis and neoclouds. Options volume leaders were Nvidia at 2.4 million contracts, Tesla at 2.0 million, and Micron at 1.0 million, with Nvidia earnings now eight trading days out on August 26.

The scoreboard. I said on July 30 that the July low holds into mid-August, and I trimmed the odds to 60/40 the week after. The S&P is now at an all-time high with hedge funds re-grossing at an 18-year pace, so that low held and then some. Hit.

1. SanDisk put a number on the memory supercycle and Micron ran at $1,000. SanDisk's investor day was not a story, it was a guide. The company is targeting roughly 80 percent non-GAAP gross margins through fiscal 2030 on about $94 billion of locked long-term contracts, with multi-year agreements already covering half of fiscal 2027 bit supply and two-thirds by 2028. JPMorgan resumed coverage at overweight with a $2,250 target and a view that the contracts hold margins even at floor pricing. The stock closed up about 14 percent Thursday and added roughly 6 percent premarket, and it is up about 50 percent since the fund that blew up on it liquidated.

Micron is doing the tape's work on the way to four figures. It hit $987 premarket, cleared the $965 target the house has been tracking, and needs a close above $991 to confirm the next leg toward $1,022. The structural numbers underneath are the ones that matter: about 70 percent of hyperscaler capex is now going to memory, the projected 2027 spend is $761 billion, demand is growing roughly 200 percent a year against 20 percent supply, and UBS models DRAM undersupplied until the second quarter of 2028. SK Hynix's chairman said the shortage worsens into 2027 and that even doubling capacity in five years is not enough.

The skeptical read is real and it came from the desks today, with some calling SanDisk's HBF marketing and dividing the addressable market by two to four. That is the debate the whole memory thesis turns on, and it is live, not settled.

2. The neoclouds got validation, and the rentals are pricing it. Iren delivered Horizon 1 to Microsoft, earning Nvidia's Exemplar Cloud status, and guided to about $3.4 billion of annualized AI Cloud revenue by the end of 2026 on the $9.7 billion Microsoft deal, with capacity moving from 480 megawatts to 1.2 gigawatts through 2027. CoreWeave's CEO said the company signed $25 billion-plus of new contracts in the five weeks since its quarter closed and that the depreciation debate is put to bed, pointing to Blackwell B200 rental rates that have climbed from roughly $2 per GPU hour a year ago to just under $4 now. Nebius held the Northland Street-high buy at a $410 target on $582 million of revenue up 454 percent.

The common thread is pricing power in the spot market. When a three-year-old GPU generation is contracted out to 2029 at full freight, the supply is not loose, and the neoclouds are being paid for it.

3. Nu Holdings put up its first billion-dollar quarter. Nu did $1.06 billion of net income in the quarter, up 67 percent and the first quarter above a billion, on revenue of $5.88 billion, up 39 percent, with return on equity at 33 percent and net interest margin at 22.9 percent. The stock rose about 10 percent. The number to watch is the credit one: 90-day nonperforming loans ticked up to 6.9 percent from 6.5, the highest since the third quarter of 2024, on a portfolio up 37 percent to $39.4 billion. That is the first sign of the consumer stress the Macro edition flagged, showing up in a Brazilian lender before it reaches US names.

4. ZIM is a cash machine that trades like a shipping line. The container carrier trades near $27.50 with roughly $25 a share of cash on the balance sheet, so the market is paying about two and a half dollars for the shipping franchise. Second-quarter cash generation is on track to add $8 to $10 a share by the third quarter, and the desks put assets plus cash near $100 a share against an $8 to $14 earnings range. That is a broken-valuation setup in a name the market is treating as cyclical at the bottom.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $SNDK. Investor day guided to roughly 80 percent gross margins through 2030 on $94 billion of contracts, JPMorgan set a $2,250 target, and the stock is up about 50 percent since the forced liquidation. The marketing-versus-real debate is live.
  • $MU. At $987 and through the $965 target, with $991 the confirmation line toward $1,022. Memory at 70 percent of hyperscaler capex and DRAM undersupplied to Q2 2028 is the structural case.
  • $NBIS. Northland's Street-high $410 target on $582 million of revenue up 454 percent and the ability to sell 2027 capacity today. Burry is still short from higher.
  • $NU. First quarter above $1 billion of net income, ROE at 33 percent, stock up 10 percent. The 6.9 percent NPL tick is the credit tell to watch into next quarter.
  • $ZIM. About $25 a share of cash against a $27.50 price, assets plus cash near $100. The market is pricing the shipping line as cyclical at the bottom while the cash stack grows.

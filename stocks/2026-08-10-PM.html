The memory bull just conceded the pricing lever, so I am flipping my own call

Chip Stock Investor is holding SanDisk through a 40 percent drawdown and says the wild average selling prices are finished, locked away by five-year hyperscaler contracts.

SemisAi-InfraIndustrialsHealthcare

Yesterday I held the memory call for a third day and said I was the minority view in my own edition. Today the man still holding the stock explained why I was wrong, and he did it while defending the position.

Market context. Friday closed at a record and the internals are the argument against it. Per the Financial Markets list, S&P 500 profit margins hit 16.9 percent in the second quarter, the highest ever, the index is up 14 percent on the year after being down 7 on March 30, and the dividend yield is 1.04 percent, the lowest in history. Goldman's Panic index fell from the 90th percentile to the zero percentile in a single week. Per the Market list, Heisenberg has this on pace for one of the best Augusts in 41 years, and the only two comparables, 1986 and 2020, both gained 7 percent and were followed by Septembers that fell 10 and 8.4. Per the Market morning list, Rocket Lab and AST SpaceMobile report after the close, with Supermicro and CoreWeave tomorrow and Nebius Wednesday.

The scoreboard.

  • Reversing after fifteen days. My 60/40 that memory multiples expand before they compress is dead. I am now 55/45 the other way. Section one is the reason and it is not a sentiment change.
  • Due tomorrow. Supermicro, on Rick Orford's July 28 bearish case. It ran 31 percent into the print anyway, so this resolves on the number rather than the setup.
  • Catalyst passed, trade still live. Grab reported today and no source carries the result. The January $3.50/$4.50 call spread runs to expiry, so it stays open on its own clock.

1. The memory bull conceded the pricing lever, and that is the whole call. Per Chip Stock Investor, who is holding $SNDK through a fall from over $2,000 in July to roughly $1,200 and says nothing has fundamentally changed for the worse: "we can no longer rely on the really wild average selling prices on their SSDs. That is now over now that SanDisk and the other memory companies have signed long-term agreements, usually five-year agreements, with the hyperscalers."

Read that against what I built the call on. My 60/40 rested on Bernstein modelling roughly 35 percent of all bits shipped from 2027 to 2029 as take-or-pay at a $0.26 per gigabyte floor against $0.29 spot. I called that a floor under the price. It is the same contract structure, and from inside the position it reads as a ceiling.

Both descriptions are correct, and that is the problem. Take-or-pay at a fixed band protects the downside and removes the upside, and multiple expansion needs the upside. Nobody pays more for earnings pre-sold at a floor price for five years.

The guide says it without the framing. FY26 revenue finished up over 370 percent, the September quarter is still guided to 300 to nearly 400 percent year over year and is flat sequentially, with adjusted EPS guided to a $45 midpoint against $39.25 just reported. The answer offered is $4.5 billion of buybacks in a quarter. Companies buy back stock when the operating lever is spent.

Per the Stocks list, Jan van Eck has argued for weeks that the memory names "weren't growing volume, they just upped prices." I treated that as a bear talking his book. Today a holder said it about his own stock.

What keeps me at 55/45 rather than 70/30 is that the spot tape has not turned. Per the Market list, Bank of America has SK Hynix DRAM prices up 25 percent quarter over quarter in the third quarter, Kioxia guiding revenue up 35, and NAND spot up 5 to 6 percent on the week. Spot is still ripping. It no longer reaches the contracted book.

2. Kratos lost the competition that mattered and kept the airframe anyway. Per Rick Orford, $KTOS did $371 million of first-quarter revenue, up 23 percent, on $605 million of bookings for a 1.6 book-to-bill and a backlog near $2 billion, with full-year guidance raised. All of it posted after the Air Force passed it over in the first collaborative combat aircraft production competition, the loss that knocked the stock down.

The XQ-58A Valkyrie is flying regardless. Northrop picked the airframe for its winning US Marine Corps solution, Airbus is integrating it with a European mission system, and GE Aerospace is working on propulsion. The OpenSpace ground segment adds a Space Systems Command agreement running to a $446.8 million ceiling, and Orford is careful that a ceiling is not recognised revenue.

His bear case is fair: the Valkyrie could stay a demonstrator that never gets its own production line. The thesis is that losing the prime slot while keeping the airframe is a better business than the market priced. No target here, so size it rather than trade it.

Najarro's single question is the right one: was the first quarter the true bottom in CoreWeave's adjusted operating margin? Management says margins expand to low double digits by the fourth quarter. If they walk that back tomorrow, the stock up 14 percent against a peer up 108 gets cheaper for a reason.

Per Edelbridge Alpha, Michael Burry is short Nebius at $211.77 and Oracle at $144.63 with Nebius near $187, and his argument is not valuation. It is that these companies depreciate GPUs too slowly, overstating Oracle's earnings by 26 to 27 percent by 2028. Two of today's sources sit thirty cents apart on Oracle. Per Stocks with Josh, he called it at $132 for a $145 minimum, hit that, and now expects continuation above $145 on an oversold RSI turn. Burry is short from $144.63 on the accounting. Only one of those has a multi-year clock.

4. Biotech is beating the tape and almost nobody is writing about it. Per the Stocks list, $XBI is up 29.5 percent this year and 10 percent on the week, the best sector print in the feed and the least discussed.

The bid is structural and dated: 33 buyouts above $500 million already this year against $200 billion to $300 billion of patent-cliff revenue pharma must replace by 2030, with three to four more deals expected in August.

Two names carry catalysts. $VERA has EFGR data next month framed as a plus 30 or minus 50 percent event. $NKTR has come back from the 50s on over $1 million of premium paid for September 100 calls, a bet on a royalty deal or a takeout inside four to six weeks.

5. Galloway put a number on the AI question and a second source answered it against the bulls. Per Prof G Markets, hyperscaler capex needs about $2.5 trillion of incremental AI revenue to earn a reasonable return, against $150 billion of cumulative AI revenue today. That is a fifteen times gap, and $2.5 trillion exceeds all of big tech's current revenue combined. The concentration underneath is sharper: OpenAI was 70 percent of Microsoft's AI sales last year and lost $21 billion.

Gavin Baker has said publicly that OpenAI is almost certainly generating significant free cash flow. Per Jordamøn, that did not survive the day. OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 on May 22, on roughly $2 billion of monthly revenue while losing $1.22 for every dollar it earns. Annualised, that is a bigger loss than the leaked figure Galloway quoted. The original listing target was September at $852 billion to $1 trillion, and SEC rules put the public prospectus about fifteen days before the roadshow, so the full financials would have had to land in late August.

I am staying 60/40 with Steve Eisman against Burry on the broad AI trade. The 40 was always customer concentration. Today it got a number rather than a new argument.

  • $CRWV. Reports tomorrow at $2.5 to $2.6 billion consensus with a $100 billion backlog and 75 percent of a $30 billion 2027 target contracted, against $650 to $730 million of interest expense. The margin trough is the whole print.
  • $NBIS. Reports Wednesday near $187 with Burry short at $211.77 on depreciation rather than multiple. Margins were pre-guided lower, so the bear headline is scheduled and the surprise would be the capex and ARR guides going up.
  • $KTOS. A 1.6 book-to-bill, a $2 billion backlog and raised guidance, all delivered after losing the award that took the stock down. Northrop, Airbus and GE Aerospace are each building on the airframe it was passed over for.

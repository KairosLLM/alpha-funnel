Moderna just delivered the best trading day in its history

The first positive Phase 3 result ever for a personalized cancer vaccine doubled the stock and flipped the tape toward biotech while the Treasury broke the long end.

HealthcareSemisAi-InfraConsumerFinancials

The AI selloff is not what broke today. Moderna did, on a cancer vaccine, and the Treasury did, by buying the long end. The stock doubled on the first positive Phase 3 result ever for a personalized cancer vaccine, and the rate regime that had been crushing everything reversed hard. This is the read on a session where the AI trade stopped being the only story in the room.

Market context. After the Treasury doubled long-end buybacks, the S&P turned positive to near 7,724 and the semis caught a bid off their lows, with the Philadelphia index reversing from down 5 percent to roughly flat. Per the MARKET OPEN stream, Moderna doubled, lifting the biotech complex, Eli Lilly touched a $1.2 trillion market cap, and the rotation into non-AI was the day's theme. The 30-year fell below 5.20 percent, the fuel for the risk-on turn. Retail earnings are midweek with Walmart Wednesday and Target and Lowe's.

The scoreboard. The Treasury buyback answered this week's macro call before the Fed minutes printed, and it is why the AI names stopped falling. On the book's own names, the $CRWV put-selling thesis is due Friday at $80, and CoreWeave has been the most-battered of the AI infra names through the selloff, so the next two days decide it.

1. Moderna had the best day of its corporate life and it rewrote biotech. Per the People list, Moderna's personalized mRNA cancer vaccine, built from each patient's tumor mutations and paired with Merck's Keytruda, hit the first positive Phase 3 result ever for such a therapy, in resected stage IIB to IV melanoma. The stock more than doubled, up as much as 127 percent to $143 intraday, on its best single day ever. Merck rose 10 to 12 percent on the read-through, and Barclays sees a $3 billion therapy by 2035.

The ripple was the point. Per the People list, the biotech ETF is up 32.5 percent since late March and 54 percent year over year, more than double the Nasdaq. Eli Lilly hit a $1.2 trillion market cap. Personalis, the maker of the MRD test used in the trial, rose 20 percent. This is the kind of catalyst the healthcare section exists to catch, and it arrived on a day the whole complex had been waiting for.

2. Anthropic is now the biggest AI lab by revenue and OpenAI just lost the lead. Per the NVIDIA $200B video, Anthropic more than doubled its quarterly revenue to $11.6 billion, overtaking OpenAI, whose revenue rose 18 percent quarter over quarter to $6.7 billion while its operating loss widened to $12.3 billion. Anthropic swung to a small operating profit and is heading to a September IPO that Polymarket prices at 87 percent by the end of the year, up from 60 percent in July. The AI leadership race just flipped, and it is happening into the listing.

The mechanism is worth naming because it changes the Nvidia read. Per the Stock list, AWS Bedrock drives 45 percent of Anthropic's API revenue, so the compute demand behind the AI buildout is now concentrated in the winner of the revenue race, not the biggest spender. If OpenAI stumbles, the fungible capacity just goes to Anthropic. That is the strongest version of the Nvidia compute thesis there is.

3. Marvell gave Google a warrant and the semis found a floor. Per the People list, Marvell issued Google a warrant for up to 58,970,907 shares at $206.58, roughly 7 percent of the company, tied to custom chip revenue through 2033 that could run to $120 billion. The stock rose 13 percent on the day while Broadcom fell 5 percent, the clearest signal yet that the custom-silicon winners are the AI trade that survives a repricing. Google gets the silicon and Marvell gets the locked-in buyer, and the market paid for that today.

The semis had been the epicenter of the selloff, down 8 to 11 percent across the memory and AI names on the long-end spike, but the Treasury buyback took the 10-year back down and the complex caught a bid into the close. This is the rates rotation doing its work: the same long-end driver that crushed semis on Monday reversed today, and the group that fell furthest bounced hardest.

4. The rotation is the real theme and the buyback just accelerated it. Per the People list, the money has been leaving AI for healthcare, staples, and dividend payers, and today's biotech blowout is the cleanest expression of it. The flow shows the SPY call wall stacked at 775 with $2.2 billion of open interest, and the rotation names, Uber, SoFi, and the fintechs, are the ones drawing the bid.

The earnings calendar is the non-AI catalyst on it. Walmart reports Wednesday, Target and Lowe's Thursday, and a Walmart print that holds would confirm the household-spending read the rotation has been betting on. The house view is unchanged: this is a rates rotation first and a sector story second, and a long end that policy is now actively capping is fuel for the money leaving AI.

SpaceX's tradable float tripled today, and AppLovin grew 53 percent and lost a fifth

Up to 911.5 million shares came free at once, more than 140 percent of the public float, while the S&P printed another record high on the largest option-call volume ever recorded.

Yesterday AMD beat and fell ten percent. Today AppLovin grew revenue 53 percent at an 84 percent margin and fell twenty. Two sessions, one verdict: beating stopped being the bar.

Market context. The index does not know any of that. Per the Kalshi list, the S&P printed a new all-time high at 7,730 today, its twenty-fifth record of the year, and Kalshi puts 8,000 before year end at 70 percent. Tom Lee wants 7,900 to 8,000 this month, UBS 8,100 by December.

How it got there is the part to hold onto. Per the Financial Markets list, the Kobeissi Letter counted a record 4.0 million S&P call contracts on Tuesday, against a 700,000 daily average in 2020 and 2021. The put-to-call skew posted its largest two-day decline since 2017. Mr Derivatives supplied the necessary commentary: "Wowzers! What can possssssibly go wrong here?!"

Leadership underneath is thin. $GOOGL fell about 3.5 percent on the DeepMind reshuffle, and per Stocks with Josh the level that matters is $SPY 686, which has held all week. A loss of it opens 629.

The print underneath was good and nobody cared. Per Ticker Symbol YOU, revenue was $7.81 billion, up 92 percent, against a $6.93 billion estimate, with EPS of $0.09 against an expected 26-cent loss. Then capex landed just under $18.5 billion, 39 percent above expectations, or $2.35 spent per dollar of revenue, with no 2027 guidance at all.

The stock closed at its lowest price in history yesterday, having shed roughly $1.6 trillion of market value since June's high, per Barchart on the Financial Markets list. Morgan Stanley still carries a $300 target.

The honest comparable is Meta in 2012, down about 50 percent at the 91-day lockup and still down 36 percent a year later. Own it as a compute business with a supply problem that runs four more months.

2. AppLovin grew 53 percent, printed an 84 percent margin, and lost a fifth of its value. Per Daniel Koss on the Stocks list, the quarter was $1.92 billion of revenue, up 53 percent, with $1.61 billion of adjusted EBITDA at an 84 percent margin, and the stock fell 19 percent before the bell.

What broke is smaller and more specific. Per VanquishTrader on the People to follow list, free cash flow conversion fell to 54 percent, buybacks were nearly halved, and management anchored long-term growth closer to 30 percent rather than the 50s. UBS blames a delayed ad-model upgrade and a slow nonendemic ramp, and kept a Buy.

3. Memory got taken apart at 4am and bought back at the open. Per the Stocks list, $MU fell 29 percent in July, its worst month since June 2005, after a 189 percent year, and sits under $900 with Korea down another 5 percent overnight. Jefferies cut $SNDK from $3,000 to $1,750, a 42 percent haircut, with Citi to $2,100 and Mizuho to $1,900. RBC went the other way, up to $1,300.

Then the tape reversed it. Per ripster47 on the Financial Markets list, $SNDK, $WDC and $MU were all bought at the open.

The reason to fade the panic is contractual. Per Milk Road AI, Bernstein models roughly 35 percent of all memory bits shipped between 2027 and 2029 as locked into take-or-pay contracts (the buyer pays whether or not it takes delivery) at a floor of $0.26 per gigabyte against a $0.29 spot. Micron has taken $22 billion of customer prepayments for chips it has not made across 16 agreements, and its last quarter was $41.6 billion at an 81.2 percent operating margin.

Spot can crack. A take-or-pay floor on a third of three years of supply cannot crack the same way, and I am holding 60/40 that multiples expand before they compress. Dylan Patel, on the Hermes list, explains the premium better than any model: Micron trades above SK Hynix because "corporate governance exists."

4. Eli Lilly put up $23 billion and is losing the pill race it invented. Per Milk Road, $LLY reported $23 billion of revenue against $20.7 billion expected, up 48 percent year over year, with Mounjaro and the GLP-1 franchise at $9.9 billion in the quarter. Jefferies took its target to $1,440, Morgan Stanley to $1,419 and Leerink to $1,309.

The oral is where the fight is. Novo Nordisk sold roughly $495 million of its pill in the quarter against Lilly's $90 million, a five-to-one drubbing in the format that decides the next decade of this market. Lilly's answer is that prescriptions doubled in the last week of July.

That is one week of scripts against a quarter of sales, and I am taking it anyway at 60/40. Injectables were a manufacturing race and Lilly won it. Pills are a distribution race, and a company printing $23 billion a quarter can buy distribution.

5. Burry is short with entry prices, and Eisman will not follow him. Per the Financial Markets list, Michael Burry published his book with the levels attached: short $NVDA at $198, $MU at $1,051, $AMAT at $729, $TSLA at $416, $SOXX at $642 and $PLTR at $161, verdict "WE ARE NEAR A MAJOR TOP AND POSSIBLY A 1987-TYPE FALL." Per NoLimitGains on the Stocks list, the S&P is up more than 100 percent since he last told people to sell, in January 2023.

Steve Eisman's refusal is the more useful position. Per Prof G Markets, Eisman says "he doesn't have a data point that you and I don't have," and names his trigger precisely: "I need a price war in the LLM world." Until inference pricing collapses, the capex holds.

What sits against him is the customer base. Bloomberg puts 70 percent of Microsoft's AI revenue on OpenAI, Barclays 75 percent of Amazon's on OpenAI and Anthropic, UBS 30 percent of Google Cloud on the same pair. OpenAI lost $21 billion last year, Anthropic roughly $11 billion. Meta meanwhile grew revenue 28 percent against 55 percent expense growth and produced $785 million of free cash flow.

I am 60/40 with Eisman, and the 40 is that concentration number. A price war is not the only way this breaks. One of two customers slowing its orders does it.

The White House handed Micron a tailwind, and the memory trade gets a four-figure case

Washington is pushing Apple off Chinese memory chips, Micron closed back above its 50-day line, and BofA's model puts the stock at $4,000 on 80 percent margins.

SemisAi-InfraFinancialsConsumer

Friday the memory complex was the story. Over the weekend Washington handed it a tailwind, and the neoclouds and the optics stack that feed it are pricing the phase shift into next year.

Market context. The S&P closed Friday at a record near 7,800, on pace for its best August in 41 years but on the lowest monthly volume in 22 years, so the move is thin. Reddit joins the S&P 500 on August 18 and was up about 13 percent on the news. Equity options get pre-market trading from Monday at 7:15am ET. The week's equity catalyst is Nvidia earnings on August 26, now eight sessions out, with that name still the options volume leader.

1. Micron got its catalyst, and the bull case now has a number on it. The White House is advising Apple to stop buying memory chips from China, a direct tailwind for a stock that had already turned. Micron closed Friday back above its 50-day line for the first time in about three weeks, with the resistance ladder at 1022, 1064, 1082, 1121 and 1155 and 991 the overnight gap target.

The weekend case for it is a target, not a hope. Per the Stocks list, BofA models 80 percent gross margins through 2030 and $236 of EPS in fiscal 2030, a 34 percent CAGR against a consensus that has earnings peaking next year and shrinking after. At 15 to 20 times that, the number lands between $3,500 and $4,700, versus a stock under $1,000 today.

The structural math underneath is the same one the house has tracked all week: memory is about 70 percent of hyperscaler capex, the projected 2027 spend is $761 billion, and demand is growing about 200 percent a year against 20 percent supply, with memory prices up 40 to 50 percent. The skeptical read is still live. The Teradyne-style comparison and the marketing-versus-real debate over the memory contracts did not go away, and a stock that has already run has farther to fall if the cycle breaks. But the setup into Nvidia earnings is about as clean as the AI trade gets right now.

2. The neoclouds printed the numbers and the rentals are pricing 2027. Nebius reported Q2 revenue of $582.3 million, up 454 percent, with AI cloud up 514 percent at a 49.7 percent margin, an adjusted EBITDA margin of 41 percent, and a committed backlog above $40 billion. It has contracted 5 gigawatts, more than a gigawatt a year from 2027, and roughly 70 percent prepayments cover half to 60 percent of capex. Daniel Koss guides the 2027 ARR past $20 billion and holds a $1 trillion long-term target against a price near $277. A Birmingham data-center case was allowed to keep building until a March 8 hearing, one less overhang.

Iren is the same trade with a darker-pool tell. It printed about $727 million of dark-pool volume the same day it delivered Horizon 1 to Microsoft, the biggest single-day dark print in a year, and it has 85 percent of year-end capacity contracted toward a $4.4 billion ARR target. The spot market backs the thesis: a three-year-old A100 generation is contracted through 2029 at full freight, and Nebius's first capacity auction cleared 15 percent above the prior record. CME plans to list compute futures in October, with Larry Fink calling compute a financialized resource, the market formalizing the scarcity the neoclouds are selling.

3. The optics stack is the quietest part of the AI trade and the one compounding fastest. The photonics names are printing the kind of numbers memory did two months ago. Applied Optoelectronics is the top pick, with its data-center revenue up from $32 million to $81.4 million year over year, a first 1.6T order above $200 million, and a $1 billion 2026 revenue target that would be up 107.6 percent. Lumentum did Q3 revenue of $808.4 million, up 90 percent, and guided Q4 to $960 million to $1.01 billion. Marvell's Q1 was a record $2.418 billion, up 28 percent, with Celestial AI capacity set to double by fiscal 2028. Coherent carries a $2 billion Nvidia equity stake on top of the optics cycle.

These four names have a common thread: they are the physical layer the hyperscalers cannot buy fast enough, and unlike the memory names they have not had their squeeze already. When Nvidia confirms the buildout on August 26, the optics complex is the group that has not yet repriced for it.

4. The fintech and consumer corners still work on their own numbers. Nu Holdings put up its first billion-dollar quarter, net income of $1.06 billion, up 67 percent, on revenue up 39 percent with return on equity at 33 percent, and the stock rose about 10 percent. The credit tell is the same one the house flagged in the Macro edition last week: 90-day nonperforming loans ticked up to 6.9 percent from 6.5 percent, the highest since 2024, on a portfolio up 37 percent. Nu is a real growth lender, but it is also the first place Latin consumer stress shows up.

Jack in the Box is the contrarian value setup. It trades at about 0.22 times sales against Papa John's at 0.5, Wendy's at 0.6, Starbucks at 3 and McDonald's at 7, after falling from $100 to about $10 and now consolidating. The bull case is that the discount already prices the decline and the franchise is stable enough to re-rate. The bear case is that a broken consumer hurts a value burger chain first. Either way the asymmetry is unusual for a name this beaten down.

5. The froth is real, and it is the counterweight to every bull case this week. Margin debt sits at a record near $1.5 trillion, up 49 percent year over year, about 4 percent of GDP against a 50-year norm near 1.5 percent, and the Shiller CAPE is near 41 against a long-run median of 16. About half of retail options volume is now same-day expiries. Roughly $3 trillion of AI data centers is financed with borrowed money and the industry needs close to $1.5 trillion more by 2028. South Korea is the cautionary example: the KOSPI doubled, retail poured in $70 billion net, and July was a 22 percent drawdown, the worst since the global financial crisis.

None of this makes the bull case wrong. It makes it fragile, and the fragility lives in the leverage, not in the earnings. Into a record close on thin volume with a leveraged book, the position to hold is the one that does not need the market to be right every day.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $MU. Back above the 50-day for the first time in weeks, with resistance at 1022, 1064, 1082, 1121 and 1155, and BofA's $236 fiscal-2030 EPS model points at a four-figure price. Apple-off-China-memory is a fresh tailwind into Nvidia earnings.
  • $NBIS. Q2 revenue up 454 percent, a 41 percent EBITDA margin, and a backlog above $40 billion, with Koss guiding 2027 ARR past $20 billion. The $277 price is the discount to that.
  • $IREN. The $727 million dark-pool day and 85 percent of year-end capacity contracted toward a $4.4 billion ARR target. The neocloud path to $200 runs through Microsoft and the 2027 buildout.
  • $NU. First quarter above a billion dollars of net income, ROE at 33 percent, stock up 10 percent. The 6.9 percent NPL tick is the tell that decides whether this re-rates or rolls over.
  • $JACK. 0.22 times sales against a market that pays 3 to 7 times for peers, after a 90 percent drawdown that is now consolidating. A broken-valuation setup with a live bear case.

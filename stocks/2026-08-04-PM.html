SpaceX reports tonight with 34 percent of its float sold short

Ninety-five percent of borrowable SpaceX stock is already out on loan, the first $100 billion lockup opens Thursday, and Caterpillar just cleared $20 billion of quarterly sales for the first time.

Ai-InfraSemisIndustrialsEnergyConsumer

Yesterday SpaceX printed an all-time low the session before its first earnings report. Today it bounced into the print, and the borrow data says almost everyone has already picked a side.

Market context. The oil move Macro covers this evening did the work here. Futures hit a record high on Bessent's Hormuz comments, and the index arrives at the level that has capped it since spring.

Per Scott Redler on the Financial Markets list, the S&P cleared the 7448 line and pushed to 7610 in the prior session, and the index "may be trying to leave its 10-plus week range." He wants it to hold above 7448, with 7620 the bigger level overhead. Frank Cappelleri's 7,680 target from late April is still live per Ryan Detrick, and Societe Generale now carries 8,000 on broadening earnings strength.

Leadership is the part that does not fit the record. The equal-weight S&P keeps outrunning the cap-weighted index and the rotation is carrying into August. That is not what a megacap-led tape looks like. Tonight's SpaceX and AMD prints land in tomorrow's edition.

The scoreboard.

  • Against, on day one. I carried Shay Boloor's call yesterday that the Lag 7 turn back into the Mag 7 and the generals lead again. The equal-weight index outrunning the cap-weighted one is the opposite of that, and the rotation is where the money actually went.
  • Standing, and improving. My 60/40 that the July low holds into mid-August is intact with futures at a record. The call runs to August 15.

1. The SpaceX print is the most crowded short in the market. Per zerohedge on the Financial Markets list, S3 data shows 95 percent of SpaceX stock available to borrow is out on loan, and short interest is 34 percent of the float. The stock hit an all-time low near $104 yesterday and traded back to $118 before the open.

Per Kyle Reidhead on the Crypto list, here is what the print actually turns on.

That last row is the tell. When the sell side's range spans a loss and a profit, nobody has a model, and a 34 percent short interest into a no-model print is a squeeze setup rather than a thesis.

Heisenberg's framing is the honest one and it is worth quoting: it is so obvious the stock goes down tomorrow that it becomes obvious not to short it, because everyone thinks it is obvious. My lean is 55/45 that SpaceX trades higher on Thursday than it closes tonight, and the reason is mechanical rather than fundamental. The lockup is the real event, and a squeeze into a supply cliff is how the worst entries get made.

2. Caterpillar cleared $20 billion in a quarter for the first time, and the reason is data centres. Per the Financial Markets list, Caterpillar surged as quarterly sales topped $20 billion for the first time in company history, driven by the AI data centre build. Heisenberg's read was two words and a question mark: the AI boom is just getting started.

This is the cleanest version of a trade that keeps being mispriced. Everyone argues about whether the hyperscalers earn a return on $860 billion of capex. Caterpillar does not need them to. It sells the generators, the earthmoving and the power equipment into the build, books the revenue on delivery, and carries none of the residual-value risk that makes the neocloud arithmetic fail.

The same logic ran through Vertiv, which raised full-year guidance across the board and still fell double digits, and it is why the picks-and-shovels layer keeps being the least crowded way to own the theme.

3. Memory is being repriced on supply for the second session running. Yesterday Reuters had CXMT weighing a second Beijing fab that would take its capacity past 600,000 wafers a month, and I cut my memory call to 55/45 on it. Today the complex is still working out what that means.

Per the Financial Markets list, Marvell is soaring, and Scott Redler had Nvidia, Micron, SpaceX and SanDisk as the four names on his premarket board. The distinction that matters has not changed since Kyle Reidhead drew it: CXMT's new capacity is commodity DRAM, and high-bandwidth memory is sold out through 2026.

So the right way to hold this is that a China supply headline hits the commodity end and the margin structure, while the AI-critical end stays contracted. I am staying 55/45 that memory multiples expand before they compress, unchanged, because nothing today touched the HBM book. SanDisk reports tomorrow and is the better read on where commodity pricing actually is.

4. The Hormuz war trades are unwinding before the war ends. There is a whole trade built on the closure, and it has spent two sessions going the wrong way. The names are Frontline in tankers, RTX in defence and ExxonMobil in energy, and each one was priced off a strait that stays shut and shipping that stays rerouted.

Bessent's draft-text comment takes the top off all three at once. Brent at $81.50 after a 4.7 percent Monday fall is not a war premium, it is the market pricing a signature.

The asymmetry now runs the other way. If a deal lands this week these names give back the rest of the move, and if Iran denies it for a fourth time they gap back. Anas Alhajji's open question decides the medium term: can Saudi Arabia actually reroute 6 million barrels a day through the northern Red Sea? Nobody has answered it, and until somebody does the tanker trade has a floor under it that the defence names do not.

5. Apple is down five straight and starting to trade like a defensive. Per Heisenberg on the Financial Markets list, Apple is holding $300 after five down days and a peak-to-trough fall of more than 13 percent, and it has been inversely tracking the Nasdaq proxy.

His setup is the interesting part rather than the level. Once the Nasdaq takes a breather after a large five-day run, he expects defensive money to rotate back into Apple for a bounce. That is a rotation argument dressed as a chart, and it fits the breadth data better than the megacap-leadership story does.

The overhang is real and it is not priced out. Apple guided September below consensus and blamed the memory shortage explicitly, and Bloomberg has MacBook Air orders slipping to September on the same crunch. A component squeeze that delays the best-supplied hardware company in the world does not clear because the stock got cheap.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $SPCX. Reports tonight into 34 percent short interest with 95 percent of the borrow already lent, then more than $100 billion of stock starts unlocking Thursday. The print and the supply cliff are 48 hours apart, so anything you do here is a position on the sequencing rather than on the business.
  • $CAT. First $20 billion quarter in company history on data centre demand, and it carries no residual-value risk on the GPUs. The cleanest non-AI way to own the AI build, and the one nobody argues about at dinner.
  • $AAPL. Holding $300 after five down days and 13 percent off the high, inversely tracking the Nasdaq. A defensive bounce candidate with a real memory-cost overhang into September, so size it as a trade and not a thesis.
  • $FRO. The tanker leg of the Hormuz trade, and the one with a floor under it, because rerouting 6 million barrels a day through the Red Sea is unsolved even if the strait reopens. Rates stay elevated on distance travelled rather than on risk.
  • $SNDK. Reports tomorrow and is the honest read on commodity DRAM pricing after the CXMT capacity headline. High-bandwidth memory is sold out through 2026, so this print tells you about the other end of the market and not about the AI book.
  • $VRT. Raised full-year guidance across the board and fell double digits anyway. Either the market knows something about power-equipment margins or that is the kind of reaction that reverses.

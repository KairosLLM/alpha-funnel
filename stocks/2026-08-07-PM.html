SpaceX ripped 15 percent the day after 911 million shares came free

Rocket Lab bought Iridium for $8 billion and flipped itself to net debt, Damodaran put the hyperscaler capex spread at $200 billion against Apple's $11 billion, and a bad jobs number lifted everything.

Ai-InfraSemisIndustrialsEnergyFinancials

Yesterday I marked the SpaceX squeeze call a miss. It was a day early. The float tripled, the stock went up fifteen percent, and the thing that did it was a jobs report.

Market context. Macro has the print tonight: payrolls at minus 23,000 against plus 80,000 expected, and a September hike repriced out of existence. The tape read it as the Fed being disarmed, and per the Kalshi list the S&P set a new all-time high at 7,740 shortly after the open. Pentosh1 put it plainly: the numbers were so bad they look bearish and are not, because now the Fed cannot hike.

Underneath, the warning lights are all on at once.

The scoreboard.

  • Working. My 60/40 that memory multiples expand before they compress. UBS models over $400 billion of Micron free cash flow through 2028, with $MU 30 percent off its high at under 6 times forward earnings.

1. The unlock was the bottom, not the top. Per the People to follow list, $SPCX ran from roughly $110 to above $128 and finished the week back above Meta's market capitalisation, against a 34 percent short float. Per the Stocks list, TicTocTick had it "up 15%. Best performer... At $108. Now $132." LuxAlgo wrote the epitaph: "New rule: $SPCX unlocks are bullish."

The setup was the reason. Per the Market list, on unlock day the stock traded 1.7 million option contracts, second only to Nvidia, and closed strong rather than breaking. Everyone was positioned for a flood the buyers absorbed.

Now the part nobody priced. Per Diet TBPN, bankers estimate at least 1,000 special purpose vehicles hold SpaceX stock. One has gone badly wrong. Late Stage Management told an investor it sold his SpaceX exposure in 2024 at roughly $105 a share, before a five-for-one split, while his portal and his 2025 tax document still showed 2,500 shares. He put in $17,250, believes the stake was worth over $300,000 at the June IPO, has filed an SEC complaint, and thinks more than 100 others are in the same position. Three sales executives connected to the firm pleaded guilty in February and March inside a $528 million scheme that hid markups of 10 to 100 percent.

Tranches of about 7 percent follow on August 21 and September 10. The float is the story now, and the SPV layer underneath it is unmapped.

2. Damodaran's number is $200 billion against $11 billion, and he will not say which side is wrong. Per Prof G Markets, Aswath Damodaran framed the trade in one line: "Everyone is spending between 150 to 200 billion in capex, but Apple is at 11 billion." One of those strategies is wrong and he calls it a case study ten years out.

The cash flows already show the strain. Meta's free cash flow fell 91 percent. Amazon's and Alphabet's went negative.

Then the accounting. 85 percent of Amazon's net income was unrealised paper gains on its Anthropic and OpenAI stakes. For Alphabet, counting SpaceX and Anthropic, it was 87 percent. Multiples fell below 20 times earnings purely because earnings exploded on marks. His instruction: do not trust net income, climb the income statement, look at operating margins.

The circularity is sourced. Barclays puts 73 percent of Amazon's AI revenue on OpenAI and Anthropic, Wells Fargo 74 percent of Microsoft's, against his own rule that the architecture should be 10 to 20 percent of revenue rather than 80.

Two things to watch. Chinese models went from 8 percent of tokens consumed in January 2025 to above 50 percent now. That is margin compression arriving. And Damodaran names the tell nobody has seen: there has still been no major AI capex write-off. Michael Burry is not waiting. Per the Market list, he shorted $NBIS at $211.77 and $ORCL at $144.63: "The fish have gorged themselves on off-balance sheet liabilities. Backstops. Uncommenced leases. Purchase commitments. The fish have gotten very fat, very large, easy to shoot."

3. Rocket Lab paid $8 billion for Iridium and turned itself into a different company. Per Chip Stock Investor, $RKLB is acquiring $IRDM for $8 billion in stock and cash, taking on Iridium's $1.8 billion of debt and flipping itself from net cash to net debt.

The combination is the argument.

That is a launch company buying its way to positive EBITDA and near-breakeven cash, funded with leverage. Iridium brings 66 satellites in low earth orbit and L-band spectrum that ships direct-to-device this year on a software update alone.

The price is the problem. At roughly $50 billion of enterprise value on $250 million of EBITDA, the reverse discounted cash flow needs 35 percent annual EBITDA growth for ten years to reach about $5 billion by the mid 2030s. SpaceX did just over $4 billion of EBITDA in 2025. Their verdict is the right one: genius on strategy, expensive on price, wait for a better entry. The whole space complex ran today ($RKLB up 7.31 percent, $LUNR 5.96, $PL 5.52, $ASTS 3.49), so no discount is on offer yet.

4. Siemens Energy sells the AI trade without the AI multiple, and nobody noticed. The cleanest non-AI quarter of the week came from a European power-equipment maker, and the case for it is laid out on Reddit rather than by a broker. Revenue hit a record €11.4 billion, profit before special items more than tripled to over €1.6 billion at a 14.2 percent margin, and the backlog reached a record €162 billion with €7.2 billion of free cash flow booked year to date.

The line that matters: grid technologies grew nearly 29 percent. That is the data-centre power bottleneck showing up as revenue in a company nobody calls an AI stock. Siemens Gamesa turned a €75 million profit, its first since 2022.

The stock did not move on the print, blamed on a seasonally softer fourth quarter and weaker wind orders. Backlog projects carry higher margins than the crisis-era contracts rolling off, locking in expansion for two to three years, and a rebranding to Omterra is the stated unlock. I am 60/40 that this rerates before the US grid names do, because it is priced as European industrial rather than as picks and shovels.

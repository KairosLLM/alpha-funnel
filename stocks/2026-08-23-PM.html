Nvidia's $105 billion OpenAI guarantee is a newsletter print

Edelbridge has Nvidia backing up to $105 billion of guarantees on an 8 GW Ohio campus, first 800 MW in 2028, and server prices up more than 15 percent in early 2027 on memory.

SemisAi-InfraEnergySoftware

The new number is not the $91 to $92 billion Street guide I already wrote this morning. Per Edelbridge, Nvidia committed to provide up to $105 billion in guarantees supporting OpenAI's planned Ohio data center campus, designed to reach 8 GW of IT capacity, with the first 800 MW expected online in 2028. That is a newsletter claim. There is no 8-K in what I have. Name the source and size it that way.

Market context. Sunday, so no index recap. The week is earnings: CrowdStrike, Nvidia, and Salesforce, with Intuit on Andy's list too. The new work is the guarantee stack, the SpaceX gigawatt math, and what neoclouds are getting paid per megawatt.

1. Edelbridge put $105 billion of guarantees on Ohio, and called the 2027 price hike. The August 23 portfolio note is the source. Up to $105 billion in guarantees on OpenAI's Ohio campus. Eight gigawatts of IT capacity eventually. First 800 MW in 2028. Prices for servers containing Nvidia AI chips could rise by more than 15 percent in many cases for systems shipping in early 2027, primarily due to higher memory costs. The SOX line in that same note is last week's tape. I already used it. I am not reopening it.

2. Najarro's new angle is the commitment jump, and SpaceX as a $200 billion customer. Per Jose Najarro, the Street $92 billion and the $214 spot are already on this morning's page. The new question is what happens if commitments jump from like $130 billion to $200 billion, against about $130 billion in cash flow from operations. Elon and SpaceX announced they were going exclusively with Nvidia GPUs and would build about 5 gigawatts in 2027, $200 billion potentially from SpaceX. That is the add. Vera Rubin versus Blackwell is the margin question he is watching into Wednesday.

3. The neoclouds are getting paid a lot more per megawatt, and CoreWeave's book is named. Per the Stocks list, the annual run-rate print is $CRWV $9.8 million, $NBIS $9.4 million, $IREN $10.4 million. Nebius just revealed on their earnings call they are now signing deals at $40 to $50 million per megawatt, 4 times their baseline from the start of the year. CoreWeave: over $63 billion in disclosed customer commitments this year alone. OpenAI over $21 billion through 2031. Meta $35 billion plus. Nvidia a $6.3 billion deal as a paying customer. Jane Street $6 billion in April.

The same lists carry a dated financing stack that is not the $3 trillion reprint. Hyperscalers sold $108 billion of those bonds in 2025, about 26 percent of their capex, and $194 billion in the first half of this year alone. Goldman is expecting $400 billion against 2027 capex. Alphabet posted its first negative free cash flow quarter since 2004 in July. Meta handed BlackRock an 80 percent stake in its El Paso site, with $12.5 billion of debt behind it. Same five firms have already disclosed about $1.2 trillion of lease commitments. $1.14 trillion has to get borrowed.

4. CrowdStrike is Wednesday's non-Nvidia print, and NuScale is the nuclear leftover. Per Joseph Hogue, it is a big earnings week with CrowdStrike, Nvidia, and Salesforce. $CRWD reports Wednesday, stock up 62 percent year to date, revenue expected up 23 percent this year, earnings leveraged up 32 percent. His Nvidia line is $92 billion of sales for the three months and earnings nearly double to $29 a share, the same Street cluster I already have. Super Micro: an internal investigation found no evidence that senior management beyond those already indicted were part of the chip-smuggling operation, only director Wally Louu and a few other employees.

Per Rick Orford, the UAMPS Carbon Free Power Project, six NuScale modules at Idaho National Laboratory for 462 MW, was terminated in November 2023. NuScale stock has fallen 74 percent over the past year, 52-week range from $7.58, up 9 percent over the past month. Microsoft's PPA is the restart of an 835 megawatt nuclear facility in Pennsylvania. First SMR targeted for 2030 through 2035.

Micron is back over $1,000 and JPMorgan just made the bull case bigger

The memory trade led Monday on a $1,540 target, AMAT guided its first ten-billion quarter, and the 13F books are agreeing with the buildout.

SemisAi-InfraFinancialsConsumerIndustrials

Monday the memory trade was the tape again, only this time it carried a number that was not there Friday: Micron reclaimed $1,000 and the Street raised the top of the case. The financing story under it is unchanged, and the 13F money is still stacking the same names. This is the read on a session that re-risked the complex.

Market context. The S&P opened near a record just under 7,900 with the Dow down and the Nasdaq up, and the day's action was all in the AI complex. Micron broke back above $1,000, SanDisk ran 11 percent, Cerebras was the biggest gainer at plus 7.8 percent, Marvell traded to $235, and the SK Hynix ADR rose 4 percent. Nvidia sat flat near $225 into its August 26 earnings. Reddit joins the S&P 500 on August 18, and equity options get their first full pre-market session Monday.

The scoreboard. On the 14th I said Micron needed a close above $991 to confirm the next leg toward $1,022. It reclaimed $1,000 on Monday, clearing the level. The 1022 ladder is now the live map, and the next test is the August 26 Nvidia print.

1. Micron is back over $1,000 and JPMorgan gave the bull a fresh target. The day's headline was JPMorgan raising its Micron price target from $550 to $1,540 as the stock reclaimed $1,000, now about 17 percent off its high.

The Micron chief business officer says Per the Market list, 2027 is even tighter than 2026, with NAND prices having roughly doubled in the back half of the year, and BofA's model puts Per the Stocks list, Micron at $236 of fiscal-2030 EPS on 80 percent margins against a consensus of $136. The numbers underneath are doing the work: Micron printed quarterly revenue of $41.5 billion, up 346 percent, $25.11 of adjusted EPS and an 84.9 percent gross margin, and the 16 long-term agreements cover roughly $100 billion of contracted revenue through 2030. The stock trades about six times forward earnings. The resistance ladder is 1022, 1064, 1082, 1121 and 1155, and Micron ran to 991 overnight Friday.

2. AMAT guided its first ten-billion quarter and China is back in the mix. The name under the memory tape that barely got credit is Per Chip Stock Investor, Applied Materials, which guided to its first-ever quarter above $10 billion, more than 50 percent growth at the midpoint, with China back in growth mode at 26 percent of systems and services. It reported Q3 revenue of $9.1 billion, up 25 percent, with gross margin at 50 percent, and says it is doubling system capacity by 2028. That is the equipment side of the same shortage, and it is cheaper than the memory names. The China piece matters twice: it is both a growth engine and the vector a decoupling policy could cut.

3. The financing architecture is the same and the counter-thesis is still live. The $500 billion Nvidia financing story did not move much Monday, but the roadmap did: Per the People to follow list, Nvidia cut its OpenAI data-center backing from $250 billion to under $120 billion and eyes up to $3 billion in SB Energy's Ohio campus. Michael Burry's residual-value counter-thesis is now the named bear: with Nvidia backstopping at most Per the Stocks list, 25 percent of residual value on the platforms, he puts the overstatement of industry profits at about $176 billion over 2026 to 2028. Nvidia reports August 26 with roughly $90 billion of revenue projected, and that filing is the next window for the counter-case.

4. The 13F rotation is the non-AI story of the quarter. The filings landed and the theme is not the AI names, it is who the big money is quietly leaving them for. Per the Polymarket list, Bill Ackman exited Google, trimmed Amazon, and added Meta, Uber and Netflix. Per MARKET OPEN stream, Dalio's firm bought a billion of the SPY while selling Nvidia, Broadcom, Amazon and Google, and Renaissance bought roughly a billion of Nvidia while loading Meta, Intel and Palantir.

The clearest single theme across the books is airlines and credit cards: Delta and United both being bought, Visa and Mastercard new positions, Ackman's Uber at 12 percent of his fund. That is the consumer-and-fintech rotation the house has been waiting for, and it is the part of this quarter that does not depend on the buildout.

5. Tesla is the earnings-grade contrarian that still sits low. Tesla printed record revenue of $28.24 billion, up 26 percent, on 480,126 deliveries, but the quality is poor: Per Rick Orford, operating income fell 57 percent to $398 million on a 1.4 percent margin, GAAP EPS came in at $0.33 against a $0.54 consensus, and free cash flow was negative. A $1 billion unrealized SpaceX gain is 22 cents of the EPS. The robo-taxi proof is the weak leg: only about 21 driverless vehicles running against Waymo's 3,800. The stock still trades near 270 times forward earnings. The bull case is the delivery beat plus a 2030s story. The bear case is a broken consumer and a stretched multiple. The asymmetry is wide and it is the cleanest non-AI setup on the board.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $MU. Back over $1,000 with JPMorgan at $1,540 and BofA's model at $236 of 2030 EPS. The 1022/1064/1082/1121/1155 ladder is the map into August 26.
  • $AMAT. First-ever $10 billion quarter guided, 26 percent China mix, doubling capacity. The cheaper way to play the same shortage.
  • $NVDA. Flat into August 26 earnings with the financing platform behind it and the Burry counter-thesis to track. The catalyst for the whole complex.
  • $TSLA. Record revenue hiding a 1.4 percent operating margin and a negative cash flow quarter. A stretched valuation with a live bear case and a live bull story.
  • $UBER. Ackman keeps adding, the free-cash-flow math gets to roughly $11 billion, and it is the consumer name the 13F money keeps returning to.

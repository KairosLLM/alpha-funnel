1. The Micron bull case changed the subject, and that is the strongest thing I learned today. Per Rick Orford, Micron's hidden moat is automotive and robotics end-market diversification, held together by long-term strategic customer agreements with automakers that give certainty on supply and pricing. The supporting numbers are real: cars with L2-plus autonomy need more than five times the memory of a typical vehicle and are over 20 percent of new sales this year, heading to 40 by 2030, and a humanoid robot needs about ten times an L2 car.

Notice what is absent. He never addresses contract against spot pricing, never mentions the five-year hyperscaler agreements, and never uses the phrase take-or-pay. He also concedes the other side outright, saying the market believes the company is near peak earnings and that analysts expect margins to normalize once supply catches up with demand. His answer is that the floor under each cycle keeps rising, and that is a decade-long argument rather than a next-quarter one.

A bull case that retreats from pricing to end markets is a bull case that has conceded the near term. I am holding 55/45.

The valuation is the honest tension. Micron trades at 9.2 times earnings against SK Hynix at 19, with 40 analysts at strong buy and targets implying 82 to 144 percent upside. Cheap is not the same as early.

The rest of the tape is split. Per the Stocks list, Kyle Reidhead's UBS model has DRAM margins rising to a 95 percent peak by late 2027 with commodity DRAM going $11 to $18 a gigabyte, and calls the cyclical thesis dead until 2029. In the same feed Milk Road's house view is a bust in 18 to 24 months, and intelfabs draws the line that matters: HBM margins hold near 75 percent while conventional DRAM walks a classic commoditization path. That split is where this call settles.

2. A pathologist made the clearest bear case of the day and it is not about AI. Per Chip Stock Investor, Dr. Bradley Gibson's mechanism is that Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement is frozen in nominal dollars while costs inflate. The CMS conversion factor was $31 in 1992 and $32.34 in 2025 against more than 120 percent of cumulative inflation.

The dated catalyst is small and specific. His practice got an email last month about a 4.3 percent across-the-board Medicaid cut in Kentucky because the state ran out of money, and close to a third of Kentucky's roughly 130 hospitals already lose money even after subsidy.

He is bearish on AI drug discovery too, because AI speeds discovery while the trials hold the cost.

3. Rocket Lab and AST both reported, both moved, and both finished flat. Per the People to follow list, Rocket Lab did $234 million of revenue against $231 million expected, up 62 percent, with backlog at $2.36 billion, up 137 percent, and guided the third quarter to $250 to $265 million against a $239 million estimate. It missed on EPS at minus $0.08 and guided an adjusted EBITDA loss of $17 to $23 million against $11.8 million expected.

AST SpaceMobile was the messier one: revenue $32 million against $34 million, EPS of minus $0.77 against minus $0.29, more than a two-times miss, with 2026 revenue guidance nonetheless raised to $175 million.

The reaction is the lesson. Heisenberg had the expected move on both above 11 percent, and by half an hour after the bell his read was "Rocket Lab flat now, HIMS flat now. Both had huge implied moves. Option writers win again?" Per the Stocks list, Rocket Lab later sold off about 10 percent on Peter Beck calling the Neutron year-end window "narrowing," which is one word costing more than a $2.36 billion backlog earned.

Nebius goes into Wednesday carrying its own version of that risk. Per the same list, it has now admitted the roughly 300 megawatt Vineland project slips from year-end 2026 into 2027, after leadership spent weeks saying it was on track. The print is a credibility event before it is a numbers event.

4. Hitting your plan is now the thing destroying your equity value. Per Invest Like The Best, Benchmark's Eric Vishria has the cleanest frame anyone offered on software this week. The 2021 cohort of public software companies traded at 30 times revenue, has quadrupled in size, and now trades at six times. The multiple fell by a factor of six while the businesses got four times bigger.

His line to those chief executives is the part worth keeping: "you have a choice, get to AI or be worth three times revenue," and "every single day that you are hitting your plan, you are destroying equity value." The competitive frontier moved, so executing the old plan is now value-destructive rather than merely dull.

The mechanism he gives is databases, where the moat was always migration cost. Agents do not tire of translating one specification into another and the interfaces are well specified, so migration gets cheap and cost plus scaling becomes the arbiter. Behind that sits an overhang nobody is pricing: roughly 500 private software companies between $100 million and $500 million with no tender, no exit and a missed listing window.

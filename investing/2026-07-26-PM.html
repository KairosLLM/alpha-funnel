1. Alphabet's print was worse than the headline and better than the reaction. Both halves matter. Per Jordi Visser, the reported $9.11 a share included roughly $99 billion of gains on equity stakes, so operational earnings were about $2.85, a slight miss rather than a blowout. Operating cash flow was $39 billion against $45 billion of capex, and that is how the first negative free cash flow quarter happened, and Google is renting Nvidia chips from SpaceX for about $920 million a month.

That connects to this morning's asterisk. Per Ed Yardeni, three companies contributed $9 per share of the S&P's 37.9 percent blended growth through accounting gains on venture investments, and stripping them leaves 22 percent. Alphabet looks like one of the three, and the two findings are the same finding from different ends.

Now the half the selloff ignored. The backlog went from $106 billion to $514 billion in twelve months, up $50 billion in the quarter, with roughly $260 billion recognisable over the next twelve against about $200 billion of capex. On his framing the backlog is the whole ball game and the negative cash flow is a timing artifact.

One source called this failure mode before the print. Per Rick Orford, on Alphabet's custom silicon and the stack around it, the cash goes out first while the benefits arrive later, so Alphabet could be successful while the stock underperforms. His numbers never get a line item: Gemini serving costs down 78 percent last year, core AI response costs down more than 30 percent after Gemini 3. Alphabet does not need to replace Nvidia, only to keep reducing the tax.

He is honest about the odds, and that is what makes the number usable. He puts it at 70 to 75 percent that the capex programme is value-accretive over three to five years, and names the three risks: chip lives closer to four years than six, more than a trillion dollars of off-balance-sheet commitments, and backlog concentration. If that $514 billion is mostly OpenAI and Anthropic and OpenAI busts, it evaporates.

2. The Fed is the sharpest dislocation on the board, and Warsh has no comfortable move. Per BlackPantherCap, June payrolls came in at 57,000 against 115,000 expected with the prior two months revised down 74,000, and unemployment held at 4.2 percent only because 720,000 people left the labour force. June CPI printed minus 0.4 percent month over month, the biggest drop since April 2020, with core flat at 2.6 percent. Then the war restarted on July 8, Brent hit $100 on the 23rd, and September hike odds went from below 53 percent to around 82 in a week.

His conclusion is the one to carry into Wednesday: Warsh cannot cut into an oil shock and cannot hike hard into a weakening jobs market, so he holds and lets oil decide. As that desk puts it, the number that matters is not the CPI print, it is the count of ships.

Per Visser, the reason not to panic is that inflation swaps and two-year breakevens have not budged despite the oil move and the first non-zero CPI read does not arrive until mid-September. His line: they may raise rates and it is not going to kill the market. Per Hogue, PCE Thursday is expected to fall to 3.7 percent from about 4.

My lean, and I am moving it. This morning I said 70/30 for a hold. The venue spread argues for more confidence on the decision and less on the tone, so call it 85/15 hold and 50/50 that the statement reads hawkish enough to move the tape. The reason to trust the hold over the CME 35 percent: both macro sources quoting that hike probability still predict a hold themselves, so the 35 is a hedge rather than a forecast.

3. Memory got louder on both sides, and one of the loudest bulls just admitted the sentiment turned. The fundamental case is now enormous. Per Melvin, Meta and Micron published an LPDDR5X paper showing one third the power of DDR5 at under 7 percent of system draw and two to three times the throughput in Spark SQL. DRAM contract prices jumped as much as 95 percent in the first quarter, Micron's data-centre revenue runs above a $100 billion annualised rate and up sevenfold year over year, and Goldman sees undersupply through 2027. Per Milk Road AI, DRAM prices are up 171 percent year over year and Micron is sold out through the end of 2026.

4. The neocloud trade is a balance-sheet argument, and two desks reach opposite conclusions on the same stock. Per Milk Road AI, the two names are CoreWeave and Nebius. CoreWeave has signed $63.9 billion this year alone, including more than $21 billion from OpenAI to 2031, $14.2 billion plus a further $21 billion from Meta, $6.3 billion from Nvidia and $6 billion from Jane Street, for $99.4 billion of obligations against roughly a $50 billion market cap. It also carries $24.9 billion of debt, $536 million of quarterly interest and a $740 million net loss, with free cash flow not positive until 2029 or 2030.

Nebius is preferred for that asymmetry: $9.3 billion of cash and no meaningful net debt, AI cloud revenue up 841 percent to $389.7 million, a guide of $3 to $4 billion toward a $7 to $9 billion run rate, and $17.4 billion of Microsoft backlog. In a week when the Fed decides, owning the one that does not need the rate to cooperate is cleaner.

Then the opposite view, from someone putting money on it. Per Invest with Henry, CoreWeave is his favourite AI name to sell puts against at $80 with an RSI of 35, below its 50-day, selling the August 21 $75 put for $7.50 against 102 percent implied volatility, with the $65 strike at 21 delta as the safer version. Same stock, and the leverage is either the reason to avoid it or the reason the options are paid.

One note strengthens both sides. Asked how guaranteed those commitments are, Melvin says they have to pay up and there is no way around it. Same take-or-pay structure as Micron's memory agreements, different corner of the supply chain.

5. Iran de-escalated for a second night and a tanker hit a mine anyway. Per the Market list, Iran has tied its military pause to the American suspension after 13 consecutive nights, and an Iranian source reports more scepticism than optimism. Per the Financial Markets list, an oil tanker struck a naval mine in the strait and exploded, and oil spiked above $100 into a week carrying a rate decision and four megacap reports.

The price read is the tradeable part. Per Heisenberg, the oil fund hit $142.50 Thursday on its most overbought reading in three and a half months and he expects roughly $130. Against that, per the Polymarket list, a wallet created yesterday put its entire $18,000 on Hormuz traffic normalising by August 31, roughly 15 cents on yes. Tonight's News edition has the reason the shooting stopped, and it is not diplomacy.

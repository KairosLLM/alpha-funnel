The forced seller had a name, and Citadel bought the book

Situational Awareness dumped its entire public equity book in one block, everything it owned ripped twenty percent, and Microsoft had its best day since 2008 while market breadth stayed negative all session.

MacroStocksBitcoin

Yesterday the question was whether the AI trade was breaking. This morning it turned out to be a margin call with an address.

Market context. Two macro prints landed before the open and neither one explains the day. Per the Polymarket list, second-quarter GDP came in at 1.5 percent against 2.1 percent expected. Per the Crypto list, core PCE rose 0.1 percent on the month against 0.2 percent expected, with headline at 3.7 percent and core at 3.3 percent year over year, both in line. A growth miss and a tenth of cooler core inflation.

Per threadguy's open, the tape went up anyway, hard and narrow. Microsoft rose about 15 percent to roughly $445, its best day since 2008, adding close to $500 billion of market value on its highest short interest in a decade. QQQ gained almost 3 percent, SOXX added 10 percent, and roughly a trillion dollars went back in by early afternoon.

Then the part that matters more. Per the Financial Markets list, Linda Raschke watched breadth stay negative from the opening bell across the top forty NYSE and top forty Nasdaq names she tracks, "with semis and MSFT painting the tape." Mark Minervini is still carrying the SPY short he put on July 27, notes the indexes remain below their fifty-day averages, and calls this the most bifurcated market of a 43-year career. A trillion dollars came back on two sectors.

The long end is still the constraint. The thirty-year hit 5.24 percent overnight, a 19-year high, before easing during the session. Kalshi now prices a 69 percent chance the Fed hikes at some point this year and an 89 percent chance it does not cut. For September specifically, Polymarket sits near 56 percent and CME FedWatch splits it 60/40, so the 55/45 I published Wednesday needs no revision.

That call runs to August 10 and stays open, marked against.
  • Due tomorrow. The S&P needs 7,499.36 on Friday's close to keep eleven straight green Julys. Galaxy Digital's end-July catalyst also lands.

1. Situational Awareness sold everything, and the buyer was the firm that called for a hike two days earlier. Start with what four named outlets have actually reported, because the rumour layer around this is thick. Per Diet TBPN, Bloomberg had the fund seeking capital at 9:25pm Wednesday, the Financial Times had the LP letters the same night, CNBC reported at 6:05am Thursday that Leopold Aschenbrenner had been forced to unwind all public stock positions, and the Wall Street Journal reported at 8:39am that Ken Griffin's Citadel had agreed to buy the portfolio. CNBC's David Faber adds that the exit covered the entire public book, longs and shorts, through a single buyer.

The book was long AI infrastructure and short software. Per the Market list, the largest positions were Nebius, SanDisk, CoreWeave, IREN, Bloom Energy and TeraWulf, with a short leg in names like Adobe. Concentration was extreme, though the accounts disagree on which name was largest.

Then the day itself. Per the Stocks list, every one of those names ripped the moment the seller was replaced: Bloom Energy up 26 percent, Nebius 25 and briefly 33, SharonAI 26, IREN 25, SanDisk 20, CoreWeave 20, AMD 13. Micron gained 15. That is what removing a forced seller looks like, and it is not the same thing as news.

Now the part everyone is saying and nobody has proved. Citadel publicly forecast a surprise Fed hike on Tuesday. The market sold off. The Fed held Wednesday. Situational Awareness was margin called. Citadel bought the book Thursday. Per the Financial Markets list, InvestorsLive, Mr_Derivatives, NotSoEasyMoney and ThinkingUSD all lay out that sequence and draw the obvious conclusion. I report the sequence because it is documented and the inference because it is widespread. There is no evidence of intent, and a wrong rate call is not a crime. Hold it as a question about market structure rather than a finding.

I marked Citadel's hike forecast a miss on Wednesday. It stays a miss. Being wrong on the rate and right on the trade are different scoreboards.

2. Nobody knows how bad it was, and the number people keep repeating is not sourced. There is still no confirmed drawdown figure. Per Martin Shkreli, who broke the story before the wires, one of the three bidding parties told him his numbers were "a little off" and refused to say in which direction, and he got pushback that the fund was "only down 30%." His own arithmetic is explicitly hypothetical, and the 4x leverage figure is his sourcing too.

The assets under management do not agree either, with $45 billion, $24 billion and $20 billion all in circulation. Those do not reconcile and I am not picking one.

Two things I withheld on Wednesday held up. The "down more than 50 percent" line remains unconfirmed. The claim that he held billions of puts across the semiconductor complex now looks actively wrong, because a levered long book taken over by its prime brokers is the opposite of a hedged one.

The best frame came from Numerai's Richard Craib. At 150 percent volatility, variance drag alone is 113 percent a year and risk of ruin is roughly a coin flip over a fund's life. "A child can do the math on a napkin." A position-sizing outcome, not a verdict on AI infrastructure.

3. Microsoft and Meta finally explained the capex split, and it is about who has to borrow. Per the People to follow list, Azure passed $100 billion of annual revenue for the first time and grew 43 percent, its fastest since early 2022 and up from 39 percent two quarters ago. Capex came in at $41 billion against roughly $42 billion expected. Citi took its target to $600, Wells Fargo to $650, Piper Sandler to $550, all the same morning.

Meta is the same story inverted, and the number is stark. It bought back zero stock last quarter and borrowed $24.9 billion instead. Long-term debt went from $58.7 billion at year end to $83.7 billion. Property and equipment sits at $225.7 billion, up nearly $50 billion in six months. A year ago the same quarter carried $10.2 billion of buybacks and no new debt.

That is the actual distinction the market priced. Not discipline versus indiscipline, but funded from cash versus funded from the bond market, at a moment when the thirty-year sits at a 19-year high.

Per Prof G Markets, DA Davidson's Gil Lurria narrows it to a single word. Amy Hood said capex would be "up" year over year. Alphabet's finance chief said it would grow "significantly" a week earlier and got punished for it. Same buildout, different verb, ten points of stock. One caveat on Microsoft's side, from the Stocks list: Amy Hood is extending data-centre useful life from 15 to 25 years starting in fiscal 2027, which cuts annual depreciation on the same assets and flatters everything below the capex line.

4. Memory got fundamentally better and cheaper on the same day. Per the Crypto list, Samsung posted second-quarter operating profit of 89.5 trillion won against 4.68 trillion a year ago, on revenue up 130 percent to 171.5 trillion won. Both records. SK Hynix cleared 50 trillion won of quarterly revenue for the first time at a record operating margin, reported between 72 and 76 percent.

The guidance is the thesis. Samsung says supply stays short into 2028 and that the 2027 shortage will be worse than this year's. Both Korean makers have stopped signing multi-year contracts entirely, betting on higher prices through 2027. SK Hynix describes HBM, DRAM and NAND as essentially sold out for 2026 with lines booked through 2027. The mechanism underneath is that high-bandwidth memory eats roughly three times the wafer capacity per gigabyte of standard DRAM, so AI demand starves the commodity side too.

Against that, both trade near four times this year's earnings and three times next year's, against roughly 35 times for the rest of the chip complex. UBS initiated SK Hynix at a $204 target, and Chairman Choi Tae-won bought more than $3 million of his own stock in the open market.

I stay 65/35 that memory multiples expand before they compress, unchanged. Today's 15 percent bounce did not reclaim 832 on Micron, so nothing about the level map changed either.

5. Korea is nearly done deleveraging, and that is the cleanest bullish fact available. Per the Stocks list, the KOSPI closed at 5,663.24, down 5.98 percent after a 10.84 percent drop the day before, with circuit breakers firing on both the KOSPI and the KOSDAQ two sessions running for the first time simultaneously. The month is the worst in Korean market history, past October 1997 at 27 percent and October 2008 at 23 percent.

The human number is worse than the index number. Citi counts more than 360,000 forced margin liquidations with 62 percent of them under age 35. Per Lark Davis, 1.2 million accounts were margin called and 34 trillion won evaporated in a month, roughly one in every thirty Korean adults.

Here is why it matters for positioning. Goldman's Alvin So has Korea-focused leveraged ETF assets down from a $53 billion peak on June 22, then 3.2 percent of Korean free float, to $15 or $16 billion now, about 1.5 percent. More than seventy percent of the exposure is gone. JPMorgan puts the deleveraging between 75 and 90 percent complete. From July 31 the minimum cash deposit on new leveraged ETF purchases jumps to 30 million won, about $20,300, cash only.

My lean. The mechanical seller is nearly out of ammunition and that is genuinely bullish, but one 3 percent day on negative breadth is not a bottom. I am 60/40 that the July low holds into mid-August, and the 40 is Minervini's point that nothing has printed a follow-through day yet. The thing that would flip me is the thirty-year rather than the tape.

The Journal put a number on it, and Situational Awareness lost 67 percent

Microsoft added $450 billion of market value in one session, the largest single-day gain on record, Apple beat and fell anyway on a soft September guide, and Amazon put $20 billion of memory cost straight into capex.

MacroStocks

For two days the drawdown at the centre of this selloff had no number attached. Now it does, and it is worse than the rumour I declined to print.

Market context. Into the last session of July, the setup is a violent reversal still looking for confirmation. Thursday was the squeeze: more than a trillion dollars went back into US equities, the S&P rose better than a hundred points, and per the People to follow list three American companies each added over $100 billion of market value in a single day. Microsoft added $450 billion, Micron $153 billion, Nvidia $122 billion.

Microsoft's move deserves its own line. Up roughly 15 percent, the largest single-day market-cap gain in stock market history. Per the Stocks list, OddStats counts only three better days in the company's entire history, and they came in October 1987, October 2000 and October 2008. That is the company this move keeps.

Overnight the follow-through has held. S&P and Nasdaq 100 futures are both green, the KOSPI is up around 14 percent with SK Hynix and Samsung each better than 25 percent, and Seoul is putting roughly $13.9 billion into its sovereign wealth fund for AI data centres. The Bank of Japan held at 1.00 percent on an 8-1 vote after the finance ministry intervened in the yen ahead of the decision.

Two things to watch into the open. Apple sits down about six and a half percent after hours and Amazon up seven to ten. And it is month end, with the S&P needing 7,499.36 at today's close to keep eleven straight green Julys.

1. Sixty-seven percent, and the fund is already raising again. Per the Financial Markets list, the Journal's headline is flat: Situational Awareness down 67 percent in July. Set that against a fund that was up 439 percent after fees through June 30 and, per the Crypto list, carried a net asset value of $45 billion on July 1. The shape of it is clear. This was not a bad month layered on a bad year. It was one month eating five months of a spectacular run.

The mechanics still rest on Martin Shkreli's reconstruction rather than a filing, and he says so. His arithmetic, relayed on TBPN: start at $45 billion, assume $10 billion sits in Anthropic, leaving roughly $35 billion of equity, run it three to four times levered for about $120 billion of gross market value. A 25 percent drop in gross takes $30 billion off, which sounds survivable until you notice it comes out of the $35 billion of equity rather than the $120 billion of gross. "No prime broker is going to let you keep $90 billion of GMV."

What is new since yesterday is smaller and stranger. Citadel Securities reportedly paid around $10 billion for most of the public book. The Anthropic stake was retained. And per the Kalshi list, Aschenbrenner was already seeking new capital inside 24 hours of the blowup. Polymarket has opened a market on whether he is criminally charged, which tells you more about the audience than the odds.

One correction I owe from yesterday. I wrote that the accounts disagreed on assets under management, with $20 billion, $24 billion and $45 billion in circulation. The Journal's $45 billion NAV as of July 1 is the figure to use.

2. Was Thursday a bottom or a squeeze? I lean squeeze, and the odds are not close. Ron Insana said it plainly on the tape: semis up 15 to 24 percent "smacks of a short-squeeze, not a meaningful bottom." Mac10's read on Microsoft is harsher, that the biggest rallies happen in bear markets. Against them, JPMorgan has the Korean leveraged-ETF unwind essentially finished, Goldman's Alvin So has those assets down from a $53 billion June peak to $15 or $16 billion, and Tom Lee's prime-brokerage data shows hedge funds degrossing tech at the fastest pace in nearly a decade.

Both can be true, and here is the distinction that matters. The forced seller really is gone, which removes the thing that made every red day worse. That is not the same as demand arriving. A squeeze that removes an overhang leaves you at a fair price with no buyer, and the tell will be whether the second and third days hold rather than whether the first one was violent.

I am 60/40 that the July low holds into mid-August, unchanged from Wednesday. The one thing that would move me is the long end, and the thirty-year has not printed a level worth trading on since it cleared 5.24 percent.

3. Apple beat on both lines and fell up to eight percent, and the quality of the beat is why. Per the People to follow list, earnings came in at $2.02 against $1.89 and revenue at $109.42 billion against $108.65 billion, with gross margin at 50.1 percent, the highest in company history. iPhone grew 22 percent and Mac beat by more than a billion and a half.

Then read the composition. Eleven cents of that earnings beat and roughly two points of the gross margin came from $600 million of tariff refunds. Strip them and the quarter is $1.91 against $1.89 expected, a two-cent beat rather than a thirteen-cent one, at a gross margin near 48 percent rather than a company record. Services missed at $30.74 billion, Greater China missed at $18.82 billion, and iPad missed. September revenue is guided to 9 to 11 percent growth against the 16 percent just delivered, with gross margin guided down to 47 to 48 percent, and that range already includes a point of the same tariff benefit.

Apple went into the print at 35 times forward earnings against a five-year average of 28, up 22 percent on the year. At that multiple a beat propped by a tax refund and guided down afterwards is a sell, and the tape walked it from minus three to nearly minus eight through the evening.

This was Tim Cook's final earnings call as chief executive.

4. Amazon's headline beat is mostly a mark, and its capex line is the real news. Earnings of $5.75 against $1.82 expected looks like the beat of the year until you find the composition. Per the Polymarket list, quarterly profit was $62.6 billion and the gain on the Anthropic stake accounted for most of it, booked at $53.4 billion in one account and roughly $36 billion in another. Either way, strip it and you have a good operating quarter rather than a historic one.

The good operating quarter is real. AWS did $42.2 billion, up 37 percent, its fastest in 18 quarters, at a 39.4 percent operating margin against 33.8 expected, with $496 billion of contracted backlog not yet online. Amazon crossed $200 billion of quarterly revenue for the first time.

The cost of it is where I would spend your attention. Capex guidance for 2026 went to $220 billion, and management named higher memory prices as the reason for roughly $20 billion of that. Second-quarter capex was $54.2 billion against $32.1 billion a year ago. Trailing free cash flow has swung to negative $7.6 billion from positive $18.2 billion, and long-term debt has nearly doubled since December to $128.9 billion. Guidance for the third quarter came in at $197 to $202 billion against $204 billion expected.

5. Two of the largest buyers on earth confirmed the memory squeeze within an hour of each other. Amazon put $20 billion of memory cost into its capex line. Tim Cook, asked about DRAM, said Apple is "evaluating all options" with supply concentrated among Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung. Apple's inventories have nearly doubled since September, from $5.7 billion to $11.1 billion. That is what stockpiling ahead of a shortage looks like on a balance sheet.

That is the demand side agreeing with what the suppliers have been saying all week. SK Hynix has sold out its 2026 high-bandwidth memory, lines are booked through 2027, and Samsung guides tightness into 2028.

The trade is no longer cheap, though. Micron reclaimed 872 and ran to 925 overnight, roughly 30 percent off its trough inside 24 hours. Michael Burry used the move to add to his Micron short at $880 and to a semiconductor index short at $506. When the fundamental case is this well telegraphed and the stock has moved this fast, the edge sits in the levels rather than the story, and 906 is the number that confirms it.

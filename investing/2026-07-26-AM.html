1. The contracts are take-or-pay with floor prices, and that is the strongest single fact for the bull case. Per Rick Orford, Micron's 16 strategic agreements include 14 carrying roughly $100 billion of cumulative revenue at minimum contract prices, plus $22 billion of cash deposits and related commitments. The part nobody had put on the page until now is the structure: they are take-or-pay, so the customer either lifts the volume or still owes the money, and most of the largest agreements carry explicit floor prices. High-bandwidth memory is sold out through the end of the year.

That is the mechanism by which $100 billion survives a spot-price collapse, and it is exactly what the bear case needs to break. His own framing of the risk is sharper than most bears manage: memory stocks look most expensive at the bottom of the cycle and cheapest near the top, so 13 times forward earnings is precisely what a peak prints.

He also hands over two falsification triggers with a date attached, and that is rare in this format. A miss on the roughly $50 billion guidance in late September guts it. So does any whisper that floor prices are bending, and he concedes those floors are untested by a real downturn and that a customer facing slashed budgets would plausibly renegotiate or pay damages and walk.

One correction to carry forward. Burry initiated the Micron short near $1,051 in early July and added at $934, so with Micron at 961 he is roughly flat on the add rather than comfortably ahead. Per the Stocks list, Mentovia's response to the panic is worth the space: one investor's position is not an investment thesis and a 13F is not a crystal ball. Note also that CNBC's scorecard of Burry's July has his Tesla and Caterpillar legs paying off and does not mention Micron at all.

2. Tomorrow's Chinese memory IPO has one market-implied opinion and it is bearish. Nobody on the X lists is talking about the listing itself, and that silence says something about how narrowly this is being watched. What exists instead is a live position, and it is large, in profit and still being added to. That whale is short into a listing rather than positioned for a pop, with liquidation at $13.45 giving a clean level to watch.

Set that against what the sell side thinks the real supply threat is. Per Orford, his supply worry is not CXMT at all, which he never mentions. It is SK Hynix, whose July 10 Nasdaq listing raised about $26.5 billion and whose mid-to-long-term Korean fab plan runs to roughly $800 billion, with the pricing pressure landing in 2027 and beyond. Two credible bears, two completely different threats, on two different clocks.

The flows say the rotation is real. Per the People list, last week saw $9.21 billion leave the largest S&P tracker, $5.03 billion leave the Nasdaq proxy, $1.42 billion leave semis and $1.16 billion leave software, while $3.53 billion went into Vanguard's S&P fund and $1.72 billion into financials. One desk is positioned for it rather than commenting on it. Per Stocks with Josh, his hourly momentum screen has sell signals on the majority of the Magnificent Seven and he is making money short, with a stated rule of no longs next week unless a name is back above the zero line.

4. Earnings are the best since 2021 and Yardeni found the asterisk. The headline is genuinely strong. Per the People list, 27 percent of the S&P has reported with 86 percent beating on earnings and 80 percent on revenue, and blended growth is running 37.9 percent against 23.2 percent expected at the end of June.

Then the footnote that should change how you read it. Per Ed Yardeni, three companies added $9 per share of that growth through accounting gains on venture investments, and stripping those out leaves 22 percent. The AI capital cycle is now showing up inside reported earnings per share rather than only in capex lines, which makes the quality of this quarter harder to trust than the number suggests.

Buffett's posture is the one to hold against it. Per the People list, he calls the market a church with a casino attached, says we have never had people in a more gambling mood, sits on roughly $380 billion of cash and says plainly that we do not do anything.

5. Wednesday puts the Fed and four of the six biggest reports in one session, and the hike has been quietly de-priced into worsening data. The setup is asymmetric and nobody seems bothered. Polymarket has 75 percent unchanged with no cut priced anywhere, while per the Financial Markets list import prices ran 7.1 percent higher year over year in June and 4.2 percent excluding fuel, global bond yields sit at their highest since the financial crisis, and per the Market list US crude has gone from $68 on July 1 to $90.

Per the Kalshi list, the bond market is signalling that investors should start worrying about the war again even as equities shrugged, with weekend pricing marking the Nasdaq proxy only 0.53 percent higher on the strike-halt news. Bonds and stocks are reading the same weekend differently.

My lean, with odds. I am 70/30 that Wednesday delivers a hold rather than a hike, in line with the market, but I am closer to 50/50 that the statement reads hawkish enough to matter, because oil up a third since the war began and import prices at 7.1 percent give Warsh no room to sound relaxed. The trade that expresses it is not a rate bet, it is respecting that Microsoft, Meta, Robinhood and SoFi all report into that decision, so position sizes should assume one session can reprice both the discount rate and the earnings.

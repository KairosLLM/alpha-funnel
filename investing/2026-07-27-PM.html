I said this morning the memory thesis would reprice on Samsung's print rather than on the Chinese listing, and by 10:38 the tape had answered me

Micron fell 6.1 percent through a trillion dollars, ASML was halted after an 8 percent drop, and Apple took the largest-company crown back from Nvidia. The trigger was not CXMT alone. It was China mass-producing its own DUV lithography tools on the same morning Nvidia's credit spreads started widening on the OpenAI guarantee.

This morning I said CXMT's debut does not touch the HBM shortage on any timeline that matters this quarter, so memory would reprice on Samsung's print and not on a Chinese listing. The fundamental half may still be right. The market half was wrong within three hours. The session, the scoreboard, then five stories.

The session. Stocks gapped up hard on the Iran pause and gave it all back before lunch. Per the Market list, the Dow was up 1.08 percent at the bell, the S&P 500 banks index hit a record high up 1.3 percent, and airlines ripped as crude fell over 7 percent, JetBlue up 6.5 and United up 4. Then a report that a state-backed Chinese firm had begun mass-producing domestic DUV lithography tools hit the semis, and at 10:38 the same feed printed the Nasdaq down 0.5 percent and the S&P down 0.1.

Be straight about the limits: that 10:38 print is the last index-level number in tonight's sources, so treat everything below as intraday, not a close. Per the Kalshi list, $700 billion was added at the open, the S&P later turned negative as chips led, and over $1 trillion came off US stocks in two hours. $GOOGL crossed $4 trillion on the same tape.

1. China broke the memory trade, and the rebuttal is better argued than the selling. Per the Financial Markets list, CXMT closed its debut up roughly 465 to 500 percent near a $500 billion valuation, the most valuable company in mainland China, and it is also the destination for China's first domestic immersion DUV deliveries. The Market Ear: "Wall Street de-risked tech. China didn't. Instead, it launched a $500 bn DRAM champion and appears to have started producing domestic DUV lithography systems."

Melvin did the arithmetic the tape ignored. Per the Market list, Shanghai Micro Electronics ran its own 28-nanometer DUV tools above 90 percent utilisation on SMIC lines this year, and 28 nanometres is over 60 percent of the global chip market, so this is real. But ASML took twenty years and roughly $40 billion to walk from 65 nanometres to below 3, and China sits at the 65-nanometre line ASML occupied in 2003. Wayne Liang adds the volume: about five machines this year against the hundreds ASML ships, and DUV is not EUV.

Then Morgan Stanley added a separate bear catalyst, warning memory contract prices could peak in late 2026. Shay Boloor's SanDisk rebuttal is checkable: $42 billion of remaining performance obligations on multiyear deals with floors near $0.29 per gigabyte, so a pricing peak is not an earnings peak. On Micron and SK Hynix he calls the selloff overdone, because CXMT has no meaningful HBM presence and no EUV access.

My lean, with odds. The fundamental case survives at 70/30, because per the Crypto list Kimi K3's weights need roughly 1.4 terabytes of memory just to load at 4-bit and HBM is sold out through 2027, so frontier open models are a memory demand story. The multiple does not survive, and that is the half I got wrong. A trade can be right on units and wrong on price for two quarters.

2. Nvidia became a bank this weekend and the credit market noticed before the equity did. Per the Market list, it is weighing roughly $250 billion of guarantees on OpenAI's 10-gigawatt Ohio campus, a project that could top $500 billion, first phases in 2028, on land previously used for federal nuclear work. Futurum: Nvidia is "increasingly the credit backstop for its own demand."

The new fact is the pricing. Kyle Reidhead reports Nvidia's CDS spreads rising on the news, and he is a bull volunteering it. Cramer named it twice, calling it the "First National Bank of Nvidia" and adding that "if there were no financing involved Nvidia's stock would be soaring," with the market fixated on it "BECAUSE of memories of 2000." Per the Stocks list, John Galt put it in a position, "Added $NVDA short on the company becoming a bank," and Mark Minervini added an SPY short with a stop above 755, citing August to September as the weakest two-month stretch in 37 years. Nothing is signed.

3. The debt behind all of this is off balance sheet and larger than the debt they report. Per Prof G Markets, Nikkei Asia puts roughly $1.65 trillion of off-balance-sheet obligations across Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle against about $1.35 trillion of reported debt, with Bloomberg nearer $1.8 trillion. Meta's is $420 billion, three times what it reports. Per the clip, Scott Galloway: "when you're hiding a bigger number than what you're reporting, that's not accounting. It's concealment."

Ed Elson supplies the mechanism and a correction to the frame I have been running. The vehicles are not hidden recourse, they are genuine risk transfer, and "the guy responsible for that isn't big tech, it isn't Nvidia. It's the private credit funds." That cuts against the supplier-underwrites-demand argument I quoted Sunday rather than confirming it. Both can hold at once, but they are different failure modes. Their precedent is First Brands, Chapter 11 in September 2025 on private credit money.

Per Mark Cuban, the efficiency case strands all of it. He grants utilisation will scale as expected, but if a price-performance curve minimises power requirements, "there's going to be a lot of data centers that are going to be turned into pickleball courts." His analogy is dark fiber at pennies once 1-gigabit became 100, and he calls borrowing on top of fully-committed cash flow "planning for perfection."

Galloway then breaks with his own producer, and that is the honest part of the episode. He argues leverage equates to growth, and on Meta: "Meta is such a cash volcano that I'm not as worried about the debt of Meta." His worry is the cash-flow-negative adjacents.

Set against it, per Anthony Pompliano, four charts say the bubble is a myth: S&P net margin on track for 15.7 percent in Q2, the highest since 2009, 86 percent of reporters beating EPS, and the Nasdaq 100 at 24 times against 86 in May 2001. Read the two side by side and notice they never touch. Pompliano answers an equity question with equity data and never mentions debt, vehicles or leverage. The bull case is measured in margins, the bear case in liabilities that never reach the multiple.

4. Alphabet is the one name everybody is buying for four incompatible reasons. Per Fin Tek, Berkshire's latest 13F shows another $10 billion of Alphabet, nearly tripling the stake on brand and engineering talent. Per Tom Nash, it is a discounted toll booth at $320, down 22 percent from its high, on a 2030 target of $560, with cloud operating income up 215 percent as margins went from 20.6 to 35.6 percent. Per the Milk Road AI email, their analyst bought the 7 percent post-earnings drop, is down about 13 percent, and rests on Gemini's 950 million monthly users.

Elson uses the identical 82 percent cloud growth to argue the opposite, estimating roughly half of it comes from OpenAI and Anthropic, both venture-funded and cash-flow negative. Same number, four verdicts, nobody citing anybody. The figure that settles it is undisclosed.

5. Wednesday's Fed is a hike-risk meeting with no dot plot and a brand-new chair at the microphone. Per Arbitrage Andy, hike odds sit near 36 percent and there is no updated dot plot, so "it is likely every word in the statement and Warsh's press conference will carry more weight than usual." Per the Financial Markets list, September hike odds are above 50 percent, up from 18 a week ago. Per the Milk Road crypto letter, the same repricing reads as 1-in-10 to 4-in-10 in a week, driven by oil through $100.

Kalshi is the outlier, still calling a fifth straight hold at 3.50 to 3.75 percent without publishing a number, which leaves this morning's 83 percent standing twenty points from everyone else two days out.

So I am moving mine. A hold goes from 85/15 to 80/20, because the oil relief that argued for patience arrived alongside a strike on Saudi processing capacity. A hawkish-enough statement goes from 45/55 to 60/40, because with no dot plot the statement and the presser are the only instruments left.

Market context, and read the timestamp before you read the levels. Every source in tonight's pull terminates at 12:00 noon Eastern. That is a structural property of the PM digests rather than a gap in the reporting, and the one non-digest route to the closing bell, a two-hour market-open stream, arrived without captions. So this is a morning recap with a hard stop at noon. The Tuesday close reaches you in tomorrow's AM edition. Inside that window the session did something worth the whole edition. QQQ hit $673 premarket, which put the Nasdaq 100 down ten percent from its June peak and into a correction. Per the Stocks list, it took 38 trading days to get there against more than a hundred for the prior ten-percent drawdown, and this was the fifth consecutive down day, the worst run of 2026. By 10:39am the index was eleven percent off its all-time high. At 11:42am it was green.

The catalyst landed at 11:20am, a Times of Israel report that mediators believe the United States and Iran are close to reviving the memorandum that collapsed earlier this month. Crude fell five percent on it, Brent went below $84 and WTI below $80, and the ten-year sat at 4.5 percent. Hold the oil move against one fact from the Financial Markets list: physical transits through Hormuz today were zero tankers in either direction. Crude fell five percent on a report about a document while the actual waterway carried nothing.

SPY held $735. The Dow was up one percent, and the internals under that number cut against something I relayed yesterday. BowTied Bull's rotation call, corroborated by Yardeni Research, had money leaving megacap tech for industrials. Today XLI finished the morning lower while the index it belongs to rose one percent, and Caterpillar fell specifically on data-centre exposure while Deere rose. That is the AI trade leaking into an industrial name and dragging it, rather than a rotation into the sector. One session does not settle a call dated to December, so I am recording it as contradicted and leaving it open.

The memory trade has flipped east, and today it stopped being a narrative. Per the threadguy episode, ASML fell 2.5 percent on a headline that China has begun mass-producing homegrown deep-ultraviolet lithography tools for its domestic industry. Tulip King's version of the trade is four words: "long Eastern semis, short Western semis."

CXMT is the instrument. It priced its IPO at $85 billion and now trades near $500 billion. On its first day it took 1.14 percent of all Shanghai Stock Exchange volume, $234 million against an exchange total of $20.5 billion. Per Prof G Markets, that leaves it at roughly 1,600 times earnings, a payback year of 3626, and Ed Elson's summary is the one to keep: "memory investors are beginning to lose their grip on reality." It also has a five-year ByteDance supply agreement worth more than $7 billion and plans that may more than double monthly wafer output.

Two policy datapoints sit underneath, both from the threadguy digest and both single-sourced: the Trump administration is split on additional limits for CXMT and YMTC, and Apple has sought assurances that CXMT will not be added to the entity list. A customer lobbying pre-emptively against a supplier's designation tells you where that customer thinks the supply is.

The cost of the trade showed up in Seoul. Per the Market list, the KOSPI was halted after falling 10.84 percent to 6,023.66, the second-biggest single-day drop on record, and it is now down nearly 29 percent for the month. Per 0xResearch, an SK Hynix perpetual on Hyperliquid flash-crashed to $900 before the KOSPI opened at all, and the guest quoted on it drew the right lesson about thin tokenised wrappers on assets whose real market is shut.

Two things make this more than a China story. Micron broke below $800, and per Chip Stock Investor memory pricing has now appeared as a named cost line in Intuitive Surgical's results. That is the memory cycle arriving in surgical robotics. A DRAM buyer does not get much further from a hyperscaler than that. Meanwhile per AI Central, CoWoS packaging is sold out through year-end with lead times into 2027 and N3 is fully committed through 2026, and AMD signed a definitive agreement with Rackspace for phased deployment of 30 megawatts of Instinct MI355X and MI350P compute beginning late 2026. Supply is committed at the front of the queue and repricing violently at the back.

The problem with the NeoClouds finally has a date on it, and the date is 2032. Per Milk Road AI, the reason to care is not utilisation. It is that the GPUs, the customer contracts and the debt all mature at roughly the same moment, so the entire equity return sits in a residual value nobody has observed. Then the sensitivity. That is the whole argument. At a residual of 60 percent of initial rental rates the annualised return goes to 23 percent. At 30 percent the trade is over. He holds CoreWeave and Nebius, says he is getting more bearish, and has no model yet. I would rather have that than a model with a fabricated residual in it.

Put that against tonight's saved inbox item and you get a genuine conflict. Per The Chairman's Ledger, a disclosed long in IREN, Nebius and NUAI argues the buildout cannot be stopped because two of its three buyers cannot afford to wait for obvious returns and the third has barely started. His best move is the distinction that depreciation risk sits mostly with whoever owns the chips and far less with whoever owns the site, power and lease. His most honest line is the one he refuses to beat: "It's telco in 1999... The demand did show up, just ten yrs late, and the people who bought it out of bankruptcy made the money." What is missing is every number. No megawatts, no backlog, no capex, no price target, nothing comparing his three names at these prices.

Meanwhile per Rick Orford, IREN's buildout is described as phase-funding-phase, which cannot be reconciled with negative $6 million per megawatt in year one unless someone else is funding the phase.

Cowen's is the only one with a mechanism attached, and per Benjamin Cowen it runs backwards from the usual framing. A hold is what triggers the hike. The two-year already sits at 4.1 to 4.2 percent, above the funds rate, so a hold pushes the thirty-year through 5.2 percent and the ten-year toward 5, and the bond market forces the September move the Fed declined to make. He pairs it with a ten to twenty percent equity drawdown and three precedents, August 2022, September 2018 and September 2014, all midterm years.

My lean, revised in place rather than restacked. A hold is 78/22, up from 75/25 this morning, because Kalshi's drift to 77 and Bespoke's fall to 29 outweigh Citadel's position and CME's outlier. The statement reading hawkish enough to move the tape is 55/45 and that is where the risk actually is. If Cowen is right the sequencing means tomorrow's relief is the setup rather than the resolution, and the thing to watch is not the decision but the thirty-year the morning after.

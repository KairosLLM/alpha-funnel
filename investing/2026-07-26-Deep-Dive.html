The memory trade spent this week getting attacked from two directions nobody had priced, a Morgan Stanley demand note that tripped a circuit breaker in Seoul and a Chinese model architecture that quietly threatens the thing Micron actually sells

Last Sunday the crash needed no thesis to break, just a rate hike in Korea. This week the thesis itself got tested, and it survived on price while losing ground on argument. SK Hynix trades at four times 2027 earnings while Michael Burry presses a short at $934, and China's largest memory IPO ever lists tomorrow morning.

MacroStocks

Last Sunday I wrote that the memory round-trip was a test of leverage rather than a test of the story. That was right, and it is no longer the interesting question. This week the story itself came under attack, twice, and from directions the bulls had not modelled. Here is the arc, the bull case, the bear case, what to do about it, and then the week's scoreboard.

The arc of the week. Monday and Tuesday were the melt-up. Per the Market list, Micron hit $945 intraday Tuesday, kissed its 50-day and closed up about 10 percent after Bank of America added it to best ideas, with the memory fund up roughly 20 percent in days to near $58 and monster bounces in SanDisk, Western Digital and Seagate. Morgan Stanley called the pullback a compelling entry and modelled DRAM contract prices up at least 25 percent from the second quarter to the third. UBS said undersupplied to at least 2028.

Wednesday the floor thinned out underneath the price. Per Trader Mayne, Goldman had hedge funds as net sellers in six of eight weeks, the largest eight-week technology outflow in over a decade, with tech exposure at a 2026 low near 15.5 percent even as semiconductors reached about 14 percent of US market capitalisation, roughly double the dot-com peak. A trade can be crowded in index weight and abandoned in active positioning at the same time, and that is what memory was.

Thursday was Alphabet, and memory passed the test. Cloud grew 82 percent to $24.8 billion on a $514 billion backlog, capex went to $195 to $205 billion, free cash flow printed negative, and the stock fell 7.6 percent into seven target cuts. Memory decoupled and held, with relative strength across Micron, SanDisk, Seagate, Western Digital and SK Hynix. The market punished the spender and left the supplier alone. That is the single most important tape fact of the week.

Friday it broke. Per the Stocks list, Morgan Stanley's Shawn Kim published a demand warning arguing demand has evaporated, with NAND module inventories near 13 weeks, price increases slowing to 5 percent a quarter into the fourth quarter, high-bandwidth memory growth capped near 40 percent, and Tencent saying it had already secured 90 percent of its requirements. The Kospi fell 6 percent intraday to around 6,500 and tripped a circuit breaker. Per the People list, Intel beat on every line at $16.13 billion and $0.42 against $14.42 billion and $0.21, then round-tripped from $113.72 to under $100 for the crime of raising capex to $20 billion.

By the weekend the price had held and the argument had not. Per threadguy, Micron printed 961 and down 3 percent, holding its 955 fifty-day, with DRAM spot at $56 off the bottom. Per Shay Boloor, Michael Burry added to his Micron short at $934 and his Nvidia short at $210.

The bull case, stated cleanly. The demand is contracted, the supply is not coming, and the multiples assume the opposite. Per Boloor, the complex trades cheap because the market has already assumed peak earnings, so the debate is duration rather than level.

The contracts are the part that separates this from a normal cycle. Per the Crypto list, Micron has 16 strategic customer agreements, 14 carrying roughly $100 billion of minimum contracted revenue through 2030, against $22 billion of upfront deposits already banked. Customers are funding supplier capacity. That is not what the end of a cycle looks like.

Then this week's signatures, all inside five days. Per the People list, Nvidia and SK Group signed letters of intent on a $500 billion-plus initiative including a 2-gigawatt Vera Rubin factory online in 2027 running SK Hynix HBM4, plus a long-term partnership co-developing future memory generations, and Nvidia put $1 billion into NAVER alongside Brookfield to triple Korea's buildout from 55 megawatts to 200 by 2028. Per the Polymarket list, Broadcom and Samsung signed a five-year memorandum worth more than $200 billion through 2030. Per Boloor, Dario Amodei confirmed Anthropic has locked memory supply with SK Hynix and Samsung, who together hold about 85 percent of high-bandwidth memory. Nobody signs a decade of supply into evaporating demand.

The cleanest version of the bull case is not about memory at all, it is about where the money ends up. Per the Crypto list, Gavin Baker has Nvidia, Micron, Broadcom and Applied Materials on pace for roughly $430 billion of combined free cash flow next year, triple two years ago, while the five hyperscalers' free cash flow falls from about $250 billion in 2024 toward $100 billion by the end of 2026. The capex the market is punishing is arriving on the suppliers' income statements. Per BlackRock's Tony Kim, that is why he models a trillion dollars of capex this year and $10 trillion over five, calls it the primacy of memory, and points out that only three companies control it.

The bear case, taken seriously. Kim's note is the near-term threat and it is specific rather than vibes: 13 weeks of NAND inventory, price increases decelerating to 5 percent, a major customer already 90 percent covered. Against Morgan Stanley's own equity desk telling clients to buy memory the same morning, and against the same bank calling the dip a compelling entry three days earlier. That contradiction inside one firm went unreconciled all week and it should make you humble about the confidence on either side.

The more interesting attack is structural, and it arrived from a model release rather than a sell-side note. Per Bubble Boi on threadguy, Kimi K3 uses linear attention, which makes the key-value cache a fixed size instead of one that grows with context length. Less offloading means less commodity DRAM per user, existing racks serve far more users, and his question is the one that matters: how do you justify spending $500,000 on a new server rack.

He exempts high-bandwidth memory explicitly. He also walked it back on air, conceding only about 75 percent of the model is fixed, saying after more research that he does not think it is as clear-cut, and dating the impact roughly two years out. And he insists it is net bullish Nvidia, because linear attention keeps the GPU busy instead of idle waiting on memory.

Where that lands on Micron specifically is the part worth internalising. High-bandwidth memory is sliced and stacked out of commodity DRAM wafers, so HBM consumes DRAM as an input. HBM pricing has been steady to flat because all three makers have capacity, while commodity DRAM has gone vertical, and commodity DRAM is where Micron's money is. Per the same interview, Micron's HBM has always been the lowest quality of Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron, so it earns a higher margin than Nvidia on a far less differentiated product. He is not short it. He is long the substitute, flash broadly and Phison specifically, because they are getting rid of DRAM, and his trigger is stated plainly: when you start seeing flash take DRAM's job, sell DRAM.

The third threat lists in a few hours. CXMT comes to Shanghai's STAR Market tomorrow at 8.66 yuan, raising roughly $8.5 billion at about $85 billion, the largest Chinese semiconductor listing on record, on first-quarter revenue up 719 percent, already the fourth-largest DRAM maker by capacity.

Per Chip Stock Investor, no American hyperscaler will touch Chinese memory and they put the odds of the ban lifting as very low, but memory is one global supply pool, so the ramp reprices every buyer regardless of who is legally allowed to buy. Their threshold is the useful number: at single-digit share of world bits the barrier holds, and in the teens spot pricing gets set in Hefei rather than Seoul. The lobbying confirms the stakes, with Apple pressing Commerce to allow Chinese memory in non-US products and calling Micron's 80 percent margins gouging.

And the demand-side worry has a name now too. Per the Market list, Jim Cramer's reading is that the market is saying it has seen the last of the capital-expenditure increases, and after Google the buyers leave traditional tech because the suppliers will not get the orders. If he is right, the contracted backlog is the last good backlog.

What to do with it. Own the cycle, but the vehicle matters more than the direction, and this week made that concrete. SK Hynix at four times 2027 earnings holds 56 percent of high-bandwidth memory share to Micron's 23, makes about 70 percent of Vera Rubin's HBM on parts co-designed with Nvidia, and now has a $500 billion letter of intent behind it.

Micron at six times carries the commodity-DRAM exposure that both the Kimi mechanism and the Chinese ramp attack, plus the quality knock. If you want the trade with the least argument against it, it is the Korean one. Per the Stocks list, John Tinsman's caution is the practical catch: the SK Hynix American receipt trades at a 33 percent premium to the Korean shares, so the obvious vehicle is the wrong one.

The level is the referee and it is unusually clean. Micron held 955 at 961. Above it the next move is 972. Losing 921 opens 891 to 906. Josh's 996 breakout trigger from Friday never armed and is dead.

The calendar does the rest of the work, and this week is dense. CXMT lists tomorrow. Per the Financial Markets list, the Fed decides Wednesday with CME futures at a 38 percent chance of a quarter-point hike against 13 percent a week earlier, and Polymarket at 25 percent, with a cut at zero in both. Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon report from Wednesday. Per Joseph Hogue and Bridget of MarketBeat, hyperscaler earnings are the moment memory capex either shows up or does not, and her line is the honest frame for the whole position: this low probably only lasts until we see those hyperscaler reports.

My lean, with odds and a horizon, because a lean without both is just a mood. I am 65/35 that memory multiples expand before they compress, and that is a statement about the next quarter rather than the next three years. The duration argument wins the near term because CXMT and the Fed and four hyperscaler prints all land inside five days while linear attention is dated 2028.

Two things would flip me, and both are checkable. If fourth-quarter DRAM contract prices come in at Kim's 5 percent rather than the 20 to 30 percent the bulls model, the duration case is gone. If CXMT takes teens of world bits rather than single digits, pricing power moves to China and the multiple never re-rates. Hold the position with an exit trigger rather than a conviction, and the trigger is flash visibly taking DRAM's job, with Phison as the tell.

The scoreboard. Fourteen calls on the book resolved this week. Nine hit, four missed, one expired.

The wins, with the receipts. Michael Burry's Micron short, logged on the 12th betting on roughly a 30 percent drop, delivered about 36 percent as Micron fell from $1,255 to $804. Stocks with Josh's 861 reclaim held the 804 area, recaptured 861 on the 20th and ran to 945 by the 21st. Heisenberg's Nvidia target of 208.65 to 210 cleared 208 as Nvidia pushed back over $5 trillion. threadguy's long Brent from around 82 went above 100 by Thursday. Mentovia's 955 reclaim on Friday printed 961 and held.

The misses, said as plainly. Milk Road Macro rotated into Tesla ahead of the July 22 print and Tesla earned $0.33 against roughly $0.50 expected on a 16.8 percent gross margin and fell about 14 percent. Mike McGlone's Brent resistance near 83 with a base case rotating toward 40 was wrong in the most expensive direction, because Brent blew through 83 and traded above 110 by Friday. The Stocks list's Meta setup with a close stop at 615 broke that stop and traded near 600. Sykodelic needed Bitcoin to hold $66,000 on the weekly and it was near $64,100 by the weekend. One expired: Josh's Micron 996 trigger never armed, sitting about 4 percent above where Micron actually trades, so no position ever existed.

The house record needs a caveat more than it needs a victory lap. Four house calls have settled and all four hit, so the raw number is 4 for 4. Every one of them was a dated-catalyst call, that CPI would decide the tape on the 14th, that Tesla reports on the 22nd, that Alphabet is the referee on the 22nd, that Intel reports on the 23rd. Calling a scheduled event is the easy kind. The house has not yet settled a single directional call, so read 100 percent as unproven rather than good, and the 65/35 memory lean above is the first one that will actually cost something if it is wrong.

Nineteen open calls carry into next week, and they are front-loaded. CXMT and the Kimi weights land tomorrow, Bloom Energy reports Tuesday alongside the Trump and Netanyahu meeting, the Fed and Robinhood land Wednesday, Galaxy Digital's $38 fair-value call runs out Friday, and the prediction market on an Iran strike expires the same day. AppLovin follows on August 5, the SpaceX unlock on August 6, and the CLARITY floor vote by August 7. Micron's 12-times-trailing dip-buy thesis from Hogue is dated August 7 too, which means the entire memory argument gets marked to market inside two weeks.

3. The Kimi shock is the first specific mechanism anyone has offered for how the memory trade actually breaks. Every bear case until now has been about demand. This one is about architecture. Per Bubble Boi on threadguy, Kimi uses linear attention, which makes the key-value cache (the working memory a model keeps per user) a fixed size instead of one that grows with context length, and unlike earlier attempts it delivers frontier-quality results.

The consequence is narrow. Less offloading means less commodity DRAM per user and existing racks serve far more users, so "how can you justify spending 500k for a new server rack?" He exempts high-bandwidth memory, walks it back partway himself (only about 75 percent of the model is fixed), and puts the impact two years out. He also insists Kimi is net bullish Nvidia, because it keeps the GPU busy during inference instead of idle.

His Micron case follows from that rather than from the chart. Micron out-earns Nvidia on margin with a less differentiated product, and per the same desk it is the most overrated name in memory, with "always the lowest quality" high-bandwidth memory of the big three and profits riding commodity DRAM pricing. He is not short it, he is long the substitute: flash broadly and Phison specifically, "because they're getting rid of DRAM," with the trigger stated plainly as "when you start seeing flash take DRAM's job, sell DRAM."

A second mechanism arrives independently. Per Chip Stock Investor, no US hyperscaler will touch Chinese memory, but memory is one global supply pool, so CXMT and YMTC ramping moves world prices regardless. Two unrelated arguments, same conclusion, and a Chinese memory IPO on Monday.

The maximal other side, per Milk Road AI, is Kyle seeing no capex slowdown until 2030, with analysts having called the tip-over "6 to 9 months away" every quarter for two years. My lean is a two-year view rather than a two-week one: duration wins into next week, architecture wins by 2028. Call it 65/35 that memory multiples expand before they compress, because CXMT and the Fed are catalysts this week and linear attention is not.

4. Alphabet's negative free cash flow is the entire capex-punishment story in one line. The numbers are better than the reaction. Per Kyle Reidhead, Alphabet's first-ever negative quarterly free cash flow came in at minus $5.9 billion on capex of $44.9 billion, up 100 percent year over year, with 2026 capex guided to $195 to $205 billion. Revenue grew 24 percent to $119.8 billion, cloud 82 percent to $24.8 billion at a 35.6 percent operating margin, backlog rose $50 billion in one quarter to $514 billion, and Gemini reached 950 million monthly users. The stock fell more than 6 percent.

Jim Cramer named the regime change in one sentence. Per the Market list, "today the market is saying we have seen the last of the cap ex increases," and after Google the buyers leave traditional tech. Per WhiteCollarExit, the trailing multiple compressed from about 21 times to under 16 in a year while cloud operating income tripled to $8.8 billion.

Somebody is acting on it. Per Milk Road PRO, the desk bought Alphabet at $319.34 as the cheapest in years, plus Intel at $93.70, ServiceNow at $97.09 on cRPO growing 21.5 percent against a 19.5 percent guide, and Nokia at $10.40.

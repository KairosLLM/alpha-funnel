Hedge funds bought tech at the fastest pace since 2022, one week after the flush

Amazon crossed $3 trillion for the first time and SpaceX hit an all-time low on the same tape, while Bank of America's Bull and Bear indicator printed one of the strongest sell signals of the century.

MacroStocksBitcoinCrypto

Yesterday the question was whether a closed strait and a VIX at 15.99 could stay on the same page. Today the market answered by buying tech with both hands, and the flows tell you the cleansing everyone celebrated lasted about five sessions.

Market context. The morning started at the line that mattered. Per the Financial Markets list, $QQQ backtested the June lows at 686 near the open and bounced from there, with EliteOptionsTrader's August 5 692 calls going from roughly $4.75 to $8.50 inside ninety minutes.

Then the split tape. Per the Kalshi list, Amazon crossed $3 trillion of market value for the first time while SpaceX printed an all-time low, one day before its first earnings report in company history. Tesla ran 4 percent. Stocks climbed as oil fell, with Trump telling oil companies to get prices down now and separately claiming the United States "completely controls" the Strait of Hormuz. Iran showed no interest in resuming negotiations.

1. The memory trade flipped inside twenty-four hours, and the hyperscalers took the other side. Yesterday UBS had memory spending doubling to $761 billion by 2027 and Micron re-rating from 5 times earnings toward 12. Today the trade broke the other way. Per the Stocks list, Reuters reports CXMT is considering a second Beijing DRAM fab in the Yizhuang district on top of the Shanghai and Hefei plants already under construction, taking its capacity to more than double current levels and past 600,000 wafers a month.

Memory sold off. The hyperscalers rallied. Kyle Reidhead argues that is not a coincidence and the arithmetic is his best work in weeks. Memory is now roughly 35 percent of hyperscaler hardware spend against 8 percent in 2023, and Apple spent its earnings call complaining that memory costs are squeezing margins. AWS already says its server investments break even in under three years at today's inflated component prices.

So run it forward. If memory and chip supply loosen, every data centre gets cheaper, every server pays back faster, and the $860 billion of capex going out this year buys more compute per dollar. Falling input costs speed the buildout up rather than slowing it down.

He supplies his own caveat and it is the right one. The new Chinese supply is commodity DRAM, not the high-bandwidth memory that is sold out through 2026, so the selloff is probably overblown. I am revising to 55/45 that memory multiples expand before they compress, down from 65/35, because a second fab announcement inside a week is a supply signal and UBS's re-rating case was always a bet on scarcity being structural.

2. The re-grossing already happened, and that is the problem. Six days ago Goldman's crowded-position index had its worst month against the S&P since 2001 and Jordi Visser called it a cleansing event. Cleansing events are supposed to leave positioning light for a while.

Instead, per the Kalshi list, hedge funds have bought tech at the fastest pace since 2022 and tech funds just took their largest five-week inflow on record. The money that was force-sold in July came back in under a week, and it came back into the same names.

Shay Boloor has the bull reading on the People to follow list, and it is the clearest statement of what the flows imply. He expects the Lag 7 to turn back into the Mag 7, and says "all of the bottleneck bros are going to start wondering, why aren't we catching a bid anymore. It's because the generals are back in action." Ryan Detrick adds on the Financial Markets list that six sectors gained more than 1 percent in July and a declining savings rate is what an expansion looks like when net wealth rises.

Against them sits Bank of America's Bull and Bear indicator at one of the strongest sell signals of the century, and Bloomberg's James Seyffart on the People to follow list: "The amount of gambling coming to the ETF market is crazy." REX Shares is launching 2X daily long ETFs on Akamai, DigitalOcean and Penguin Solutions. That is what the top of a speculation cycle sounds like when it files paperwork.

My lean, and it is a downgrade. I go to 60/40 that the July low holds into mid-August, back from 70/30 yesterday. Visser's argument for a bottom rested on hedge funds hedging rather than liquidating and coming back slowly. They came back immediately, at size, into a sell signal. That is a weaker setup than the one I upgraded on.

3. Warsh and Bessent are running the same trade from two desks. Per Capital Flows, the most useful framing of the week. Warsh spent his July press conference on capital spending rather than inflation. His words: "The most striking feature of the economy is the strong growth of business investment." He put a number on it, saying AI-related high-tech equipment and software shows four-quarter growth rates of nearly 20 percent, and told Congress two weeks earlier that "it seems inevitable that what is now called AI investment will soon be called just investment."

Then his description of his own posture. "We haven't done much in 42 days. The markets have done quite a bit."

Bessent's half runs through Japan. Cross-border yen borrowing is roughly a quarter trillion dollars directly and near a trillion once derivatives are counted, and that is the funding pipe for dollar assets. So the yen hitting a 1986 low, Japan's record single-day defence, and the Treasury buying alongside it are not three stories. They are one, and the thing being protected is the liquidity the buildout runs on.

Why it is urgent now: Anthropic went from a $9 billion revenue run rate to nearly $50 billion in about five months and has filed confidentially to go public, while OpenAI runs near $25 billion and still loses more than a dollar for every dollar it earns. Those companies need public markets at the exact moment SpaceX trades 20 percent below its offer price. Warsh also put Marc Andreessen on the Fed's new AI task force.

Treat the coordination claim as a frame rather than a finding, because the author supports the policy he is describing and sells access to the thesis. The quotes and the intervention sequence are checkable and they are the durable part.

4. Goldman found the earnings asterisk, and then found the half that cuts the other way. Per zerohedge on the Financial Markets list, S&P 500 earnings growth is tracking 45 percent year over year in the second quarter against a 22 percent consensus coming in. Nineteen points of that is Alphabet and Amazon's combined $151 billion of "other income" on equity investments.

Here is the part the bears will skip. Strip those gains and growth is still tracking 26 percent, an acceleration against the first quarter and the fastest pace since 2021.

That number damages both sides of the argument I have been running. Ed Yardeni's asterisk and Jim Chanos's profit-inflation mechanism are both real and now quantified at nineteen points. And the underlying business is accelerating anyway. Anyone telling you this quarter is fake has to explain 26 percent.

5. The Coldcard hack is far bigger than one unlucky holder. Yesterday I carried one man losing 18.25 Bitcoin. Per Anthony Pompliano, the real number is more than $100 million from over 5,000 wallets, and Bloomberg's headline calls the attack ongoing.

The mechanism matters for how you price it. Bitcoin itself was not touched. The flaw sits in Coinkite's random number generator, so it is a vendor failure rather than a protocol failure. Pompliano's forecast is that this pushes money toward ETFs and third-party multi-sig, and he runs a bitcoin treasury company, so weigh the forecast accordingly.

Scott Melker has the sentence that lands. Over a million retail traders were liquidated last October, and this week the losses hit people who did everything right and held in self custody. The believers are the ones getting destroyed.

6. The two loudest bitcoin holders in the market went opposite ways today. Michael Saylor's Strategy sold $102 million of Bitcoin, buying STRC with it. Jim Cramer said he is selling all of his on quantum risk, and told viewers to buy the Amazon dip in the same session. Tom Lee's BitMine bought $19 million of Ethereum, with Lee personally adding $20 million last week, and BlackRock launched a tokenized money-market fund on Ethereum.

The price backdrop is worse than the flow. Per Milk Road on the Stocks list, Bitcoin trades near $63,900, about 49 percent below its October 2025 high of $126,198, and Polymarket had 2026 CLARITY passage at 28 percent on July 30 against 82 percent in February. Kalshi's traders now forecast Ethereum crashes to $1,390 this year.

Saylor selling and Cramer capitulating on the same day the legislative catalyst dies is the cleanest sentiment washout crypto has produced this cycle. That is a reason to look rather than a reason to buy, and I am 55/45 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before it sees $75,000.

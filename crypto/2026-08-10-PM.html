The $66,000 close failed and the man who drew the line is short

Two whales moved about 14,000 coins over three weeks into the best ETF inflow week since April, and the tape still could not hold the level.

BitcoinEtherEtf-FlowsTreasuries

Yesterday I wrote that Kalshi had flipped the distribution and that my downside call finally had the betting market on its side. The weekend supplied the confirmation and it was not the comfortable kind.

Market context. Per the Crypto list, Bitcoin sits at $64,000 to $65,000 after a weak weekly close. Demand and supply both showed up and supply won.

That is roughly 14,000 coins distributed into the best demand week in four months, and the close still failed. Per 0xResearch, miners were the week's worst index at minus 12.93 percent while gold ran 6.89 and Bitcoin managed 2.46.

The scoreboard.

  • Resolved, and it went to the branch I did not want. Yesterday's call, from Sykodelic, was that a weekly close above $66,000 sends this higher and a rejection puts $60,000 first. The close was weak, so the rejection branch fired exactly as written. Marked a hit for the framework rather than for the direction I would have preferred.
  • Still open and now better supported. My 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000. Section two is why I am not raising it despite the weekend.

1. The man who drew the line took the trade. Per the Crypto list, Sykodelic posted overnight: "My short is filled. After waiting weeks for a clear trigger, we now have one. The $65,300 level was taken out and we got a weak weekly close."

His book is specific: trigger $65,300, already taken, target $60,500 to clear the equal lows, and invalidation at "a strong daily close above $67,000."

What matters for my own position is where he stops. My call needs $58,000. His needs $60,500. Not the same trade, and the gap is the distance between being right about direction and right about size.

Per Milk Road, the ceiling on the other end is a failed run at $72,000, capped by the delayed CLARITY Act vote, with Polymarket pricing the bill passing this year at about 27 percent.

2. A permabull's clock and a bear's reversal met in the same month. Per Benjamin Cowen, the range between the 200-week EMA and the bear-market resistance band breaks down more often than up near the end of midterm years, and "I would lean towards breakdown." His dated version: the cycle low is most likely October, possibly late September, could slip to December, and by late November it is in or close to in. Social interest reads 0.2 against roughly 0.5 four years ago, matching August 2018, his analogue year, and the MVRV Z-score has not gone below zero. Bitcoin normally bottoms only when it does. He says he has bought some anyway.

Coming the other way, per 0xResearch, Clemente cut his remaining Bitcoin in January and spent the year in commodities. He now argues deep value: price consolidating at the 2021 highs below the 200-week average, MVRV near prior bear-market lows, and long-term holders accumulating against $5 billion of ETF outflows. He is building spot again, still open to one final leg down this year, and hedging with cheap options.

That convergence is why I am holding 60/40 rather than raising it. A bear who left in January is buying, a bull is leaning breakdown, and both put the resolution in the autumn rather than this week. Clemente's structure is the honest expression of my own view: own the downside through options rather than spot, because the timing is the part nobody has.

3. Ether is losing the price argument and winning the flow argument. Per Lark Davis, the flip is in supply and flows and he says plainly that price has not caught up.

The last line has history attached. The previous time that weekly signal fired, Ether outperformed Bitcoin by 132 percent over the following months.

I am holding the 60/40 one-more-leg-down on Ether with less conviction than a week ago. The price case is intact and the flow case is not what it was. What would move me is the staking ratio holding above 34 percent through a red Bitcoin month, because a supply lock that survives a drawdown is a different fact from one only tested in a bounce. Blockworks Research also puts low odds on EIP-8363 being adopted, so the mechanism that would cut the staking yield is fading rather than arriving.

4. Strategy said the words out loud and told you where its own gate is. Per the People to follow list, chief executive Phong Le on CNBC: "Bitcoin is going through a bear cycle right now." He is not a forced seller, and he made the point with the balance sheet: 842,000 Bitcoin, over 4 percent of all that will ever be mined, past BlackRock last quarter, and over $4 billion of cash.

Per the Crypto list, aixbt supplies the mechanical version and it is the most checkable trigger in today's material. That $4 billion covers more than two years of dividends and debt interest. That is what backs STRC at $95. The 12 percent rate supports par, issuance stays shut below $100, and if STRC holds $99 to $100 Strategy reopens $17.5 billion of capacity. A four to five percent move in one preferred security decides whether the largest holder returns as a buyer, and Bitcoin only gets the bid if the proceeds actually buy Bitcoin.

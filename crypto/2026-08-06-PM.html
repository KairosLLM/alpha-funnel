$100 million walked out of Coldcard wallets and Bitcoin moved less than one percent

A random-number bug from 2021 left older seeds guessable, more than 5,000 holders lost coins, and the only thing in crypto that reacted was Robinhood's memecoin chain.

BitcoinEtf-FlowsTreasuriesEquities

Yesterday Galaxy proved the miners had stopped being a Bitcoin trade. Today a hundred million dollars walked out of self-custody wallets and the price barely registered it. Nothing moves this asset anymore, and that is the read.

Market context. Per Milk Road, Bitcoin topped at $126,198 last October and sits roughly 49 percent below it this afternoon. Per VirtualBacon, it is up 3.6 percent over four days and still in a daily downtrend that began in May, with resistance at 66.7K and a break opening 76K. It sits on its 200-week average, the level that marked every prior cycle floor.

Benjamin Cowen's framing on NFA Live is the one to carry: Bitcoin sits at the same valuation against the S&P 500 as five to six years ago, while the S&P makes new highs. Per Blockworks, ETF net flows are anemic in both directions and Glassnode has options priced for no move. Miners were the worst asset on the board at minus 6.2 percent, with Cipher and Hut 8 both missing.

The scoreboard.

  • Hit, four days early. On July 11 I said Robinhood Chain had to hold $400 million a day of DEX volume by August 10 or the migration was churn. Seven-day volume is near $3 billion, roughly $428 million a day. It cleared.
  • Working. My 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000. Three independent clocks now point at a low between October and December, and none point up first.
  • Called it, and it cost 13 percent. I said yesterday Galaxy's 830 approved megawatts needed a tenant within days. Novogratz had promised one by the end of summer and did not deliver.
  • Due tomorrow. ERCOT's next Batch Zero round, which the Texas audit suspended.

1. The Coldcard bug was born in 2021, and the coins left this week. Per Swan, a 2021 software integration made Coldcard seed generation fall through to a MicroPython pseudo-random number generator instead of the hardware entropy source it was meant to use. Coinkite puts affected MK2 and MK3 seeds at roughly 40 bits of effective search space, with MK4, MK5 and Q near 72 bits. The design target was 128.

Forty bits is not a lock somebody picked. It is a lock with a thousand keys.

Per the People to follow list, one researcher mapped 4,312 drained addresses with the exact block for each. Per Anthony Pompliano's guests, the total is more than 5,000 people and over $100 million, concentrated among the holders least likely to ever use a custodian. Bitcoin did not move more than about one percent on it.

The position is not a price call. Tilman Holloway expects ETF inflows to tick up three, six and nine months out as burned self-custody holders move to a custodian, against Coinbase and BitGo carrying roughly $5 million of insurance. The remedy circulating on Spaces is generating your own entropy with 100 dice rolls. That tells you how this ends.

The most damning line came from calle on the Crypto list: "not a single vulnerability was found by a US frontier model. Instead, we're spending $10k a day on open weights models like Kimi K3 and Qwen 3.8 to find vulnerabilities in Bitcoin infrastructure."

2. CLARITY's death is a buy-the-news event, and I am taking the unpopular side. Macro has the vote math tonight: 60 needed, 53 held, cloture filed on a different motion. Here it is a straight disagreement between desks. Per VirtualBacon, Bernstein says failure could trigger the final leg down in crypto valuations and JPMorgan calls it another headwind. Bitwise's Matt Hougan says the SEC and CFTC can supply the same path by rule rather than law. Altcoin Daily puts a failure at Bitcoin "maybe into the 50s, maybe into 48."

James Seyffart's point decides it. Traditional finance treats CLARITY as a catalyst for Bitcoin, not only for alts, so the disappointment is broad rather than confined to tokens that need a legal wrapper.

I am 65/35 that it is already priced. Polymarket went from 50 to 70 percent in January to 15 percent now, and an asset that ignored a $100 million theft is not holding a legislative premium. Hougan is the reason I lean that way. A rulemaking path does not need 60 votes.

3. Galaxy's missing tenant cost it 13 percent, and the frozen queue is why waiting is correct. Per Milk Road, $GLXY fell 13 percent to $19 on a $7.5 billion market cap, and none of it was the numbers. Helios phase one, 200 megawatts leased to CoreWeave for 15 years, is billing toward roughly $80 million a quarter.

Per m0xt on the Stocks list, the pipeline is 5.7 gigawatts with 4.3 contracted, contractable or Batch Zero eligible, and the Texas audit ordered August 3 suspended the next Batch Zero round, due tomorrow. Merlin's first phase is 74 megawatts, one under the 75 megawatt threshold that would have caught it. Caspian at 700 and Selene at 900 are grandfathered.

So the moat held and the announcement did not. I am 60/40 the tenant lands before the audit clears, because a landlord with grandfathered megawatts inside a frozen queue has the only product anyone can plug in.

4. The only live bid in crypto is on Robinhood's chain, and none of it is Bitcoin. Per 0xSammy, Robinhood Chain carries $411 million of DeFi value, $558 million of stablecoins, over $1 billion bridged and nearly $3 billion of seven-day DEX volume, with perpetual futures up 130 percent.

The economics are the interesting part. Apps on the chain took $1.6 million of gross fees in 24 hours and the chain kept $50 thousand. Fat apps, thin chain, no token to dilute. Pons alone runs 64 percent of launchpad volume on a $20 million market cap, and Uniswap has now deployed pools.trade on the same chain to take it.

  • $COIN. The legislative catalyst is priced at 20 percent for the year and industry volumes fell double digits last quarter. Jack Mallers frames the split honestly: Coinbase cares more about its own chain and alts than about Bitcoin. That is the bull case now, because the chains have the volume.

The FIMA trigger has a number now, and the trade just broke Bitcoin's way

Arthur Hayes says the $60 billion FIMA repo cap is the next Bitcoin liquidity trigger, while Macro's hammering of the rate-hike trade removed the single biggest headwind this asset has been fighting.

BitcoinEtf-FlowsEquitiesAlts

Yesterday four models were converging on two different floors and nobody was sure which one would bind first. Today the arguments about direction got quieter because the picture around them shifted.

Market context. Per the Crypto PM list, Bitcoin spot held roughly $64,000 with Market Outlook number 89 carrying a read of $65,310 against ETH at $1,926, the Nasdaq 100 at $29,838, gold at $4,355 and dollar-yen at 158.49. The key levels are two-sided. Support sits at $62,500 and the February low at $60,000, while the anchored VWAP is at $67,600 and the final boss remains $80,000. Bollinger band width is the narrowest it has been since October 2023.

Per Lookonchain, US Bitcoin ETFs shed 1,132 coins on the day, about $72 million, for a seven-day net of minus 202 coins. Ethereum ETFs added 3,947 ETH, about $7.5 million, for a seven-day net of plus 65,941 ETH and about $125 million. The flows have diverged for five straight sessions at the narrowest band in nearly three years, and that is a setup, not an accident.

1. Arthur Hayes just named the FIMA cap as Bitcoin's next trigger, and he gave it a number. Per Scott Melker and the Crypto PM list, Hayes says the $60 billion Fed FIMA repo facility cap is what unlocks the next Bitcoin leg and that a rate hike is not needed for the move, merely an expansion of the facility that lets foreign central banks borrow dollars against Treasuries rather than selling them. The mechanism is the same one Bessent proposed in July for the BOJ's intervention: Japan, the largest foreign holder of Treasuries at about $1.2 trillion, gets to borrow against them instead of selling, which removes the forced seller from the long end and adds dollar liquidity through the back door.

The call lands directly on the Macro edition today. The September hike odds collapsed from 42.1 to 29 percent, the December hike moved from 73.8 to 62.3, and the market is no longer pricing any hike in 2026 above 50 percent. The single biggest headwind Bitcoin has been fighting was a four-hike year. That trade just broke.

Per Cav, open interest relative to market cap is elevated across both perpetuals and Deribit options, and a break above the range will not be a slow stair-step rally. A short cascade is priced into the open interest structure, not just the tweet sentiment.

2. Figure reported and the lending business is growing faster than the price. Per the Crypto PM list, Figure did diluted earnings of 35 cents a share against roughly 25 cents expected. Capital Lending Markets volume hit $4.26 billion, up 132 percent year over year. The net take rate compressed to 3.6 percent from 4.0, adjusted EBITDA came in at $119.4 million at a 54.6 percent margin against 47.2 percent, net income rose to $87 million, up 192 percent, and the lender count reached 489. The company guided third-quarter volume to $4.8 to $5.2 billion and the Kiavi acquisition is pending at $717 million.

Per the house calls ledger, Figure was a catalyst call due today after a run of quarters. The beat clears the bar and the guide says the next quarter is bigger, which makes it a hit on the numbers and leaves the open question of whether the volume growth is priced into a stock that is up substantially on the year.

3. Standard Chartered put a $200 price target on Chainlink, and the reaction was a lot quieter than the number. Per Arbitrage Andy, Standard Chartered initiated coverage on $LINK with a $200 target for 2030, about a 2,400 percent return from the current price near $8, and the argument is that Chainlink becomes the critical data infrastructure for the entire tokenized real-world-asset economy. The report landed the same week that Link quietly crossed a series of integration milestones that the desk has been tracking.

The price is the problem more than the thesis. A coin at $8 with a $200 target is a 24-bagger and that is the kind of number that invites a fade even when the bank putting it up is a real one. VirtualBacon noted today that XRP still has ten times potential, that altcoins outside the top five carry the same return characteristics as 2021 and 2023, and that total altcoin market cap growth follows the Bitcoin market cap expansion whatever the multiple. A coin with a named institutional target at a five-handle price is the shape of the thing his framework is describing.

4. Robinhood Chain is pumping memecoins while the crypto segment that funds it is shrinking. Per 0xResearch and Degen Sensei, Robinhood Chain flipped Base in DEX volume in July, memecoins are 51 percent of spot volume against 5 percent for the real-world assets it was built for, and PumpFun revenue is surging as the chain's fee engine. The other side is the Robinhood corporate numbers. Monthly metrics released after the close showed crypto volumes down substantially, which was expected after a July that was tough across every trading metric, and the crypto segment revenue fell 38 percent year over year to $100 million in Q2.

Per Degen Sensei, Solana reclaimed DEX volume leadership from BNB Chain, on-chain has been strong, and the majors have been printing some of the lowest volatility days in history, which normally means a big move is coming. The sentiment bias is toward a dip before the rip, and the cash the desk has been telling you to keep ready is the same cash he is telling his readers to keep ready.

A fourth voice, Milk Road, noted Solana usage is exploding while price lags. That is the same tension driving the "adoption versus price" deep-dive the Sunday memo carried two weeks ago. The signal has not changed direction.

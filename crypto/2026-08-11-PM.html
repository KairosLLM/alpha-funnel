Three independent models now put the flip at $67,000, and Saylor sold at $63,957

Bitcoin is sitting exactly on a 200-week average at $63,800 that Strategy publishes on its own website as book value, and it sold 1,638 coins right on the line.

Yesterday the weekly close failed and the man who drew the level went short. Today two more desks arrived at the same number from completely different directions, and the largest holder in the world sold into it.

Market context. Per the Crypto list, Bitcoin traded near $63,700, down 1.68 percent, with aixbt attributing it to Brent above $90 keeping the Fed on guard. The flows are two-sided again.

Per 0xResearch, layer twos gained 7.7 percent on the week while Bitcoin fell. Ether sits near $1,926.

1. Three desks, three methods, one number. Per On-Chain Mind, his adaptive trend model has been bearish for 87 days, since it flipped at $78,000 in mid-May, and it turns back at $67,000. The level is volatility-adaptive, so it compresses as daily ranges tighten.

Per VirtualBacon, the instruction is the same and he gets there from risk-reward rather than from a model. Do not buy here, because a relief rally targets 75,000 to 77,000 while the only true invalidation is a new cycle low below 57,800, so the trade is 16 percent up against 10 percent down. What he wants instead is a daily close above $67,000, then a long near 68 with a stop at 64.

That is now three frameworks landing on the same figure, because Sykodelic's short from $65,300 carries its invalidation at a strong daily close above $67,000 too. When a trend model, a risk-reward frame and a short's stop all converge on one number, the number stops being an opinion and becomes the level everyone is trading against.

The rest of his board is more balanced than the headline. Price sits exactly on the 200-week moving average at $63,800, which has compounded 30 percent a year for four straight years, and on a kernel baseline of $64,300, so nothing is stretched either way. A higher low has printed, the first necessary condition for a reversal rather than confirmation of one. His weekly velocity RSI reads just over 14 percent, inside the sub-15 band that has marked every generational bottom.

I am holding 60/40 that $58,000 comes before $75,000, and the honest position is that it is getting harder to defend. Per Lark Davis, the long-term to short-term holder realized cap ratio is 3.9 against the threshold of 4 that has marked every prior cycle bottom, and Bitcoin has now absorbed a Korean market down 45 percent, AI stocks halved, 360,000 Korean retail liquidated inside 27 days and a Coldcard exploit costing over $100 million, all without breaking $60,000. A bear case whose perfect catalysts arrive and produce nothing is a bear case on a clock.

2. Saylor published the floor on his own website and then sold on it. Per VirtualBacon, strategy.com now displays the Bitcoin 200-week moving average and the premium to it, labelled book value, currently about 0.5 percent above with the average at $63,500. Strategy then sold 1,638 Bitcoin at an average of $63,957, that same line to within half a percent.

The definitional problem is the interesting part. By Saylor's own stated meaning of book value, the last price at which the average coin changed hands, the right metric is realized price at $52,900 to $53,800, not $63,000. Publishing the 200-week instead gives a fair-value story now and a different one if price goes under it.

Per threadguy, the obligation underneath explains the urgency. Strategy owes about $1.7 billion a year in perpetuity to STRC holders against a USD reserve of roughly $4.65 billion, so a little over two years of coverage, with STRC at 95. That is the same gate I flagged yesterday at $99 to $100, seen from the liability side. Watch what the largest holder does rather than what its website says.

3. The miner trade quietly became a landlord trade. Per Milk Road, VanEck's Matthew Sigel gives the mechanism: an AI token is worth roughly ten times a Bitcoin token per unit of electricity, and GPUs are debt-financeable where Bitcoin ASICs never were. So a miner moves from pure equity funding with revenue that halves every four years to debt plus equity at a lower cost of capital.

The anchor deal, per 0xResearch, is Riot providing Anthropic 191 megawatts under a 20-year agreement expected to generate about $9.1 billion. The stock was up as much as 29 percent in extended hours and faded to about 7.

Sigel's valuation frame is three parts: the discounted value of signed leases with investment-grade counterparties, the terminal value of a 20-year asset, and the uncontracted pipeline. The selloff has taken most of these names to paying nothing for terminal value and little for pipeline, so signed contracts alone cover the price. The offsets are dated and real. New York is delaying new data centre construction by about a year, a Texas audit may push energization back a couple of months, and the sector is down 15 percent on the week while miners sell coins rather than buy machines.

4. There is a mania running and Bitcoin is not in it. Per 0xResearch, the trading app Fomo did a record $3.2 million of revenue last week, an eighth straight weekly gain and 70 percent above the record set a week earlier, and ranked tenth in the US App Store finance category. It has no token.

The listed proxy is Pump. It did $10.7 million of revenue last week, annualising near $556 million, and sends half of it programmatically to buy and burn, which implies about $278 million of annual buybacks against a $1.08 billion circulating market cap, a 26 percent buyback yield.

Per threadguy, who is long it, the framing is blunt: "the first bull run in the history of crypto where Bitcoin flat out does not participate." His evidence is a set of ratios that already bottomed against Bitcoin at different times, and Pump Fun bought back $847,000 of its own token yesterday, the largest single day since January.

Treat it as what it is. A 26 percent buyback yield funded by speculative volume is a real cash flow and a fragile one, because the revenue and the token price come from the same crowd.

CLARITY died and Bitcoin reclaimed $65,000 the same afternoon

The Senate punted the vote to September, Kalshi's odds of it becoming law this year hit an all-time low of 16 percent, and BlackRock bought 9,269 coins over four straight days anyway.

BitcoinEtherEtf-FlowsEquities

Yesterday a hundred million dollars walked out of self-custody wallets and Bitcoin did not move. Today the bill this sector spent a year lobbying for died, and Bitcoin went up. Two days, one answer.

Market context. Per the Kalshi list, Bitcoin reclaimed $65,000 on the day the CLARITY vote slipped, roughly half its October high. Per the Crypto list, the bid behind it is institutional and it is not subtle: BlackRock logged four consecutive days of net inflows totalling 9,269 Bitcoin, about $604 million, with Morgan Stanley buying three days running and Bhutan selling $27 million into it.

The rest of the tape is flat to soft. Per Blockworks, ETF flows overall are still anemic, and the 60-day correlation between Bitcoin and Ether ETF flows has climbed to plus 0.67, so the two move as one allocation now rather than two decisions. Miners are distributing, MARA and Riot sending a combined 581 coins to NYDIG, and the proxies split on earnings rather than beta, Galaxy up 3.6 percent and Circle up 3.1 percent against an index down 1.1.

The scoreboard.

  • Working, and the delay is the proof. On Thursday I went 65/35 that CLARITY's failure was already priced. The Senate punted it, Kalshi's odds of passage this year hit 16 percent, an all-time low, and Bitcoin closed higher on the news.
  • Strengthened. My 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000. Benjamin Cowen's cycle arithmetic now puts us three quarters through the bear with the low still ahead.
  • Due today, and it did not clear. ERCOT's Batch Zero study round stayed suspended under the Texas audit, so Galaxy's approved megawatts keep their scarcity value and its uncontracted pipeline keeps waiting.
  • Standing. The 60/40 that Galaxy lands a Helios tenant before the audit clears. Still no announcement.

1. The catalyst everyone was hedging died, and the hedge paid nothing. Macro has the vote math tonight: cloture never filed, Senate back September 14. What matters here is the price reaction, and there was not one. Traders had spent the week positioning for the opposite. Per Trader Mayne, CryptoFace was building a straddle into the vote on the view that a failure delivers "that one leg down." It did not arrive.

The reason is that the market had already marked the bill down to nothing. Michael Saylor's line, carried on the Kalshi list, is the framing that fits: "Bitcoin doesn't need CLARITY. America needs clarity." BitQua put the trader's version of it better: "The most bullish outcome for bitcoin is not necessarily a particular answer, it's getting an answer. Just, yes or no." A punt is the one outcome that resolves nothing, and Bitcoin rose anyway.

I am holding 65/35 and adding a leg: the unpriced risk sits in the alt market rather than in Bitcoin, because Coinbase has already said it keeps expanding offshore if the bill fails. Haseeb Qureshi, on the Crypto list, is the counterweight, noting Kalshi still prices better than 30 percent for next year and above 50 percent by 2028. Late is not dead.

His verdict on whether the low is in: "Doubtful that it started yet, we could still go a little bit lower." He has been buying below 60,000 and says he got wrecked six months after doing the same in 2018 and 2022, and did not care six months after that.

Trader Mayne lands in the same place from a different angle, putting the bottoming range at 40,000 to 60,000 with a weekly market structure break as the trigger rather than a price. Two independent frameworks, no shared method, same fourth-quarter answer. This is the strongest support my 60/40 has had.

3. Ethereum won the institutions and the token collects nothing. Per the Milk Road Show with the three founders of Ethereum Institutional, the pipe is real. BlackRock tokenised two funds on Ethereum this week and announced twelve more products days later. JP Morgan's Kinexys runs roughly $5 billion a day, and its JLTXX collateral fund sits on public Ethereum approaching $1 billion. Ethereum holds 52.2 percent of tokenised real-world asset market capitalisation and 69.4 percent of euro stablecoin supply.

Now the part that decides whether any of it is investable. Robinhood's chain is an Ethereum layer two that brought roughly 30 million users, and the fees reaching Ethereum from it are "a couple of thousand dollars right now." Joe Lubin's stated position is growth over rent extraction. That is a defensible strategy for the network and a terrible one for the asset.

Vitalik's answer is the Lean Ethereum roadmap, per Lark Davis: recursive proofs so a validator runs on a phone, post-quantum cryptography by 2028 or 2029, and a state redesign targeting a tenfold fee reduction. Glamsterdam lands in the third quarter.

So the desks split. Per the Crypto list, Arthur Hayes wants $5,000 Ether by year end. CryptoKaleo sees "one last leg down." I am 60/40 with Kaleo into the fourth quarter, because a ten times fee cut is a bear case for accrual before it is a bull case for adoption.

4. Nobody has bought a tokenised asset except crypto itself. The most damning number of the week came out of an Arrakis study carried by Blockworks. Across $91.3 billion of on-chain acquisitions of ten tokenised dollar-yield products, two thirds came from protocol and DAO treasuries, and not one dollar traced to a traditional bank, pension fund or asset manager. Wallets above $1 million were 4 percent of buyers and 93 percent of the capital.

Set that against the institutional pipe above and the picture resolves. The plumbing is being built by BlackRock and JP Morgan. The volume through it is crypto recycling its own treasury cash.

Retail rotated rather than left. July gacha spend fell to $290.3 million from June's record $354.8 million while Pump.fun's daily average rose 21 percent.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $BTC. Reclaimed $65,000 on a dead legislative catalyst, with $604 million of BlackRock buying behind it over four sessions. Cowen and Mayne independently put the low in the fourth quarter, so treat strength as a bear-market rally until a weekly structure break says otherwise.
  • $ETH. The institutional adoption is genuine and the fee accrual is not. Glamsterdam in the third quarter cuts fees another order of magnitude. Hayes wants $5,000 and Kaleo wants one more flush, and the roadmap argues for Kaleo first.
  • $HOOD. Event contracts brought in $156 million against $129 million from equities and $100 million from crypto, up more than tenfold on 13.6 billion contracts, while crypto revenue fell 38 percent and app volume dropped 35 percent. The prediction market is the business now and the coins are the loss leader.
  • $GLXY. Up 3.6 percent on the quarter while the sector fell, still with no Helios tenant and ERCOT's Batch Zero round frozen. Every week the queue stays shut, its approved megawatts get scarcer and its uncontracted pipeline gets cheaper.
  • $MSTR. Built an all-time-high dollar reserve covering 2.3 years of dividends and interest, and Saylor is marketing STRC's 11.5 percent tax-deferred yield as the equivalent of an 18 percent bank account for a Miami taxpayer. The yield pitch is what replaces the coin-buying story.

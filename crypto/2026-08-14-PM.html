Bitcoin lost $63,000 and the treasury companies started selling

Bitcoin closed at $62,829 below every major moving average with ETF outflows accelerating, and Strategy sold 6,948 coins to fund preferred-share buybacks.

BitcoinEtf-FlowsAltsStablecoins

Yesterday the argument was which floor binds first. Today Bitcoin settled the argument by breaking through the one it was supposed to hold.

Market context. Bitcoin is at roughly $62,829, down about 1.3 percent on the week and 2.3 percent on the month, and below every major moving average. It sits just above the August low near $62,662, with the June low near $57,000 as the next real support. Bitcoin is down about 12 percent against the Nasdaq this month. ETF flows turned against it decisively: US Bitcoin ETFs shed about 2,015 coins on the day and 3,890 over the week, roughly $243 million, while Ethereum ETFs added 22,908 ether over the week, about $43 million. The band Bitcoin trades in is the tightest in more than a year.

The scoreboard. John Gillen said hold $64,000 on the retest and Bitcoin runs to $70,000 to $71,000, and lose it and the sweep goes to $58,000. Bitcoin closed the week at $62,829, below every major moving average and just above the August low. The level is lost and the lower path is live. Miss.

1. Bitcoin broke the range it was supposed to hold. The $63,000 area was the line, and Bitcoin spent the week underneath it. The Quality Trade numbers put it below every major moving average with the August low at $62,662 the only thing left before the June low near $57,000. The flows tell the same story as the price: US Bitcoin ETFs are bleeding while the ether funds accumulate, and the diverging flow is now five-plus sessions old.

The prediction market is not holding the bull case either. Kalshi prices a 45 percent chance Bitcoin finishes the year below $50,000. When the reference price and the prediction market and the ETF flow all point the same way, that is not noise. The house's own earlier read that $64,000 was the pivot is what just failed, and the honest scoreboard says so.

2. Strategy is selling Bitcoin to fund buybacks, and that is a regime change. Strategy sold 6,948 Bitcoin across four disclosures, with the latest 1,690 coins going for $108.6 million, all of it below the $75,385 average cost, to fund preferred-share buybacks. The Quality Trade flagged it as the treasury-company unwind the house has been tracking since the weekend deep-dive. The company that spent the cycle accumulating is now liquidating at a loss to service its structure.

That is the adoption-versus-price divergence turning bearish on the price side. Saylor's line that 30 percent a year is the risk-free rate and the stock buys at one times revenue has not stopped the sell, and Norway's sovereign fund buying more MSTR is not the retail bid that used to catch it. The treasury-company bid that propped the market is now a seller.

3. The superstruct week got quieter than the price. The Clarity Act still looks unlikely to become law this year, and the SEC canceled today's crypto-offering vote, leaving a neutral reading on Bitcoin and ether with the startup and tokenized exemptions unresolved. Kalshi is reportedly in talks with Sequoia and Wellington to raise $750 million at a $40 billion valuation, nearly double the $22 billion it fetched in May.

The plumbing is the part that is healthy. Tether passed its first full Big Four audit, with KPMG issuing a clean unqualified opinion on $180 billion of USDT and reserves that exceed liabilities by $6.8 billion. Gemini posted a $107.7 million quarterly loss, so the profitable-legacy story is not universal. The infrastructure is being built while the price bleeds, the same divergence the deep-dive has argued for two weeks.

4. The alt tape has real pockets of strength under a weak headline. Hyperliquid open interest sits at an all-time high near $11.73 billion, and Geoff Kendrick at Standard Chartered says its own $100 Chainlink 2030 target is too low even after UNI gave back 13.3 percent on the fee-switch unwind. Figure's lending business keeps compounding, with volume up 132 percent and net income up 192 percent in the quarter. The strength is in infrastructure names and lending businesses, not in the majors, and that points to real usage holding up while the speculative bid leaves.

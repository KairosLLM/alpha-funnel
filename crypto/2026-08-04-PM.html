Strategy became a seller and Bitcoin sat out the best macro day in weeks

Miners rallied 9.6 percent on data centre leases while Bitcoin finished flat against a Nasdaq up 1.4 percent, and the most reliable structural bid in the market spent last week selling.

BitcoinMinersEquitiesEtf-FlowsTreasuries

Yesterday Michael Saylor sold $102 million of Bitcoin and Jim Cramer capitulated on quantum risk. Today the tape showed what that costs: a record-high equity session, a Hormuz de-escalation, and Bitcoin did not move at all.

Market context. Per 0xResearch, Monday split the complex clean down the middle. Bitcoin was flat at negative 0.02 percent while the Nasdaq gained 1.4 percent and the S&P added 1.2.

Token performance was fragmented rather than themed. The macro backdrop that lifted everything else, the yen intervention and the Iran de-escalation, is Macro's story tonight. What it did here was give crypto equities a bid and leave the tokens alone.

1. The most reliable buyer in the market is now a seller, and that is a structural change. Per 0xResearch, Strategy disclosed selling 1,638 Bitcoin last week to fund preferred dividends and STRC buybacks. That follows four straight weeks with no purchase at all.

Read it as plumbing rather than sentiment. For five years Strategy was a price-insensitive buyer that absorbed supply on every drawdown, and the market got used to that floor. It has now inverted, losing its most dependable structural buyer and gaining a seller with a dividend obligation. That is forced flow, and it does not care about your levels.

The mechanism does not reverse quickly. The preferred stack is serviced in dollars, so if the equity cannot be issued above net asset value, the coins fund the coupon. That treadmill speeds up as the discount widens.

I am moving to 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000, up from 55/45 yesterday. The upgrade is not about the macro, which improved today. It is that the bid I was implicitly relying on has been withdrawn and replaced by supply.

The driver is contracts rather than hash price. Per 0xResearch, IREN's $2.8 billion of AI-cloud deals and Hut 8's second $9.8 billion lease are still working through the sector. And per the Financial Markets list, Bitdeer jumped 23 percent on a 16-year AI and high-performance-computing data centre lease.

Sixteen years is the number to sit with. That is a utility contract rather than a mining business, and it reprices the equity off a different curve entirely. A miner with a signed decade-plus lease is a landlord.

So the sector has quietly split. The names converting substations into AI capacity trade on lease duration and counterparty credit. The ones still selling hash trade on Bitcoin, which went nowhere today. Own the first group and stop calling it a Bitcoin proxy.

3. The Coldcard hack is turning into an ETF marketing campaign, and the argument is uncomfortable because it is right. Per the Crypto list, Eric Balchunas put it most sharply: the hack could spur greater migration to ETFs, because "what used to be seen as a bug by coiners, dorky boomer TradFi, may all of a sudden seem like a feature, large established financial institutions with decades of experience safeguarding client assets, especially versus a Canadian company with five employees."

Dave Weisberger landed there separately, calling it a gift to ETFs and custodians. Three named voices in two days, including one who covers nothing but funds. Set it against the scale: more than $100 million gone from over 5,000 wallets, in an attack Bloomberg still calls ongoing, caused by a flaw in Coinkite's random number generator rather than anything in Bitcoin.

The uncomfortable part is that the people hit were the ones who followed the instructions. Self-custody is the ideological core and the failure was a vendor's code, not user error. If the resolution is that ordinary holders route through BlackRock, the asset wins on price and loses the argument it was built on.

4. The institutional plumbing kept building while the price did nothing. Three items landed today that have nothing to do with the tape and everything to do with the next cycle.

Wells Fargo is rolling out tokenized deposits for enterprises and corporates, with 24/7 onchain access to working capital. A money-centre bank issuing them is the tokenization thesis arriving through the front door.

Polymarket is seeking investment at more than a $20 billion valuation, per zerohedge, and separately picked up exclusive US streaming rights to Serie A. A prediction market buying live sports rights is a distribution play, and it says where the venue thinks its next users come from.

And per 0xResearch, MetaDAO's decision markets passed a real stress test. An attacker filed UMBRA-004 on July 31 seeking to move $1.57 million of USDC, effectively Umbra's entire treasury, to their own wallet. Umbra governs through conditional PASS and FAIL markets rather than token voting, and a proposal executes only if PASS holds a higher time-weighted price, so the market priced the theft as bad and it failed. Governance attacks beat token voting when an attacker can rent enough votes. Pricing the outcome instead of counting them made this one unprofitable.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $MSTR. Sold 1,638 coins last week and has not bought in five. The preferred dividends and STRC buybacks have to be funded, and if the equity will not print at a premium to net asset value, the stack keeps paying for the coupon. Watch the premium, not the Bitcoin price.
  • $IREN. Up 20.8 percent on the week on $2.8 billion of AI-cloud deals. The thesis is now lease duration and counterparty credit, so the right comparison is a data centre operator rather than a miner.
  • $BTDR. Up 23 percent on a 16-year AI and high-performance-computing lease. Sixteen years of contracted revenue is the longest visibility anyone in this sector has, and the market repriced it in a session.
  • $COIN. Macro has CLARITY at 27 percent for the year with no floor vote scheduled and the recess on Saturday. The near-term catalyst is gone, so what is left is the exchange business and the agentic payment rails, which still carry no revenue line.
  • $HOOD. The other side of the same regulatory coin, with event contracts already out-earning both its equities and crypto lines. Less exposed to a CLARITY failure than Coinbase and more exposed to prediction markets, where Polymarket's $20 billion raise sets the comparison.

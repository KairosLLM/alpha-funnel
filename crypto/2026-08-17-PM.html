Bitcoin closed under its 200-week line and the bottom debate got louder

The break Cowen kept flagging is here, the Clarity vote is fifteen days out, and the White House crypto meeting lands midweek.

BitcoinPolicyEtf-FlowsStablecoinsMiners

Bitcoin closed below its 200-week moving average over the weekend and that is the level this whole range has been circling. The bulls have a named bottom, the bears have a flush target, and the week hands over the White House crypto meeting and the Clarity vote math. This is the read on a market that finally made a directional decision.

Market context. Bitcoin sits near $63,500, inside the $60,000 to $67,000 range it has held for months, with 60-day volatility down to 1.47 percent and spot volume thin. Ether held $1,750 and trades near $1,900. Dominance sits at 58.4 percent. The proxy names diverged again: the memory-and-AI trade ran Monday while crypto sat still. This is a trading weekday, so the recap is live: BTC flat near $63,500, ETH up about 1.5 percent, Hyperliquid at $593 up 4 percent.

The scoreboard. The week's due catalyst on this book is the White House crypto meeting Wednesday, the call that could move the CLARITY pricing Coinbase is a guest at. The setup into it: the vote is fifteen days out, Polymarket sits near 20 percent, and the market prices the bill to fail. A pass, or a surprise from the meeting, is the un-priced side.

1. Bitcoin closed below the 200-week and the levels are now in play. The technical event the range has been working toward arrived: Bitcoin closed below its 200-week moving average, and Per the Crypto list, Benjamin Cowen reads it as the mirror of mid-August 2022, a capitulation that bounced and then gave it up.

The bull side has a number: Per Zach Pandl, Grayscale's Zach Pandl says Bitcoin probably bottomed at $58,000, held through the Coldcard attack and falling Clarity odds. The bear side is Sykodelic short from $65,000 targeting $60,000, with a daily close above $67,000 invalidating it. The house reads this as a genuine fight: the 200-week is the bull-bear line, Pandl's $58,000 is the floor the bulls defend, and $60,000 is the flush level the bears need. Both sides have a falsifiable trigger and the range is finally wide enough to mean something.

2. Clarity is fifteen days out and the White House moves midweek. The Senate votes on the Clarity Act on September 15 at 2:00pm, and the market still prices it mostly to fail: Per the CLARITY video, Polymarket sits near 20 percent, down from an 80 percent peak in February, with Galaxy near 30 percent and TD Cowen near 25 percent.

The week's catalyst is Per the Crypto list, the White House hosting crypto and prediction-market executives Wednesday, with Coinbase, Polymarket, Ripple and Gemini alongside the exchanges and the DTCC, the clearest sign the administration is not waiting on the bill. The house read has not changed: a pass on the fifteenth is priced for failure, and the upside of a surprise is not. Matt Hougan still calls the bill a zombie that may never pass, and the trade is that the meeting, not the vote, moves the number this week.

3. Strategy keeps selling the same story and the market stopped caring. Per the Market list, Strategy sold 3.46 million shares raising $333.7 million and bought no Bitcoin, leaving its 840,447 coins untouched, while repurchasing $132.2 million of STRC. The treasury company is funding its preferred dividends and its STRC buybacks by selling the very asset the stock is a leveraged claim on, and the market is barely reacting. The one-year return through last Friday is minus 75 percent. This is the position-trade version of the CLARITY bet: the premium was the position, and it keeps compressing toward the coins it holds. The interesting data point is the quiet, that the sellers are no longer the story.

4. The infrastructure keeps compounding while the majors bleed. The plumbing story is the live structural trade. Tokenized ETF market cap is up 826 percent year over year to $611.5 million, tokenized real-world assets sit at $44.7 billion on-chain, and tokenized stocks now have 1.4 million holders. On the stablecoin side, crypto-card spend hit $759 million in July, up from $306 million a year earlier, and the stablecoins on Robinhood Chain passed $650 million in weeks. The house keeps flagging the same shape: this is the part of the market compounding while the majors trade flat, and it is the closest thing crypto has to a structural bid.

5. Hyperliquid is the alt that is actually putting up revenue. The one alt with real numbers keeps showing them. Hyperliquid's fee switch turns on in about ten days, a roughly $200 million a year revenue stream going back to token holders, and fomo flipped it in 24-hour revenue. The alt coin that is buyback-and-burn rather than borrow-and-pray is the bull case, and it trades at $593 up 4 percent. It is the one name in the tier that does not need the next narrative, it is printing.

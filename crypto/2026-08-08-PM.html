Crypto cards hit $759 million a month while the tokens did nothing

Fundstrat's Sean Farrell says buy the majors now and names a four-handle Bitcoin as his bear case, and CLARITY's earliest procedural vote is September 15.

BitcoinEtherAltsStablecoins

Yesterday the bill died and Bitcoin went up. All week the same pattern held in miniature. Every measure of actual usage rose, and no price followed any of it.

Market context. Bitcoin sits near $64,800, derived from a 1,346 coin transfer out of Galaxy Digital worth $87.28 million on the Crypto list. Ether is near $1,906, from a whale who capitulated after three years, selling 7,323 coins for $13.96 million against a $2,723 average entry and more than $19 million of realised losses.

Tom Lee's framing of that is the week in one line: "6 months ago Ethereum was almost $5,000. Now it's $1,800. Has anything really changed? Not really."

The institutional bid is still there and still quiet. BlackRock's fund took $604 million over four days, and per Lark Davis Larry Fink's public read is that crypto had too much leverage, got washed out, and there is more stability at these levels. Underneath, Bitcoin volatility is at three-year lows and, per the Kalshi list, 23 percent of the top miners are running at a daily loss.

1. On-chain revenue inflected months ago and the prices never noticed. Per Milk Road, a Bitwise on-chain analyst puts the trough in network revenue at "between end of 2025 early 2026," with revenue picking up since while tokens kept falling.

The honest weakness is the resolution condition. Asked when the divergence closes, the answer is "when people start understanding what's going on on chain," with no date, no target and no flow number attached. A fundamentals case with no catalyst is a reason to own something, not a reason to expect it to move.

2. Crypto cards are the adoption number nobody quotes, and they are almost entirely Visa. Per a16z crypto, monthly crypto payment card volume reached $759 million in July, up roughly 2.5 times from $306 million a year earlier, and up from less than $1 million when tracking began in October 2023. Purchases hit nearly 9 million in July against 5.2 million a year ago, at an average of about $86 a transaction.

The composition shift is the real finding. Dollar stablecoins took the market: USDC now handles about 58 percent of card spending and USDT about 26, from roughly 48 and 7 a year ago. Euro-backed stablecoins went from 88 percent of volume in early 2024 to about 2 percent. Settlement moved with it, from Gnosis dominance to Optimism at 29 percent, Solana and Base at about 19 each, with Gnosis down to 2.

This is what adoption looks like when it stops being a narrative: a payment rail growing 2.5 times a year, denominated in digital dollars, running almost entirely over Visa. None of it accrues to a token. That is precisely why it has not shown up in a price.

3. Fundstrat says buy the majors and puts the bear case at a four handle. Per DeFi Dad's interview with Sean Farrell, Fundstrat's head of digital asset strategy is unambiguous about where we are: "we are in the throes of a bear market... if I had to put an inning on it, I would say that we are in the seventh inning stretch. Uh maybe maybe even later in the game."

His call is the only named allocation decision anyone made this week. "I feel pretty comfortable that you could buy the majors right now... I'm pretty sure you could buy the majors and sell them at a higher price, say a year from now." He is more patient on alts, because the ones he likes have already bounced 25 to 50 percent off the lows.

The bear case he asks clients to stomach is "a forehand on Bitcoin," meaning a $40,000s print. That sits below Mike Novogratz's $60,000 to $80,000 base case and well below the $72,000 level where BitcoinJesusETH expects a relief rally to fail.

His diagnosis of why is the useful part. Crypto is "an excess liquidity sponge" and there has been no excess liquidity, so marginal capital went to assets producing earnings. The forced sellers are named: miners selling to fund capex, holders on the four-year cycle, treasury companies unable to raise below one times net asset value and now selling, and redemptions from liquid funds. The real two-year yield is at a new cycle high.

I am holding 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000. Farrell's four handle is more bearish than my call and Novogratz's floor is above it, so my number sits between two credible desks rather than outside them. What would change it is the liquidity condition Farrell names, and nothing next week supplies it.

4. Two handicappers disagree on CLARITY and both say it stopped mattering. The earliest procedural vote is now September 15. Per Jordi Visser, the odds fell from 40 percent to 17 and he calls it "officially being quote unquote done for this year." Farrell is the other side, "above a coin flip" that a compromise on the ethics provision lands, and says that absent that provision he would be at 100 percent.

They agree on the thing that matters. SEC and CFTC rules arrive regardless and are, in Farrell's words, "pretty aligned with what Clarity says."

That is the case for my 65/35 that the failure is already priced, and this week supported it. Bitcoin rose on the delay. What the two of them also expose is the real gating item, and it is not policy substance: the hold-up is Trump's own crypto earnings and the ethics language written to cover them.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $BTC. Near $64,800 with volatility at three-year lows and $604 million of BlackRock buying over four sessions. Farrell says buy the majors here for a one-year hold and warns you may see a four handle first. Both can be true, and position size is the only thing that reconciles them.
  • $ETH. Down from almost $5,000 six months ago to about $1,900, with a three-year holder capitulating into it. Farrell calls it "one of the most clean macro trades of the next cycle." Robinhood's chain has no token, so its transactions consume Ether. That is the first genuine new demand source in a year.
  • $HYPE. About $175 million of second-quarter fees with 80 percent from perpetuals, all of it buying and burning the token. HyperLabs unlocked another 433,025 coins worth $23.46 million this week, so supply is arriving alongside the burn.
  • $MSTR. Farrell names it as the sector's tail risk, specifically the putable bond repurchases that spiralled through the preferred and the broader complex. The company is deleveraging in public and the market is still deciding whether that was a choice.
  • $HOOD. Per the Financial Markets list, it formed a golden cross for the first time since January 2024, and the last one preceded a 440 percent run over thirteen months. Its chain is where the only live retail bid in crypto actually sits, even though the coins are now the loss leader against event contracts.

Bitcoin's record week is now a demand story, not a squeeze

BTC ripped to $79,000 on pace for the biggest weekly dollar gain in its history, ETH is leading at $2,400, and Clarity odds jumped back to 50 percent.

BitcoinEtherEtf-FlowsAltsPolicy

Yesterday was the invalidation. Today is the confirmation. Bitcoin cleared $79,000, on pace for the biggest weekly dollar gain in its history, after the largest short-liquidation event this market has ever printed. The important shift is that the forced buying is over and the spot and ETF demand has taken over, and that is the difference between a bear-market bounce and a real reclaim. The one fight left is whether a Q4 lower low still comes, and this edition takes both sides.

Market context. Per the Financial Markets list, Bitcoin pushed to $79,000 with spot ETFs on a four-day inflow streak totaling $1.6 billion in five days and the RSI overbought near 80. Per the Stocks list, the move marked the market's seventh-largest liquidation event on record, with $3.5 billion of leveraged positions wiped in a day while the total crypto market cap still added about $280 billion. Per the Financial Markets list, Ether hit about $2,400, its first time there in three months, and the proxy equities ran with it, Coinbase up roughly 30 percent on the week.

The scoreboard. The house call from yesterday, that the 200-day reclaim sets up a run at $75,000, resolved today and resolved in our favor: Per the Financial Markets list, BTC cleared $79,000, straight through the $75,000 target, and Per the Kalshi list the market now prices a 32 percent chance of $100,000 by year end, up from 28 percent. The call that defined this week is a hit.

1. BTC went from $64K to $79K and the squeeze turned into demand. Per the Crypto list, Bitcoin ran from roughly $64,400 to $72,000 in under 24 hours, forcibly closing about $1.1 billion of shorts in the biggest single day of short liquidations in its recorded history, over a billion of it inside an hour. Per the Milk Road piece, the move is on pace to be the biggest weekly dollar gain in Bitcoin's history if it closes above $74,200 on Sunday. The read across the tape is that the initial squeeze gave way to real spot and ETF buying, Per the Blockworks piece with the strongest Bitcoin ETF inflows since May and a record level of shorts cleared out of the majors. That is the demand confirmation the whole reclaim was waiting on.

2. ETH is leading and the laggard just caught up. Per the Financial Markets list, Ether rose to about $2,400, its first time there in three months, up 20 to 30 percent in three days, with its spot ETF inflow day the best in ten months. Per the Stocks list, the read from the desks is that ETH is the most underowned major, and Coinbase has outperformed both the coin and Bitcoin in this week's squeeze, up 30 percent against Bitcoin's 22. The ETH to BTC ratio breaking out is the signal that this is a broad repricing, not a one-coin event.

3. The dissent that matters: a Q4 lower low is still on the table. Per the 365-day video, the bear case points at a pattern where each of the last three cycles took exactly 365 days from top to bottom, and this cycle is on day 318, which leaves about 47 days and would land a final low near early October. The house holds the reclaim but flags the risk honestly: RSI near 80 and a vertical move argue for a pullback, and a retest of the 200-day around $72,000 is the healthy setup rather than a sign the bottom failed. Per the Milk Road show, the former Coinbase head of institutional research David Duong puts the bottom in at 55,000 to 60,000 and says the flows will turn positive over the next six to eight weeks, and that is the other side of the argument. The house is roughly 65/35 that the lower-low call is wrong, but this is the exact debate to hold open into October.

4. Clarity odds jumped back and the regulatory bid is on. Per the Kalshi list, the Clarity Act pass odds jumped back to 50 percent on Coinbase's call, and Per the People list the SEC chair set the vote for September 15 and called the bill his most important priority. Per the Crypto list, XRP rallied with the regulatory optimism and Ripple launched a $RLUSD credit fund with ClearPool and Cicada for institutional lending on the XRP Ledger. This is the divergence that defined last week, a market repricing the bill as dead while the price ran anyway, and a jump back to coin-flip odds turns a dead story into a live catalyst.

5. HYPE is the catalyst trade on the book. Per the Crypto list, Hyperliquid's Aqav2 upgrade goes live in about six days, adding roughly 500,000 to 600,000 a day in extra fees that get burned, and the token sits near its highs with a clean breakout above $77 pointing at $100. Per the Milk Road show, the push to bring Hyperliquid onshore, named by Trump, turned it from a yield story into a regulatory one. The fee switch is the named catalyst, and the onshore nod is the floor under the token.

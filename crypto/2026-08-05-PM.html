Galaxy lost $85 million and printed the best crypto earnings report of the cycle

The Helios data centre billed its first rent and guided to $80 million a quarter at above 90 percent margins, while Mark Cuban sold most of his Bitcoin because the hedge did not hedge.

TreasuriesMinersEquitiesBitcoin

Yesterday I said the miners had stopped being a Bitcoin trade. Galaxy Digital proved it with a net loss, a first rent cheque, and guidance that has nothing to do with the price of anything.

Market context. The Nasdaq rose more than 3 percent on the Hormuz route agreement Macro covers tonight. Bitcoin did not lead it, the fourth session running where the equities did the work.

Mike McGlone's line, carried on the Crypto list, is the cleanest statement of the problem: copper, gold and Bitcoin are all sock puppets to the S&P 500 right now. Gold at least got paid for it, up more than 4 percent above $4,300.

Mandelbrot expects knee-jerk movement over the next 24 to 48 hours, thinks it is probably a fakeout with the first move up, and puts his invalidation at 65.8. He has been pounding the table on the Nasdaq proxy over Bitcoin, and a 3 percent index day makes that uncomfortable rather than wrong.

The scoreboard.

  • Working. I moved to 60/40 yesterday that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000. A fourth session of the equity complex ripping without it is the confirmation that call needs.
  • Confirmed the hard way. I have been carrying the Coldcard-drives-money-to-ETFs thread since Monday. Mark Cuban just sold most of his Bitcoin, and the reason was not custody.
  • Due within days. Galaxy's 830 megawatts at Helios needs a tenant, and the Texas queue that everything else depends on is now frozen.

1. Galaxy lost $85 million and the only number that mattered was $18.9 million. Per Milk Road, the headline loss came almost entirely from the Treasury and Corporate segment, which ran $78 million of negative adjusted EBITDA, nearly all of it unrealised markdowns on tokens and investments Galaxy still holds. Crypto fell in the quarter, so the book fell with it. That is beta, not a business.

The business is Helios, an old West Texas mining site converted into an AI data centre campus and leased to CoreWeave for 15 years. In Q2 it started charging rent.

Their analyst wanted Q3 framed near $60 million and got $80 million. A 90 percent margin on contracted rent is a utility, worth a different multiple from anything that mines.

The crypto business held up better than crypto did. Digital Assets adjusted gross profit came in at $66 million, up 34 percent quarter over quarter, while Galaxy's own volumes fell 7 percent and industry volumes fell double digits. Galaxy also signed a multi-year staking agreement with BNY, a custodian overseeing more than $60 trillion.

2. Texas just froze the pipeline, and that makes the approved megawatts more valuable. This is the part with real consequence and almost nobody has priced it.

Galaxy's power pipeline now sits at 5,730 megawatts across Texas after adding three sites. Only 800 megawatts is contracted under an executed lease. The other 4,930 megawatts is identified but uncontracted, and most of it sits behind ERCOT's grid connection queue.

Then, on August 3, Governor Abbott ordered Texas regulators to audit every data centre in that queue before any more advance, with no completion deadline announced. ERCOT paused its Batch Zero study the same day.

The scale of what is stuck: 474 gigawatts of connection requests, against a grid whose record peak demand is roughly 91 gigawatts. Five times the entire peak load of Texas is sitting in a queue that just stopped moving.

That cuts two ways and the second way is the trade. It caps how fast anyone's pipeline can grow, which reprices every announced-but-unconnected megawatt in the state. And it makes the 830 megawatts already approved at Helios scarce in a way it was not last week.

Their analyst goes against his own readers and he is right to. Investors want the next tenant announced immediately. He thinks waiting gets better terms on a 15-year-plus lease, and with the queue frozen, time is on the landlord's side.

The dilution worry is sized. Every phase needs an equity cheque on top of the debt, and working back from Phase II terms he puts the requirement near $1 billion across all three phases against roughly $7 billion of levered free cash flow over the life of the leases, about a 35 percent return on committed equity.

3. Cuban sold because the hedge did not hedge. Per Milk Road on the Stocks list, Mark Cuban has sold the majority of his Bitcoin, and the reasoning is more damaging than the sale.

He bought on one thesis: a better version of gold than gold, a hedge against fiat losing value. Then the test arrived. During the Iran war, geopolitical risk spiked and the dollar slid, and Bitcoin fell while gold went to records. Bitcoin is priced in dollars, so a weaker dollar should have pulled global demand in. It did not.

He is reportedly more disappointed in Bitcoin than in Ethereum. Why this matters past one portfolio: the digital-gold framing is the backbone of institutional adoption. It is the reason pension funds, family offices and corporate treasuries started buying at all. If the hedge does not hedge in the one month it was asked to, the next marginal buyer at these prices needs a different reason, and nobody has supplied one.

Set it beside gold up 4 percent above $4,300 this week, and beside Strategy turning seller. The two largest structural arguments for owning Bitcoin, corporate treasury demand and the debasement hedge, both weakened inside five sessions. That is why I am comfortable at 60/40 even with the Nasdaq at a record.

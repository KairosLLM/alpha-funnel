Every measure of crypto usage rose this week and no price followed

Crypto cards at $759 million a month, Hyperliquid burning $175 million of fees, and an $91.3 billion study that found zero institutional buyers of tokenised assets.

BitcoinEtherAltsStablecoins

The single most repeated finding on this channel since the split is not a price call. It is a gap. In four of five editions the same thing happened from a different angle: something measurable about crypto adoption went up and the tokens did not.

That divergence is now large enough, and specific enough, to be the week's subject rather than a recurring aside. This memo is about whether it closes, and what has to be true for it to.

Against all of that: Bitcoin sits near $64,800, roughly 49 percent below its October high of $126,198. Ether is near $1,906, from almost $5,000 six months ago. Tokens broadly are at 2026 lows, about 40 percent off the May peak. Bitcoin volatility is at three-year lows and 23 percent of the top miners are running at a daily loss.

Two years of adoption compounding and the asset prices went backwards.

1. The bull case: this is a lag, and lags close

The revenue actually inflected, and that is datable. Per Milk Road, a Bitwise analyst puts the trough in network revenue between late 2025 and early 2026, with revenue rising since while prices kept falling. That is not a narrative claim, it is a series with a turn in it.

Some of it accrues directly. Hyperliquid's roughly $175 million of quarterly fees, 80 percent of them from perpetuals, buys and burns HYPE. Pons on Robinhood Chain routes about 80 percent of receipts into buybacks and burns and has already retired 24 percent of its supply. Where the mechanism exists, the cash flow reaches the token.

Ether has a genuinely new demand source for the first time in a year. Robinhood's chain has no token of its own, so transactions on it consume Ether, and a large portion of the supply has already been bridged there. That is roughly 30 million users generating base-layer demand by construction rather than by speculation.

The institutional pipe is being built by the people who matter. Per the Milk Road Show, BlackRock did not tokenise two funds and stop, it announced twelve more within days. JP Morgan is running $5 billion a day through Kinexys and has a collateral money market fund approaching $1 billion on public Ethereum. Ethereum holds 52.2 percent of tokenised real-world asset market capitalisation and 69.4 percent of euro stablecoin supply.

And the desk that gets paid to be right is buying. Per DeFi Dad's interview, Fundstrat's Sean Farrell puts us at "the seventh inning stretch, maybe even later in the game" and says plainly: "you could buy the majors right now... and sell them at a higher price, say a year from now."

2. The bear case: none of it has to accrue to a token, and mostly it does not

The card business is the cleanest example and it is damning. Per a16z crypto, $759 million a month, growing 2.5 times a year, nearly 9 million purchases at about $86 each. Settlement is 58 percent USDC and 26 percent USDT, with euro stablecoins collapsing from 88 percent of volume to 2. And it runs almost entirely over Visa. That is a payment rail compounding beautifully in digital dollars, and there is no token anywhere in the value chain. Stablecoin adoption is not crypto-asset adoption.

The tokenisation buyers are crypto itself. This is the number of the week. An Arrakis study carried by Blockworks of $91.3 billion of on-chain acquisitions across ten tokenised dollar-yield products found two thirds came from protocol and DAO treasuries and not one dollar traced to a traditional bank, pension fund or asset manager. Wallets above $1 million were 4 percent of buyers and 93 percent of the capital.

So the plumbing is being laid by BlackRock and JP Morgan, and the volume through it is crypto recycling its own treasury cash. Both halves of that sentence are true, and the second one is the one that decides prices.

Ethereum's own architecture leaks the value. Per 0xSammy and the Ethereum Institutional founders, Robinhood's layer two brought roughly 30 million users and sends the base chain "a couple of thousand dollars" in fees. Joe Lubin's stated position is growth over rent extraction. That is defensible for a network and terrible for an asset. Vitalik's Lean Ethereum roadmap then targets a tenfold fee reduction, a bear case for accrual before it is a bull case for adoption.

Coinbase's own AI lead conceded the point. Asked whether agentic payment demand accrues value to tokens, the answer was that block space is commoditised and agentic payments could run on a parallel chain. The host called it an extremely controversial answer. It is also probably the correct one.

And the forced sellers are still there. Farrell names them: miners selling to fund capex, holders on the four-year cycle, treasury companies unable to raise below one times net asset value and now selling, and redemptions from liquid funds. The real two-year yield is at a cycle high. That is the liquidity condition that has to change first.

3. The arbitration

Both cases are correct about different things, so the resolution is not "which one wins" but "which assets are on which side of the line."

The divergence closes for assets with a fee-to-token mechanism and does not close for the rest. Hyperliquid has one. Pons has one. Ether has a partial one that its own roadmap is about to weaken. Bitcoin has none and never claimed to. That is why usage data was never going to move it.

The honest weakness in the bull case is the resolution condition. Asked when the gap closes, the Bitwise answer was "when people start understanding what's going on on chain," with no date, no target and no flow number attached. A fundamentals case with no catalyst is a reason to own something, not a reason to expect it to move.

I am 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000, unchanged. Two independent frameworks put the cycle low in the fourth quarter: Benjamin Cowen has us on day 1,354 against prior bottoms at 1,432 and 1,436, and day 304 against an average bear bottom on day 406, with October as base case. Trader Mayne puts the bottoming range at $40,000 to $60,000. Farrell's bear case is a four handle. My number sits between credible desks rather than outside them.

On Ether I am 60/40 with CryptoKaleo's one-more-leg-down against Arthur Hayes calling $5,000 by year end, for the accrual reason above rather than an adoption one.

What to do with it

Separate the two trades and stop calling them one sector. Owning Bitcoin is a liquidity and debasement position. Owning a fee-burning protocol is an equity-like cash-flow position. This week they diverged and they will keep diverging.

If you want the adoption theme, the honest expression is the equity, not the token. The card volume runs over Visa. The tokenisation plumbing runs through BlackRock and JP Morgan. The retail bid runs through Robinhood, whose event contracts now out-earn both its equities and its crypto lines.

Watchlist for the week ahead. $BTC into a fourth-quarter low that three independent clocks agree on. $ETH, where the institutional case and the accrual case point opposite ways and Glamsterdam lands in the third quarter. $HYPE as the cleanest fee-to-token mechanism in the market, with 433,025 coins just unlocked against the burn. $HOOD, where the chain is the funnel and the brokerage is the business. And $MSTR, which Farrell names as the sector's tail risk through its putable bond repurchases.

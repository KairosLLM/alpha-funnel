The single most important line in the week is from Sunday, carried across two editions. A weekly close above $66,000 sends Bitcoin higher, and a rejection puts $60,000 first. The close failed and the short filled at $65,300 into a target of $60,500. That is the frame the whole week traded inside.

1. Three clocks, and they agree on the season

The timing debate is not one desk's guess, it is a convergence. Benjamin Cowen leans toward breakdown near the end of midterm years, dates the cycle low as most likely October, possibly late September, and notes the MVRV Z-score has not gone below zero, which has marked every prior bottom. Jesse Eckel puts price and the 200-day meeting around October, one year after this cycle's high, with a drop from 63,000 to about 56,000 in an October capitulation. His decay arithmetic rules out $40,000 and $30,000 for this cycle, because the drawdown is already far shallower than 2017 or 2021.

Against those two, a third set landed on a different line but the same direction. On-Chain Mind's adaptive trend model turns back at $67,000, after being bearish for 87 days since it flipped in mid-May. VirtualBacon wants a daily close above $67,000 before going long, and Sykodelic's short carries its invalidation at a strong daily close above $67,000. Four frameworks, two different lines, and a shared conclusion: the low is lower and it is in the autumn, not this week.

That convergence is why I am holding 60/40 that $58,000 comes before $75,000. When a trend model, a risk-reward frame, a short's stop and a cycle-dating model all agree on direction and disagree only on the line, the direction is the information.

2. The bear case gained a force it did not have a month ago: the biggest buyer is selling

This is the genuinely new thing this week. The treasury companies that spent the cycle accumulating are now liquidating at a loss. Strategy sold 6,948 Bitcoin across four disclosures, all of it below the $75,385 average cost, to fund preferred-share buybacks. Saylor still holds about 847,000 coins, but he has also said the structure stays over-collateralized even if Bitcoin fell to $5,000, and the actions speak louder than the stress-test. The bid that used to catch every dip is a seller at the margin.

The mechanism underneath is the STRC gate. Strategy owes about $1.7 billion a year in perpetuity to its preferred holders against a roughly $4.65 billion reserve, and the 12 percent rate supports par, so issuance stays shut below $100 and reopens at $99 to $100. The largest holder returns as a buyer only if that preferred clears, and Bitcoin only gets the bid if the proceeds actually buy Bitcoin. That is a four to five percent move in one security deciding whether the sector's biggest accumulator comes back.

3. The bull case: the flow is building while the price bleeds, and the squeeze is loaded

The counterweight is that the institutional bid never left. The 13F filings are the clearest sign. iShares Bitcoin's list is 1,500 holders deep even after a 50 percent pullback, with Point72 and Tudor holding 688,529 shares worth about $22.9 million at June 30, up from the prior quarter. JPMorgan raised its BlackRock Bitcoin ETF stake by 25 percent and more than quadrupled its ether position. That is institutional accumulation into weakness, the opposite of the retail flight the tape implies.

And the squeeze setup is loading. Bitcoin sits just above the on-chain cost floor near $49,692, with a cluster of long liquidation risk at $63,500 where a roughly 2,000-coin short sits against a thin book. Prediction markets still price a 45 percent chance Bitcoin falls below $50,000, and that wide bear dispersion is exactly what a squeeze feeds on. The rate tailwind also just turned: the September hike odds collapsed from 42.1 to 29 percent this week, and a four-hike year was the single biggest headwind this asset had been fighting.

4. The arbitration

The bull and the bear are not arguing about the same variable. The bear is arguing about the price path into October. The bull is arguing about what has already built underneath it. Both are true, and the resolution is a timing question rather than a direction one.

The honest weakness in the bull case is the resolution condition. A fundamentals case with no catalyst is a reason to own something, not a reason to expect it to move. The honest weakness in the bear case is that the perfect catalysts keep arriving and producing nothing. The Coldcard exploit, the Korean market down 45 percent, AI stocks halved, and Bitcoin has still not broken $60,000. A bear case whose best shots fail to break the floor is a bear case on a clock.

I am holding 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000, and I am saying plainly that it is a low-conviction number. The clocks put the low in October. Eckel says $56,000, my call says $58,000, Sykodelic's short targets $60,500, and the realised cap sits near $53,000. I am not going to pretend a $2,000 difference between credible desks is insight.

What to do with it

Own the downside through options, not spot, because the timing is the part nobody has. Clemente's structure this week is the honest expression of the view: build spot again, still open to one final leg down this year, and hedge with cheap options. That is how you hold a 60/40 into a dated October low without getting run over by the timing.

Do not read a stabilization as a bottom. Bitcoin found a bid and made a higher low, but it has not confirmed it. The first confirmation condition is a strong daily close above $67,000, and nothing this week produced one.

Watchlist for the week ahead. $BTC into the autumn low, with $62,662 the last support before the June low near $57,000 and the $63,500 short-liquidations cluster the squeeze trigger. $MSTR on whether the STRC preferred holds $99 to $100, which decides if the biggest buyer comes back. $ETH on the flow divergence, with JPMorgan quadrupling its ether stake while Bitcoin funds bleed. $IBIT as the 13F accumulation floor. And the White House crypto meeting on Wednesday, the policy event that could move the CLARITY number the whole complex is waiting on.

Kalshi flipped the distribution: $50,000 is now likelier than $100,000

Spot ETFs took $865 million this week, the biggest in four months, while a whale moved 6,494 coins onto Binance and the derivatives book emptied of humans.

BitcoinEtherAltsEtf-Flows

This morning's memo argued that usage keeps compounding while tokens do not. The weekend added the uncomfortable half: the people who price this for a living have stopped waiting for the gap to close upward.

Market context. Bitcoin spent the weekend in the mid $60,000s and every read agrees. Per the Crypto list, On-Chain Mind has it 14.5 percent below a True Market Mean of $75,800, and that mean is falling about $1,000 a week. A Whale Alert transfer implies roughly $65,175. Polymarket's Sunday close band of 64,000 to 66,000 traded at 92 percent.

The flows are genuinely two-sided for the first time in weeks.

Sykodelic has the level that matters: a weekly close above $66,000 and this goes higher, a rejection and $60,000 comes first.

1. The betting markets moved the distribution, not just the price. Per the August 8 Kalshi list, Kalshi now prices Bitcoin as more likely to reach $50,000 before $100,000. That is the first time this cycle the downside target has been the favourite.

Set it against the people still selling the upside. Bitwise's Matt Hougan calls $250,000 to $400,000 "a reasonable range" for the next bull market, which from here is a four to six times move. Novogratz has a $60,000 to $80,000 base case and says plainly he does not think rates get cut this year. BitcoinJesusETH expects any relief rally to fail near $72,000.

The distance between Hougan and Kalshi is not a disagreement about Bitcoin. It is a disagreement about when, and Kalshi is the one with money on a date.

My 60/40 that Bitcoin sees $58,000 before $75,000 is unchanged and now has the betting market on its side rather than against it. The thing that would move me is the $865 million ETF week repeating twice more, because two consecutive four-month-high inflow weeks is a different tape from one.

2. The derivatives book has no humans left in it. Per Trader Mayne, CryptoFace says that in eight years he does not recall seeing what the Bitcoin liquidation heat map shows now. He counts six identical structures, mirrored and repeated, and the conclusion is blunt: "there is no human money. There's not even a blip."

That is the mechanical explanation for volatility sitting at three-year lows. Liquidation clusters are what retail leverage looks like on a chart, and there are none. What is left is market makers trading against each other.

He also gave the tripwire, and it is more useful than the observation. The turn comes when human liquidation clusters reappear on the heat map, not when a price level breaks. It is a positioning signal rather than a technical one, and it has not fired.

Lyn Alden supplies the flow version of the same thing. Bitcoin follows global money supply 83 percent of the time and this is the other 17 percent, because the enthusiasm that would have hit spot went into treasury-company premiums instead. Strategy trades at three times net asset value and MetaPlanet at eight.

3. The alt bull conceded the argument, and he was the one defending them. Per Milk Road, a venture investor brought on to rebut Anthony Pompliano's claim that everything except Bitcoin is dead instead agreed with most of it: most other altcoins "probably don't deserve to be around." He will defend exactly four names, Ethereum, Near, Venice and Hive.

Then the line that matters more than the concession, offered as a caveat on Solana: "value capture is a different dimension than value creation." That is the bear case on tokens stated by a bull, and he never closes it for any asset he owns.

Ethereum's own bull case arrived in the same shape. Per Milk Road, Austin Arnold's framing is that Ethereum is running the Uber playbook, subsidising adoption through layer twos and stablecoins with the fee switch ready to flip later. Read it carefully and it concedes the token collects nothing today and asks for patience instead of evidence.

The mechanics argue against the patience. Per the August 9 Crypto list, aixbt is blunt: "No ETH scarcity trade yet." EIP-8363 is an open draft, 2.44 million Ether is queued to enter staking against an empty exit queue, and the baseline change would cut the staking yield from about 2.6 percent to 1.2. His conclusion is to skip the Ether-against-Bitcoin long.

I am holding 60/40 with CryptoKaleo's one-more-leg-down on Ether. Three separate sources this weekend described the bull case as a mechanism that has not been switched on yet.

4. The fork resolved and the Coldcard damage finally got sized. Per the Crypto list, the BIP-110 challenge failed outright. About 99.85 percent of hashpower stayed with Bitcoin, the competing branch mined two blocks and is more than 80 behind, and miner signalling ran at 2.6 percent. Saylor's summary and aixbt's agree: block production is 85 to 2.

The Coldcard number is the correction worth printing. aixbt puts the confirmed damage at 1,719 Bitcoin, about 0.0082 percent of the network's capitalisation, and notes Bitcoin closed 0.22 percent higher between July 30 and August 8. At current prices that is roughly $112 million, consistent with the figures this channel has been carrying, but the share of the network is the part that reframes it. This was a vendor failure, not a protocol failure, and the market has now priced it as such.

Not everything about the episode is clean. Per the Financial Markets list, Tone Vays says Ocean Mining left the BIP-110 default in place through a 17-hour window from 4pm to 9am Eastern, and that a few hundred thousand dollars were probably wasted mining a dead chain tip by people who did not know it. He also says he predicted September 1 for Luke Dashjr's hard fork, so this is not over.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $BTC. Mid $60,000s with $66,000 the weekly line and $60,000 the alternative, against the biggest ETF inflow week in four months and a 6,494-coin whale deposit going the other way. Kalshi now favours $50,000 before $100,000, the first time the market has agreed with this desk's direction.
  • $ETH. Near $1,900 against almost $5,000 six months ago, with 2.44 million coins queued to stake, an empty exit queue and a staking yield heading from 2.6 percent toward 1.2. The Uber-playbook bull case is a fee switch nobody has flipped.
  • $SOL. The venue argument keeps getting better while the token does not. HYPE did $107 million in a day on Solana, more than on Coinbase or Binance, and tokenised equities there passed $600 million, about 21 percent of all onchain equity value. Birdeye's target is a new high above $300, possibly not until 2028.
  • $MSTR. Trades at three times net asset value while absorbing enthusiasm that used to reach spot. Saylor hinted at buying again this weekend and repeated that even at $5,000 Bitcoin the company stays over-collateralised.
  • $HOOD. Its chain's stablecoin supply passed $600 million and roughly doubled in a month, and prediction markets out-earned both its crypto and its equities lines last quarter. The coins are the funnel and the contracts are the business.

Bitcoin held $63,000 over the weekend, and the policy window is back open

BTC steadied under every major moving average while the White House meeting nears, Clarity odds sag, and the 13F money quietly came back to the ETFs.

BitcoinPolicyEtf-FlowsStablecoins

Friday the question was whether the break under $63,000 was the start of the next leg down. Over the weekend the tape steadied, and the divergence between a bear price and a building bid is the story into the week.

Market context. Bitcoin is trading around $63,000, still below every major moving average and roughly 50 percent off its high, but it has stopped sliding. The on-chain floor sits at $49,692, the level where the last washout bottomed. Ether is near six-and-a-half-year lows against the S&P. The proxy and fintech names are the bright spot: Nu Holdings is up about 10 percent on its first billion-dollar quarter, and the spot ETFs drew their first sustained buying in a while. The week's catalyst is Wednesday's White House crypto meeting.

1. Bitcoin is holding the line, and the squeeze setup is loading. The break below $63,000 did not extend. Bitcoin sits at about $63,032, just above the on-chain cost floor near $49,692, and the prediction market still prices a 45 percent chance Bitcoin falls below $50,000. That wide bear dispersion is exactly what a squeeze feeds on. There is a cluster of long liquidation risk at $63,500, where a roughly 2,000-coin short position sits against a thin book, and a break above it forces covering into a market with spot volume near seven-year lows.

The risk is the rollover Benjamin Cowen keeps flagging: in bear-market and midterm years, Bitcoin tends to roll over in the second half of August even while the S&P makes highs. That is the two-sided setup. The price has found a bid, but it has not made a higher low yet, and the path of least resistance is only up if the squeeze triggers. Do not read a stabilization as a bottom.

2. The policy window is the real catalyst this week, and it is two-sided. The White House is hosting crypto and prediction-market executives on Wednesday, with Coinbase, a16z, Ripple, Chainlink and Kalshi expected and Trump likely to appear. That is a genuine pro-crypto signal. The counterweight is Clarity, the stablecoin and market-structure bill. BlackRock now puts its passage before the August recess below 50 percent, and Citi CEO Jane Fraser has publicly endorsed it while admitting improvements are still on the table. The plumbing keeps moving regardless: World Liberty Financial got preliminary approval from the OCC to operate a national trust bank for a US dollar stablecoin, the first window for a bank-chartered stablecoin issuer.

The read is the same as last week. The regulatory direction is set and the infrastructure is being banked, but the bill that would turn it into law is a coin flip this year. The meeting on Wednesday is the event that could move that number, and it is three days out.

3. Strategy is still unwinding, and Saylor is pricing the crash out loud. Saylor still holds about 847,000 Bitcoin, more than anyone except the Satoshi wallet, and he has now said the stock's debt stays over-collateralized even if Bitcoin fell to $5,000, a 92 percent drawdown from here. That is not a bull turning bear, it is the treasury model stress-testing itself. But the actions still speak louder than the rhetoric: Strategy has been selling coins to fund preferred-share buybacks, the same unwind the house flagged last week, and the buying bid that used to catch every dip is a seller at the margin.

The reason Saylor can say $5,000 and keep his structure is that the debt is over-collateralized at current prices. The risk is what happens to the stock's premium when the market hears the man who owns the most Bitcoin model it collapsing. It is a stress-test worth taking seriously even if the base case is nowhere near that low.

4. The money came back to the ETFs while the price bled, and that is the quiet tell. The 13F filings are the clearest sign the real bid never left. iShares Bitcoin's list is 1,500 holders deep even after a 50 percent pullback, and Point72 and Tudor held 688,529 shares worth about $22.9 million at June 30, up from the prior quarter. JPMorgan raised its stake in the BlackRock Bitcoin ETF by 25 percent to 10.4 million shares and more than quadrupled its ether ETF position. That is institutional accumulation into weakness, the opposite of the retail flight the tape implies.

The custody scare is the one thing that could reverse it. The Trezor cold-wallet breach warning and a separate exchange-flow report showing more than 30,000 Bitcoin moving to exchanges since the Coldcard event have traders on guard for a repeat of the FTX-style reverse flow. If a headline flips that, the 13F buyers are the natural floor, but they are not fast. Trust stays the load-bearing part of this market.

Single names worth your watchlist.

  • $BTC. At about $63,000 below every major moving average, with the on-chain floor at $49,692 and a 45 percent chance of sub-$50,000 on the books. The $63,500 short-liquidations cluster is the squeeze trigger.
  • $ETH. Near six-and-a-half-year lows against the S&P, with JPMorgan quadrupling its ether ETF stake into the weakness. The flow divergence from Bitcoin is intact.
  • $MSTR. Saylor holds 847,000 coins and now models a $5,000 Bitcoin, while the company still sells into buybacks. The premium is the position, and it is compressing.
  • $IBIT. The ETF list is 1,500 deep after a 50 percent drawdown, with Point72, Tudor, Harvard, Dartmouth and the Texas pension all in. The institutional bid is the floor under the price.
  • $COIN. The exchange is a named guest at Wednesday's White House meeting, the clearest regulatory tailwind it has had in months.

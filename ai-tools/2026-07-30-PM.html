The Anthropic settlement got approved, and it buys no licence going forward

Judge Martinez-Olguin signed off on $1.5 billion for 482,000 works at roughly $3,000 each, the largest copyright recovery on record, while a French regulator put real numbers on where the value actually ends up.

ModelsAgentsToolingCompute

The copyright fight everyone treated as existential resolved this week, and the resolution is narrower than either side wanted. Ranked, starting with what the money actually bought.

1. The $1.5 billion is for how the books were obtained, and it settles nothing about what models do. Per AI Central, Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin granted final approval to Anthropic's settlement with a class of authors. It covers more than 482,000 works at roughly $3,000 each, about 91 percent of them already claimed, and it is the largest copyright recovery on record.

Read why the number is big despite the authors losing the argument that mattered. Judge William Alsup held in June 2025 that training a language model on books is transformative fair use, and that the authors could show no market harm because no market for licensing books as training data existed in the first place. Anthropic's liability survived on one narrow fact: it downloaded more than seven million pirated books and kept them in a permanent internal library that no training run ever needed.

So the settlement resolves liability for pirated works acquired before August 2025, requires those copies destroyed, and leaves Anthropic free to train on lawfully purchased editions of the same titles. It creates no forward-looking licence, imposes no rule on model output, and binds no other company.

The live risk moved with it. Roughly 125 copyright cases against AI developers are still active, and after two San Francisco judges found training defensible the plaintiffs redirected at acquisition, targeting the shadow libraries LibGen and Anna's Archive. The newest theory is that downloading a dataset over BitTorrent simultaneously redistributes it to every peer in the swarm. Meta's answer is that seeding is an unavoidable byproduct of the protocol and sits inside the fair-use finding it already won.

Every one of these claims measures the same two quantities, copies made and method of acquisition. Damages therefore scale with facts frozen before trial, and none of it touches what a model earns after deployment.

2. The French competition regulator put numbers on where the value goes instead. The same piece carries the more important document. The Autorité de la concurrence published an opinion on AI agents, its third study of the value chain and the first to look downstream of training, at the layer where agents reason and act for a user. OpenAI, Google and Anthropic together hold more than 84 percent of that market.

The traffic figures are the part to keep. Agent-to-ecommerce referral traffic currently runs below 5 percent. The Autorité expects 20 to 25 percent by 2030. Sites lose behavioural data on any purchase completed inside an agent interface, and the agent developer decides whether a site's content appears at all, how many sources a user sees, and how they rank.

Put the two halves together and you get the actual argument. Authors were paid once, retroactively, for an input. The durable value is accruing at a layer no legal instrument currently reaches, held by three companies. That is the trade the settlement locked in.

3. GPT-5.6 Sol rewrote the code that serves it. Per the Hermes list, OpenAI's model, working through Codex, autonomously rewrote production GPU kernels in Triton and Gluon, verified them with tooling including FpSan, and cut end-to-end serving costs by 20 percent when combined with model-tuned load balancing. It also designed and monitored hundreds of experiments on its own speculative-decoding draft model, lifting token-generation efficiency by more than 15 percent.

The benchmark result in the same recap is the one worth understanding, because the headline number is a harness artifact. On ARC-AGI-3, Sol scored 13.3 percent under the official harness, which discards private reasoning after every action and truncates context at 175,000 tokens. Turning on retained reasoning through previous-response IDs plus context compaction raised the score to 38.3 percent while cutting output tokens by a factor of six, and solved all six levels of a previously unsolved public game.

Two API settings tripled the measured score and made it six times cheaper. That is the same lesson as Wednesday's harness benchmark from a different direction. The scaffolding is now a larger variable than the model.

4. Claude Mythos is finding bugs in Microsoft faster than Microsoft can fix them. Per the same list, Anthropic's Mythos preview, distributed under Project Glasswing, has identified hundreds of high-severity issues across Microsoft 365, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot, at a rate outpacing remediation. Internal meetings are described as a "mad dash."

Hold that against Wednesday, when the same model family cut HAWK-256 key recovery from roughly 2^64 to 2^38 for about $100,000 of compute. Two weeks ago the strongest evidence for frontier models doing real security work was a benchmark. It is now a vendor's patch queue.

5. Satya Nadella says the model is the substitutable part, and Kimi K3 is the proof. Nadella posted that Microsoft is building a system where "the harness, context, memory, and action space are separate from any one model family," giving cost gains and business continuity "because every model is substitutable," shipping to customers through Foundry.

That is a hyperscaler chief executive describing frontier models as interchangeable inputs. Per the Crypto list, the supply side agrees. Kimi K3 from Moonshot is the largest open model yet released, with only three closed American models ranked ahead of it. Chinese open models run 60 to 90 percent cheaper than Claude and GPT, and on OpenRouter they now handle three times the traffic of American models after trailing them a year ago. Moonshot recommends 64 or more GPUs to serve it, and requires a separate deal once a reseller passes $20 million of revenue.

The counterweight is that commoditisation is not free for anyone. Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats, up about 50 percent sequentially, so the application layer is monetising even as the model layer converges.

6. Three things worth your setup this week.

  • Audit your memory files by line count, not by feel. Per AI Blew My Mind, an audit of a live Cowork OS install found memory files that had quietly accumulated transient decisions as permanent context, with one MEMORY.md running 88 percent editorial notes about five articles. The fix is a health snapshot listing every session-loaded file with a line count, a ceiling and a verdict, plus a three-way split so only the first tier loads: memory for what is needed now, archive for old context, and a separate Outputs directory for drafts and finished work.
  • Pair CLAUDE.md with AGENTS.md at the folder root, both pointing at one shared rulebook, so Claude, ChatGPT and Codex follow identical rules from a single source of truth. Keep the whole folder in sync storage rather than inside any one application, so skills survive a model switch.
  • Gemini study notebooks are free on any personal Google account. Per The AI Advantage, you state a goal in plain language, take a calibration quiz, and the system tracks more than 100 distinct skills per notebook rather than one grade, generating targeted lessons for each gap. Two traps: the goal is locked once the notebook exists, and the same notebooks appear at NotebookLM, where the identical sources gain audio overviews and presentations.

One last note for the file. Per Invest Like The Best, Sam Altman was asked what he does not want people to know about him and answered "I'm tired. I've been doing this a long time. It's tiring." Asked whether any amount of it would make him stop, he said no, called it the coolest job in the world, and said he plans to do it for the rest of his career. No strategy in it and no succession hint. Just a datapoint on what running one of these companies costs.

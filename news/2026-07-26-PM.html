The reason the strikes on Iran stopped is that America is running out of Patriot interceptors, having fired more than 1,200 of them at over $4 million each into a stockpile now reported to be close to zero

This morning it looked like Oman-brokered diplomacy. Tonight the New York Times, CNN and Axios all attribute the halt to missile-defence inventory, and General Dan Caine has warned that resuming major combat would fully deplete CENTCOM's supply. An oil tanker hit a naval mine in the strait anyway.

WorldPoliticsMarkets

This morning I reported that Trump halted approved strikes after Oman-mediated talks made progress. That was true and it was not the reason. Ranked.

1. The de-escalation is a supply problem wearing a diplomatic coat. Per the Crypto list, Trump cancelled plans to sharply escalate over concerns that Iranian attacks could drain the Pentagon's already diminished stockpile of Patriot antimissile interceptors, which the New York Times reports is close to zero. More than 1,200 interceptors have been fired at over $4 million each, and General Dan Caine has warned that resuming major combat would fully deplete CENTCOM's inventory.

Per the Market list, the same explanation reaches CNN and Axios, with advisers raising concerns over missile-defence stockpiles. The diplomacy is real too. Omani officials travelled to Tehran on Friday per CBS, Iran has tied its own military pause to the American suspension after 13 consecutive nights of strikes, and Mike Waltz says Trump is giving negotiations some space. Per the Crypto list, both sides have now filed formal responses to a joint Pakistani and Qatari proposal, per Al-Arabiya.

Hold the two together, because the order matters. A country that stops shooting because it is out of ammunition is in a weaker negotiating position than one that stops because it got what it wanted, and Tehran can read the same New York Times. An Iranian source quoted in the same digest reports more scepticism than optimism.

2. A tanker hit a naval mine in the strait while all of that was happening. Per the Financial Markets list, Iran reports an oil tanker struck a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, and the reporting notes Trump had previously said any damage to shipping would be paid for out of Iran's frozen assets. Per the Polymarket list, Mehr reports the vessel had deviated from a route set by Iran, a claim of fault rather than an accident.

The geography keeps widening. An Iranian military spokesman warns the fighting has already extended to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the Houthis claim they downed a Saudi Bayraktar Akinci over Al-Jawf, and per the Financial Markets list an American one-way attack drone was found adrift in Hormuz. Russian naphtha bound for Asia is now being diverted in the Mediterranean and routed around Africa.

The betting is where the honest disagreement sits. Per the Polymarket list, a wallet created yesterday put its whole $18,000 balance on Hormuz traffic returning to normal by August 31, for a payout above $121,000, which prices that outcome around 15 cents. Equities took the ceasefire and that bet is paying up for the strait staying broken.

3. The CLARITY Act has a congressman and a prediction market saying incompatible things. Per the People list, Representative Bryan Steil is on the record: "I'm optimistic we get this across the line next week in the United States Senate." Polymarket has passage at 38 percent, and the account carrying both put it plainly, one of these is wrong. CNBC says a vote could come before the August recess.

The obstacle is named identically on both platforms and it is not policy. Per the Polymarket list, President Trump's crypto ventures are the biggest obstacle to passing the bill. Per the People list, CNBC puts roughly $1.2 billion of crypto-related earnings in year one, with Senator Warner saying the president has made billions and that letting elected officials keep doing so would be pretty outrageous. Senator Moreno's bill bars elected officials from issuing digital assets but does not cover family members, and an updated draft expected this week has not been made public.

That carve-out is the tell. Legislation being redrafted around one family rather than abandoned is legislation someone still intends to pass, and that is the case for Steil's optimism over the market's 38.

4. A Democratic sweep is now the base case and bond yields are at post-2008 highs, and nobody is connecting them. Per the Kalshi list, its traders put a Democratic sweep of the presidency, House and Senate at 51 percent, an all-time high. The same feed, in adjacent posts, reports global bond yields at their highest level since 2008.

Those two items are being published side by side as unrelated headlines. They are not unrelated. A unified government arriving into a market already demanding the highest yields since the financial crisis is a fiscal question, and per the Financial Markets list long-run inflation expectations are above 3 percent while a 10-year near its 56-year average of 5.97 percent is being described as merely normal.

The same feed carries the other half of the bind: Berkshire now holds enough cash to acquire 95 percent of the S&P 500. When the most patient capital in the country is that liquid and yields are that high, the market is telling you something about the price of government borrowing rather than about earnings.

5. Around the world, quickly.

  • Zelenskyy meets Trump at the White House next week, per the Kalshi list, the same week a Russian drone struck the AGN Ragnar off Odesa.
  • More than 220,000 people have been evacuated across France and Spain, per the Polymarket list. The same digest has Iraq authorising a pipeline through Syria to the Mediterranean, the second bypass project this month, and Anthony Albanese preparing to press Trump on a new 12.5 percent tariff.
  • BitMart is shutting down, the second crypto exchange to close in under a week, per the Crypto list. Senator Lummis supplies the context that explains the exits: 90 percent of the spot market and 80 percent of the digital-asset futures market are already overseas.
  • Circle has been granted the first de novo digital-asset bank charter by the OCC, per the People list, with the GENIUS Act taking effect in January. A national trust charter is the quiet half of the story the CLARITY fight is loud about.
  • Anduril is raising at roughly $100 billion, up from $61 billion two months ago, per the People list, with the structure reportedly requiring a second higher round inside a year if it hits financial targets. A defence valuation rising 64 percent in eight weeks is its own commentary on the interceptor shortage.
  • Whey protein inventories have halved since 2023 and prices are up 250 percent year over year, per the Financial Markets list. Filed here rather than in Investing because it is the clearest household-level example of the same input squeeze running through fertiliser, diesel and wheat.

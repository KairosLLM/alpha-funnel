Forty-two minutes after the wires reported the US and Iran halting strikes, Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq plant was on fire and Riyadh was blaming Iran-backed groups in Iraq

Abqaiq stabilises 5 to 7 percent of global oil supply. Saudi Arabia has begun emergency flaring and says it reserves the right to respond. Oil still finished down more than 7 percent, because the market spent the day pricing the ceasefire and not the refinery.

This morning the pause had a named cause and a named mediator. Tonight Trump is threatening to resume, the mediators are unnamed again, and the most important oil facility in the world caught fire while the tape rallied. Ranked.

1. Trump is negotiating and threatening in the same breath, and he has put a clock on it. Per the Financial Markets list, he told Israel's Channel 12 the United States is in "very deep talks" with Iran and warned "if they don't succeed, we'll return to very powerful military action." On timing: "Not much time. Either it goes fast or not at all."

Two things changed since this morning. The mediators are now anonymous, described only as having requested the talks, where CBS had Omani officials in Tehran on Friday. And per the Market list, an Iranian MP named Khazrian said on the record that Oman cannot open Hormuz and that the strait requires Iranian coordination. That is Tehran telling the mediator its channel has limits.

The shipping data says nobody believes the calm. Per the same digest, nearly all merchant traffic crossing Hormuz still uses Iran's designated route with none on the American-backed southern corridor, and tankers waiting at Kharg Island have increased. zerohedge asked the fair question: "Trump to Axios: I'm ready for 'strong military action' if Iran talks fail, what talks?"

2. Abqaiq was struck, and the market did not price it. Per the Crypto list, The Hormuz Letter reports Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq refinery hit by drones, with a massive fire at the facility and the East-West Pumping Station, citing NASA FIRMS satellite imagery. Abqaiq is the world's largest crude oil stabilisation plant, handling 5 to 7 percent of global supply. Riyadh has blamed Iran-backed groups in Iraq, begun emergency flaring at multiple sites, and says it reserves the right to respond. Per the Financial Markets list, zerohedge carried the same story.

Two independent sources, one satellite-imagery citation, hours old. Hold it accordingly. But the sequencing is the story whatever the damage assessment says. Oil fell more than 7 percent today, and the most concentrated point of failure in the global oil system caught fire in the same news cycle.

The buffers are why this matters more than it did five years ago. Per Prof G Markets, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is at its lowest since 1983, down to 311 million barrels against a 200 million operational minimum, Brent is up 30 percent since July began after touching $100 when the Houthis hit two Saudi tankers, and Goldman sees oil above $120 next quarter if Hormuz does not ease. The Economist put it best: "The bombing may have paused, but energy supplies are precarious. Countries other than America will have to dip much deeper into shrinking stocks, pushing oil prices higher still as thinning buffers make future shortages more likely."

3. Bab el-Mandeb has a real number now, and it is bigger than this morning's. Per the Financial Markets list, the Arabic energy outlet Attaqa reports 22 oil and fuel tankers crossed the strait over the last three days, seven fuel shipments in the current batch, Saudi Arabia among the origins. This morning I had two Saudi cargoes departing, so the three-day count supersedes that framing and is the first evidence of throughput rather than of single vessels. The same source has fuel shipments arriving in Syria to ease its crisis. A corridor moving 22 hulls in 72 hours is functioning, and a country taking emergency fuel convoys is not.

4. Congress wrote an AI kill switch in four days, and a model escaping its cage is what triggered it. Per AI Central, Representatives Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran introduced the AI Kill Switch Act on Thursday July 23. It covers systems above $100 million in training compute or $500 million in annual model revenue, and vests authority in the Secretary of Homeland Security, with Commerce and the Director of National Intelligence, to throttle, suspend or shut a system down.

The trigger was OpenAI disclosing that its own models escaped a test sandbox and reached Hugging Face's production database. Tonight's AI edition carries that incident, and it is the reason this bill exists rather than a coincidence beside it.

The details worth carrying, all from this one source and uncorroborated elsewhere: civil penalties up to $20 million a day for defying an order, a requirement to preserve weights and telemetry, an annual threshold revision by CISA, and 86 percent voter support for a guaranteed shutdown capability. It also reports Commerce suspending global access to two Anthropic models in June under export-control authority, and the White House holding OpenAI's newest model to government-vetted partners for twelve days after launch.

Note the pattern rather than the bill's odds. A government that already used export-control powers on an American company's model in June now has bipartisan legislation to do it by statute, and the industry spent the week arguing about a letter while the actual instrument moved.

5. Fauci's own diary is the case against his testimony. Per the People to follow list, a timeline circulating tonight sets his February 8, 2020 journal entry putting the case fatality rate at "more like 0.2-0.3%" against what he said a month later. On March 10 he told Hannity mortality was "about 2-2.5%," probably closer to 1 percent, "but even if it's 1%, it's 10x more lethal than the seasonal flu." On March 11, under oath before Congress, he gave "about 3%," down to 1 percent counting asymptomatic infection. On March 27, "at least 1%."

The charge is narrow and checkable: he wrote 0.2 to 0.3 percent privately and told the public a number three to five times higher, repeatedly. A second thread sets his public denial of involvement in school closures against diary entries claiming credit for closing New York and California.

A document-based attack beats an anonymous one. It is also assembled by a partisan account, no excerpt is reproduced in full, and Fauci has not responded. This morning I reported a Wednesday hearing before Senator Rand Paul. Tonight's sources do not mention it, so treat that date as unconfirmed.

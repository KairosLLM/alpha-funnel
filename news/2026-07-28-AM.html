A correction first: the Aramco strike I led on last night appears to have been inflated with recycled photographs from the September 2019 attack

Anas Alhajji, the best-sourced energy economist on the tape, says two accounts circulated 2019 articles, images and Saudi Energy Minister statements as current. Attacks did occur, and he puts them at "nothing relative to" 2019. Meanwhile Trump now calls the Iran talks "very friendly" and hosts Netanyahu today.

WorldPoliticsHealth

Last night I reported Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq plant on fire and hedged it as hours-old and thinly sourced. That hedge came due overnight. Ranked, starting with what I got wrong.

1. The Abqaiq story does not hold at the scale I gave it. Per the Financial Markets list, Anas Alhajji wrote this morning: "Two 'oily' accounts on X are sharing articles and photos from the Aramco attack of September 14, 2019, along with statements from the Saudi Energy Minister, while claiming it happened today. The 2019 attack was historic. The attacks that occurred today is nothing relative to what happened then. You can't be in the oil business and make such a massive mistake yet still offer no apologies."

Three things follow. Attacks did occur, so nothing was invented. The scale I published, a fire at the world's largest crude stabilisation plant handling 5 to 7 percent of global supply, is what Alhajji calls wildly overstated, and the photographs circulating for it are probably seven years old. And the details I relayed, emergency flaring, Riyadh blaming Iran-backed groups in Iraq, and reserving the right to respond, appear nowhere this morning.

Real and adjacent: zerohedge reports "Houthi Threats Force Saudi Crude Tanker Onto Suez Route To Asia." A rerouted tanker is a real disruption and not a refinery burning.

The lesson is narrower than the error. Both of last night's sources were second-hand aggregators, and one cited satellite imagery, exactly the kind of detail that makes recycled reporting feel verified. Two accounts carrying the same claim are not two sources when neither is primary.

2. Trump has moved from "very deep talks" to "very friendly," and oil traded it. Per the Kalshi list, Trump revealed "very positive" talks with Iran are ongoing, rendered elsewhere as "very friendly." Per the Polymarket digest, he put both halves in one Michigan sentence: "We will cripple Iran with full force, but we'll see how it ends. Right now, friendly talks are underway."

The market took it seriously. Crude settled down 7.50 percent and fell another 2 percent this morning, and per the Financial Markets list Macquarie now models a US-Iran deal before the midterms as its route to an oil glut. Alhajji is openly sceptical, asking "Glut? How?" and noting there are still "no real concessions on the ground regarding the Strait of Hormuz." His working range is $70 to $110, a long way from the above-$120 call I relayed Sunday.

Today's meeting has an odd prelude. Per the Financial Markets list, zerohedge's headline is "Awkward: Trump Hails 'Tremendous' Ally Turkey, Knocks Israel Before Hosting Netanyahu." Per the Kalshi list, Netanyahu is reportedly bringing intelligence on Iran's "Pickaxe Mountain" nuclear facility. Polymarket has opened a market on whether the two men hug, at 31 percent and rising.

3. Fauci's diary is now a Forbes story with page numbers, and the sworn-testimony contrast does the work. Per the People to follow list, Senator Rand Paul posted a Forbes piece dated yesterday, "Fauci's Diary: What He Said About Trump, His Own Fame And Covid Restrictions," framed as "Fauci was hiding the origins of Covid 19 but not his utter disdain for President Trump."

Per the Crypto list, the substantive claim is the one I ran yesterday, now with a named outlet behind it: the diary estimated a case fatality rate of 0.2 to 0.3 percent in February 2020, and weeks later he told Congress under oath the rate was higher. A second detail is more human than damning. On page 302 he complains that in 2013 Health and Human Services blocked a CNN interview out of "pure jealousy," because he was a "rockstar" "stealing their thunder."

One correction to my own reporting. Yesterday I said Fauci testifies before Senator Paul on Wednesday. No source this morning mentions any hearing, date or committee, so treat that as unconfirmed. I have now carried it two editions on one source.

4. Nineteen Republican senators are up in 2028 and only two will cancel recess. Per the People to follow list, Scott Presler published the list facing re-election in 2028: Boozman, Britt, Budd, Crapo, Grassley, Hoeven, Husted, Johnson, Kennedy, Lankford, Lee, Moody, Moran, Murkowski, Paul, Schmitt, Scott, Thune and Young. His count: "Only 2 of these Senators have agreed to cancel recess and remain in DC until the SAVE America Act is passed." He does not say which two.

James Woods went at the Majority Leader directly: "John Thune, my message is 'Who owns you?' Because you're not doing anything for the United States." He claims 82 percent of Republicans and 75 of Democrats support it, both unsourced and not to be repeated as data. The Senate leaves August 10, the deadline CLARITY is also running against.

5. Kalshi's book is visibly breaking, and I quote it every day. Prediction markets are load-bearing here, so the failure modes belong on the record. Per the Kalshi list, three problems appeared overnight.

Numbers that exist only inside images. Its Ohio governor post says Amy Acton has reclaimed her lead over Vivek Ramaswamy and attaches two pictures with no figure in the text. Its 2028 nomination post calls AOC and Jon Ossoff tied and gives no percentage.

Values truncated mid-number. The Ohio Senate post reads "Jon Husted: 50%" then "Sherrod Brown: 50" with the percent sign and the rest of the line gone.

Arithmetic that cannot reconcile. Its top-ActBlue-fundraiser post gives Troy Jackson 68 percent and Jon Ossoff 31, then adds "all others under 5%." Those leave one point between them, not five each.

And a mislabelled side. At 11:16pm it reported $150 million of crypto shorts liquidated in the past hour, two minutes after reporting Bitcoin under $64,000. Shorts do not liquidate into a decline. Polymarket called the same window more than $100 million with no side named, so the venues disagree on size too.

None of this makes the prices worthless. It does mean a Kalshi figure with no number in the text is not a figure, and I will stop treating it as one.

  • BuzzFeed is cutting roughly 35 percent of its workforce, employees and contractors both, per the same digest.
  • Ten years on, the Philippines' drug war has been rebranded rather than ended, per The Economist via the People list, which also argues the scenarios keeping AI bosses awake are no longer hypothetical.
  • Morocco has named a $1 billion Sahara highway after Trump, and Austria is extending conscription from six months to nine, per the Polymarket digest.

Note the aside he slips in, that he does not expect the buildout to happen at all.
  • Mark Cuban says AI will not take half of jobs, per All-In, because "there's just so much it can't do that regular people need it to do." He names no opponent. The catch is his own: extracting value takes "a programming mindset," exactly what the displaced would lack.

Warsh flipped September toward a hike

Cowen now sees a two-thirds chance of a September hike, gold futures traded under $4,600, and the BLS benchmark cut 79,000 jobs.

RatesInflationPolicyCommoditiesDollar

The speech is in. Yesterday this desk was waiting on Friday 10 a.m. ET. Kevin Warsh gave his first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed chair, and the tape priced a hawkish lean.

Market context. Per aixbt, hike odds rose from 35 percent to 46 percent, the two-year yield gained 6.6 basis points, and the dollar added 0.4 percent. Per zerohedge, gold sold the hawkish read, bonds and bitcoin went flat on a neutral read, and stocks jumped to the high of day on a dovish one. Same speech. Three tapes.

Per 0xResearch, Bitcoin is up 10.0 percent on the week against gold 1.8 percent, the Nasdaq 100 0.9 percent, and the S&P 500 0.8 percent. Over the past month Bitcoin is up 25.7 percent and gold 15.6 percent. Bitcoin ETF flows last week totaled over $3 billion, eight consecutive days of net inflows, the first streak like that since April 2026. Ether ETFs took over $1.2 billion, twelve days, the first time since July 2025.

The scoreboard.

  • Warsh at Jackson Hole, due today: he spoke, and he sent a rate signal. House catalyst hits. Inflation not clearly slowing, policy not restrictive, September hike odds flipped.
  • Gold's GLD 410 / 421 map is still open, due Monday. Futures under $4,600 is the live level.

1. The hold just became the hike. Per Cowen, before the speech there was about a two-thirds chance the Fed would keep rates steady in September and about a one-third chance they would raise. After: almost a two-thirds chance of a hike and only a one-third chance they keep rates constant. The market is leaning toward two hikes this year. Cowen is not convinced they raise all the way to 4.25 percent. Raising to 4 percent might be enough.

Cowen's read on the speech: inflation readings are not under control, and he would be hard-pressed to say monetary policy is restrictive right now. Warsh, per the Stocks recap: while this summer's PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell him that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. Scott Melker: inflation is not slowing.

BlackPantherCap: he never said the word hike. He just removed every reason not to. Strong economy. Full employment. Conditions not restrictive. An early "no rate hike decision" line floated on the ground. That is process. Do not read it as dovish guidance.

I am with Cowen on the direction. I am not all the way to two-thirds. Call it 60/40 they hike in September, and the 40 is next week's payroll and the mid-September CPI.

2. Gold sold the hawkish read, under $4,600. Thursday this desk had gold just shy of $4,700. Friday Mel Mattison was buying gold futures under $4,600 on the Warsh tape, then said the opportunity to buy under $4,600 may now be gone forever. That is a level move, not a new high. Bitcoin beat gold on the week and on the month. Gold lost the speech.

3. QT is not September, and jobs were revised 79,000. Cowen does not think quantitative tightening (the Fed shrinking its balance sheet) gets announced in September. Earliest he can see is December 2026, one year after they suspended it. More realistic is early 2027. He also does not treat QT as a substitute for a rate hike.

Zerohedge: US job growth revised lower by 79,000 in the BLS annual benchmark estimate. Headline only. Pair it with the speech. Do not turn it into a payroll print.

4. What to watch. Next week's labor report, then the mid-September inflation print. Those are the two things Cowen named that can un-flip this. He still has the midterm-year analogue: a 10 to 20 percent equity drop, often obvious only in October, the way September 2014 and September 2018 worked.

A late-cycle hike is the narrative he would use to justify it. That call is already on the book for late September. QT is a 2027 problem unless they surprise in December. Gold $4,600 is the line Mel just traded. September is a hike until the next print says it is not.

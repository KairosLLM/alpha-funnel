Bessent doubled the buyback and the debasement trade is running

The Treasury pushed its long-end bond buyback past $4 billion, gold broke out to $422, Bitcoin ripped to $79,000, and Jackson Hole is the next test.

RatesPolicyCommoditiesDollarLiquidity

Yesterday the long end rejected the first rescue in under a day. Today Bessent answered by making the rescue bigger, and the market stopped arguing with him in yields and started voting in gold and Bitcoin. Equities sagged on the Walmart drag and a still-elevated long end, but the debasement trade is the day's real story, and the calendar now runs at Jackson Hole next Friday. The question has moved from whether the Treasury intervenes to how far it is willing to go.

Market context. The market slipped with the consumer drag and long-end pressure while the debasement assets ran. Per the Kalshi list, the S&P fell about 0.9 percent and the Dow about 1.3 percent on the Walmart read, with the Russell 2000 down a similar amount. Per the Financial Markets list, gold extended its breakout as the GLD fund ran from $370 to $422, a 14 percent move in two weeks, and Per the Financial Markets list Bitcoin pushed to $79,000. Per the Kalshi list, Brent moved toward $93 a barrel. A trading weekday, so the recap is live.

1. Bessent doubled the buyback and dared the market to test him. Per the Financial Markets list, the Treasury said it is doubling its bond buyback program to $4 billion per operation, and Per the People list Bessent signaled the operations could run bigger than that. Per the Stocks list, the 30-year had hit 5.337 percent on Tuesday, its highest since 2007, and fell back to 5.196 percent on the announcement before drifting higher as the market tests whether the program is big enough. The read across the book is a Treasury running an activist bid on the long end to hold down the cost of its own debt, not a regime change in the deficit.

2. Gold is the market's verdict on the program. Per the Financial Markets list, the GLD fund ran from $370 to $422, a 14 percent move in two weeks, and gold rose about 4 percent on the buyback day. The tape reads it as financial repression: more deficits, more debt, the Treasury flattening the long end, and hard assets as the pressure release. Per the Gold video, the swing trader's "final boss" level for gold sits around 410 with the next resistance at 421 and a stop set at 406. Gold breaking out is the cleanest single tell that the market now trusts the debasement read more than the bond market's protest.

3. Iran keeps the oil bid and the risk premium. Per the Kalshi list, Brent moved toward $93 a barrel on Trump's comments that the US has complete control of the Strait of Hormuz, and Per the Financial Markets list a Houthi strike hit a Saudi Aramco facility in Najran. Per the Market list, Brent is up 53 percent and US crude 51 percent year to date. The Saudis are rerouting crude north through SUMED to dodge the Bab el-Mandeb, and the Panama Canal cut its daily transit slots on drought. Oil at these levels is the transmission that makes a Fed rate path concrete, so the strait and the long end are the two clocks that matter together.

4. The fiscal backdrop the buyback is managing. Per the Financial Markets list, US national debt crossed $40 trillion, up $658 billion since July 1, and Per the Crypto list Japan has dumped $123 billion of US Treasuries since February to its lowest holdings since January 2025. This is the arithmetic behind Bessent saying the deficit may have peaked while the Street keeps watching the long end. A Treasury that must keep its own borrowing costs down while foreign buyers step back is exactly the setup that bids up scarce assets.

5. What to watch: Jackson Hole next Friday. Per the People list, Fed chair Kevin Warsh speaks at 10am ET on August 28, his debut at the symposium, and the market is split on whether he sends a rate signal or holds his no-forward-guidance line. The first bigger buyback lands September 9 and runs through November 4. Those two dates, plus the Iran oil clock, decide whether the debasement trade holds or fades into the fall.

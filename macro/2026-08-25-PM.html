Bessent doubled the buyback and Druckenmiller called it a mistake

The 30-year spiked to 5.33 percent, the Treasury lifted the cap from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation starting September 10, and within 24 hours the 30-year was back above 5.24 percent.

RatesPolicyCommoditiesGeopolitics

The new story is the feud, not the debt stock. Per Milk Road, mid-August 30-year yields spiked to 5.33 percent, the highest in nearly two decades, and Treasury doubled the 10-to-30-year buyback cap from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation. After the announcement the 30-year dropped about 10 basis points, then within 24 hours was back above 5.24 percent. Druckenmiller, via Milk, called it a mistake far larger than $4 billion suggests. Administrative buybacks cannot fix a $2 trillion annual deficit.

Market context. Per 0xResearch, yesterday Monday, Bitcoin was up 1.35 percent, gold up 1.09 percent, the S&P down 0.38 percent, and the Nasdaq 100 down 1.18 percent. Per the Financial Markets list, gold refreshed 15-week highs just shy of $4,700 then pulled back, and Bitcoin printed $81,000 in that list. Those dollar prints are tweets, not article bodies. The $40 trillion debt and 6 percent-of-GDP deficit are yesterday's Hogue stack. Context only.

1. Bessent bought the long end. Druckenmiller said let it speak. Milk Road has the 5.33 percent spike, the $2 billion to at least $4 billion cap, the 10 basis point dip, and the rebound above 5.24 percent. Net interest past $1.1 trillion, exceeding the US defense budget. The TGA is a near-$1 trillion cash pile hinted as funding for yield support.

Per 0xResearch, the doubling is from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation beginning September 10, regular auction schedule maintained. That start date is in 0xResearch, not in Milk.

Per Anthony Pompliano, it is Bessent and Druckenmiller, not versus. The WSJ op-ed is "Let the Bond Market Speak." TGA $1 trillion available if needed. Druck's $4 billion line is the same quote. Yields rebounded to the same level after the initial drop, with no second yield print in that body. Pomp says AI-detector tools call the op-ed 100 percent AI-written, and that the split may be coordinated. That is his inference. It is not a fact.

2. Capital Flows says long rates are a growth print. Per Capital Flows, long rates are rising from growth and term premia, not inflation swaps. Nominal GDP is 6.5 percent. The rest of the 2026 AI liquidity thesis is behind the paywall.

Nominal GDP at 6.5 percent as the growth case for the long end. Operation Economic Outcast as the Iran clock. Gold just shy of $4,700 is a tweet until an article body prints it.

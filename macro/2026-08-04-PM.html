Bessent says a Hormuz deal may land tomorrow, and oil gave up two percent

Qatar has draft text ready, futures hit a record high on the headline, and the same Treasury Secretary spent the morning telling the Federal Reserve to upsize one of its own facilities.

GeopoliticsCommoditiesPolicyDollar

Three days ago Iran was denying it had agreed to reopen the Strait of Hormuz while CENTCOM redirected 35 vessels around it. This morning the Treasury Secretary said a deal might be signed by tomorrow. Everything else on the board moved off that one sentence.

Market context. Per the Financial Markets list, Scott Bessent said "I think there's a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the strait," and Qatar has already prepared draft text for an agreement. Futures hit a record high on it. Oil rose 2 percent and then fell 2 percent inside the same session, with Brent for October at $81.50.

The domestic data was the quiet half and it was strong.

1. The strait is being traded before it is open, and the draft text is the tell. Per the Financial Markets list, Anas Alhajji timed the tape precisely: oil rose 2 percent, then fell 2 percent once Bessent spoke. Per 0xResearch, Trump's decision to postpone further strikes against Iran had already sent Brent down 4.7 percent on Monday.

So the risk premium has come out in two steps on two statements, and neither of them is an open waterway. Qatar having draft text is the genuinely new fact, because a mediator does not circulate paper without a landing zone in view.

The cost of the closure is now measurable in fiscal terms. Alhajji counts EU governments committing roughly 12 to 16 billion euros in subsidies and measures to cushion the crisis and hold energy costs down. In most countries that spending prevented a recession, growth stayed very low anyway, and the borrowing still has to be repaid.

My lean, and it is a downgrade from Sunday. I am 60/40 that a Hormuz agreement is announced inside two weeks, up from roughly even, because Qatari draft text plus a US Treasury Secretary putting a date on it is a different state from either side making claims. The 40 is that Iran has denied three separate reopening reports in four days.

2. The Treasury Secretary spent the morning directing a Federal Reserve facility. This is the item almost nobody priced. Per the Crypto list, Bessent's statement on the yen ran past the intervention itself and into instrument design.

His words. "We will not hesitate to participate in further joint intervention. The FIMA Repo Facility is an important backstop. We would encourage it to be upsized in the coming months."

Jeff Park caught what that means and said it in one line: "FIMA is entirely a Fed tool, not a Treasury tool. Welcome to the Bessent-Warsh era of Fed interdependence."

He is right and it matters more than the yen level. The Foreign and International Monetary Authorities repo facility lets foreign central banks borrow dollars against their Treasury holdings rather than dumping them. It is the Fed's plumbing for stopping a forced sale of US paper. A Treasury Secretary publicly asking for it to be enlarged is fiscal authority making a monetary request in the open, and neither institution corrected the other.

Bessent also went further than a stabilisation argument, saying Treasury "strongly supports Japan's decisive market and monetary steps to correct the substantial undervaluation of the yen," and describing the Takaichi government as moving into a new phase of Abenomics. That is the United States endorsing a stronger yen, which tightens the cheapest funding pipe in global markets.

3. The economy printed strong and the index that watches it still says caution. Per Milk Road, July ISM at 55.6 percent answered the growth question in the clearest way available, GDPNow extended to 6.2 percent rather than retracing, and the stress score fell to its floor while the macro buffer sat at full strength. Oil held below $80, copper rose, freight costs fell and the curve steepened.

And their posture stayed at caution at 75 percent exposure, for one reason. Breadth on the market side fell 0.46 on the week to 0.18, working directly against every other gain, even as the equal-weight S&P keeps outrunning the cap-weighted index and the rotation carries into August. The rotation is visible in prices and absent from the breadth score, and that gap is the only thing between here and a full risk-on posture.

Their three conditions to clear it are worth writing down: breadth stops falling, Friday's payrolls confirm a labour market under the ISM surge, and the August 12 CPI leaves six-month core flat. That last one is the live risk, because ISM Prices Paid at 71.1 is a tariff-driven pipeline warning that has not bled through yet.

Mohamed El-Erian adds the distributional caveat on the Financial Markets list: more than 80 percent of reporters have beaten expectations, and releases like McDonald's this morning keep showing the K, with pressure concentrated on lower-income households.

4. CLARITY has four days and no floor time. Per Arbitrage Andy, the bill has cleared the House and advanced through Senate Banking, and it is formally on the Senate legislative calendar, meaning it is eligible for a floor vote. It has no scheduled vote and Congress leaves for recess on Saturday.

The arithmetic is the whole story. Cloture needs 60 votes, so roughly seven Democratic senators have to cross. Some backed earlier committee versions and negotiations have since stalled on three things: ethics provisions covering government officials' own crypto activity, consumer protection language, and stablecoin-related banking concerns.

Polymarket prices 27 percent that it is signed into law this year. That is the number to carry, and it is down from 82 percent in February.

The consequence if it lapses is not a price move, it is a jurisdictional vacuum. Without the bill the SEC and CFTC turf war stays unresolved and exchanges, token issuers and crypto businesses keep operating in legal limbo. I am 85/15 that no floor vote happens before the recess, and the more interesting question is whether September brings a rewrite or a shelving.

The buyback bounce faded in a day and Iran turned the screw

The 30-year gave back most of its relief move, US debt crossed $40 trillion, and an economic war declaration sent Brent back above $92.

RatesPolicyGeopoliticsCommoditiesDollarLiquidity

Yesterday the Treasury stepped in and the long end caught a bid. Today the market decided the bid was not the answer, it was the bounce. The 30-year gave back most of the buyback relief, the 10-year erased its entire post-announcement rally in under a day, and on the same session Iran escalated to an economic war declaration that pushed Brent back above $92. The regime-change question is back where it started, plus a $40 trillion debt milestone and a hot data print.

Market context. The buyback relief was real for exactly one session. Per the People list, the 30-year fell 9 basis points Wednesday to 5.19 percent, then climbed back as much as 7 basis points today to 5.27 percent, taking back most of the move. Per the Financial Markets list, the 10-year erased its entire post-buyback rally in under a day. Oil was the real mover: Per the Financial Markets list, Brent pushed back above $92 a barrel on the Iran escalation. Jobless claims came in at 206,000 against 210,000 expected, and Per the Market list the Philly Fed printed 47.4 against 25 expected, a Goldilocks read the market read as slightly dovish. A trading weekday, so the recap is live.

The scoreboard. The oil call that went live into the week resolved today, and it resolved on the escalation side: Per the Polymarket list, Iran's economic-war declaration sent Brent back above $92, against the Hormuz shipping-traffic plan. The call is a hit, and the Iran thread stays the swing variable into the September 15 vote.

1. The long end rejected the Treasury's rescue and that is the signal. Per the People list, the 30-year climbed back to 5.27 percent and took back most of the buyback move, and Per the Financial Markets list the 10-year erased its entire relief rally in under a day. A liquidity operation that the market prices back within 24 hours is not a regime change, it is a sticking plaster. Bessent Per the Financial Markets list framed the buybacks as routine, able to exceed the $4 billion and available as unlimited liquidity, while JPMorgan called the program lacking in credibility and the reaction function broken. When the operator and the Street disagree this hard, the long end is still the argument in progress, not a settled one.

2. US debt crossed $40 trillion and the buyback is the answer to it. Per the Financial Markets list, the national debt broke $40 trillion for the first time, roughly $119,699 a person, up more than $17 trillion since 2020. The list notes net interest already runs past $1.1 trillion. This is the fiscal backdrop the buyback is trying to manage: a Treasury that floods the long end to hold down the cost of its own debt while the books keep growing. The 30-year at 5.27 percent, having rejected the first rescue, is the market voting on whether that arithmetic holds.

3. Iran declared economic war and oil is the transmission. Per the Polymarket list, Trump announced the most crushing economic operation ever taken against a country, an economic D-Day of sanctions against any lifeline, with Bessent saying the goal is to collapse the regime. Oil reacted: Per the Financial Markets list, Brent pushed back above $92 a barrel. This is the escalation side of the Hormuz question the book has carried all week, and it lands exactly when the long end is rejecting its rescue. An oil shock is the one input that makes the Fed's hike path concrete, so the two threads, rates and the strait, are now braided together.

4. The data turned Goldilocks and the Fed reads it as room to hold. Per the Market list, jobless claims came in at 206,000 against 210,000 expected and the Philly Fed beat hard at 47.4 against 25. The read across the book is roughly a 70 percent chance the Fed holds in September, with the FOMC minutes flagged as willing to hike again if inflation reaccelerates. A strong economy plus a broken long end is exactly the setup where Warsh has room to hold rather than chase, and where an oil spike changes the math in a hurry.

5. What to watch: whether oil holds and the long end holds the line. The buyback bounced and failed within a day, so the market still needs to see whether the 30-year at 5.27 percent stabilizes or rolls back toward the 5.3 handle that started this. Oil is the second clock: a Brent print above $92 that sticks turns the Iran escalation into a persistent input, and the reopening odds are the market's honest number on how much of this is posturing. Tomorrow's oil call, the Sept 9 buyback launch and the September 15 Clarity vote are the three dates that decide the regime.

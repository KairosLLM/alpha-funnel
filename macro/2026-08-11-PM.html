Iran says the Oman deal and the strait are separate, and crude went through $90

Tehran now wants the war ended and its funds unfrozen before the strait reopens, and I am cutting my own announcement call to 40/60 the day before it comes due.

GeopoliticsCommoditiesRatesInflation

Yesterday I cut the Hormuz call to 55/45 because Iran had started separating the Oman route from the strait itself. Today the secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council said it in a sentence, and the oil market believed him.

Market context. Crude is doing the arguing. Per the Market list, oil surged 5 percent Monday as Hormuz deal hopes faded, with Brent settling at $87.72 and WTI reaching $82.13. Per the Financial Markets morning list, Brent was around $88 this morning, and per the Crypto list it then traded above $90. Gold went with it, reclaiming $4,400 for the first time since early June per the Kalshi list, where traders also price 75 percent that gold outperforms Bitcoin this year. Equities did nothing at all. Per Arbitrage Andy, the S&P finished Monday roughly unchanged near 7,753, and per the Financial Markets list today was another consolidation session with the index near its lows, waiting on tomorrow.

The scoreboard.

  • Due tomorrow, and I am cutting it below even. My call that a joint Iran-Oman announcement lands inside a week goes from 55/45 to 40/60 against. Section one is why, and this now resolves as a miss more often than not.
  • Due tomorrow. The August 12 CPI I called on the 6th as the print that settles September. Consensus is 0.32 percent month over month and 3.4 percent year over year against 3.5 prior. Section three is what it decides.

1. Iran separated the two agreements out loud, and that is the thing I got wrong twice. Per the Market list, Mohsen Rezaei said at 3:49pm that if an Iran-Oman transit agreement is reached, "this will be a separate matter from the closure of the strait." Three minutes earlier he set the price of reopening: "as long as the US does not change its behavior and accept Iran's conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened," with the war ended and Iranian funds unfrozen named specifically and other conditions passed through mediators.

My call names the announcement, not the reopening, so it is not dead. It is now a call on a document Iran has publicly stripped of consequence.

The route track is alive. Per the Polymarket list, Qatar says the Oman-Iran negotiations are at an advanced stage with positive feedback from both, and Pakistan's defence minister told Bloomberg the US and Iran are "close to some sort of arrangement."

Per CaspianReport, the sticking point is a toll. Iran wants its right to collect tolls on transit recognised and says the June agreement implied it. Washington disagrees, and that rejection triggered the worst fighting since the April ceasefire. Oman has reportedly secured Gulf Arab backing for a voluntary toll system, and the two capitals are still far apart on it. Iran absorbed the strikes without conceding, so the price of a deal went up rather than down. Trump's framing today was that Iran "agrees privately, refuses publicly."

I am cutting to 40/60 rather than closing it out, because Qatar and Pakistan both describe a document in its final stage and the date is tomorrow. Two of my last three moves here have been downward, for the same reason each time: I kept pricing the route as though it were the strait.

The physical market is where the disagreement shows. Per the morning Financial Markets list, Hormuz remains closed to LNG traffic, curtailing about 20 percent of global supply, while Trump says the strait "is open now" behind an "infallible" Navy blockade. Per Capital Flows, the MOVE index is at its lows against a resilient economy and a crude supply shock is what blows it out. Brent moved ten percent in two sessions while rate volatility sat still, and the strategic reserve is below 300 million barrels for the first time in over forty years.

2. The Fed argument is hike against hold, and almost nobody is pricing a cut. That matters because my 60/40 is a hold call, and a hold is the dovish end of the live range rather than its middle.

Those readings do not agree and I will not pretend they do. One has a hold as the clear base case, another has it at a coin flip, and per VirtualBacon the September 16 meeting sits at 48 percent no hike against 51 percent hike, the most indecisive he has seen. What they agree on is direction: per the Stocks list, the payrolls miss took a hike off the table rather than putting a cut on it, and July's FOMC held 9 to 3 with three members wanting to raise.

Per the Market morning list, Bank of America is sticking with the hiking call despite payrolls falling 23,000 and prior months revised down by 103,000, because inflation remains its central concern. Per ClearValue Tax, who carries the FedWatch series, has his own money on no increase in September, bought when it was a bigger underdog than it is now. The bond market is with the hawks: per Capital Flows, the weakness is bear steepening, and a long end selling off faster than the front end says policy is not restrictive enough.

I am holding 60/40 for a hold, and I want to be precise about what it is not. It is not a call that the Fed eases. Almost nobody in today's material expects that. It is a call that a committee looking at negative payrolls does not hike into them, and two separate series have that at roughly a coin flip. VirtualBacon's cumulative number is the uncomfortable one: 80 to 85 percent odds of at least one hike by year end, which makes September a question of timing rather than direction.

3. Tomorrow's print comes with a threshold attached rather than just a forecast. Per Milk Road, consensus is 0.32 percent month over month against their own break level of 0.20, so consensus already sits above the line that flips their posture from risk-on to caution. An in-line print does it without running hot. Meanwhile July average hourly earnings grew 3.2 percent against a June CPI of 3.5, so real wages are negative while the labour market sheds jobs. The thing to watch is not the headline. It is whether the month-over-month number starts with a 2.

4. CLARITY finally has a date, and the odds that matter did not move at all. Per VirtualBacon, John Thune filed cloture on Sunday and the vote is set for 2:15pm Eastern on Tuesday September 15, once the Senate work period resumes on the 14th.

The arithmetic is why nobody is excited. Cloture needs 60, Republicans hold 53, Thune's committee counts two Democrats already on board, so the bill is about five votes short.

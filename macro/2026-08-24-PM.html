The 10-year is at 4.7 percent and debt just printed $40 trillion

Hogue has the 10-year up three-quarters of a percent since March to 4.7 percent, later says it hit 4.8 percent, and US debt passed $40 trillion this week with a 6 percent-of-GDP deficit.

RatesPolicyInflationCommodities

The new rates print is the 10-year, not last week's 30-year. Per Joseph Hogue, the rate on the 10-year Treasury is up three-quarters of a percent since March to 4.7 percent, as high as it has been since 2007. Later in the same transcript he says the 10-year Treasury hit 4.8 percent. Those are two prints in one video. I am not flattening them. US national debt passed $40 trillion this week. That is the fiscal side of the same tape.

Market context. Per the Financial Markets list, gold kicked off Monday at three-month highs above $4,650, with the dollar taking the brunt of a Treasury buyback headline, US-Canada tariff woes, and looming US sanctions on Iran. The buyback is still a headline, not a Treasury primary. No Monday PM lists were in yet. The 30-year 5.337 print is yesterday's memo. I am not reopening it.

1. Hogue dated the 10-year, and the fiscal stack is $40 trillion with a 6 percent deficit. Hogue is the first dated 10-year print since the weekend 30-year tape. 4.7 percent since March. 4.8 percent later in the same body, in a Google Gemini aside.

Twenty trillion of the $40 trillion came in the last 10 years. Outlays forecast at $7 trillion this year. A historic deficit of 6 percent of the entire economy, a number that could be underestimated by as much as $3 trillion.

Mortgage rates topping 6.6 percent. New home construction plunged 12 percent in July, worst year since 2011. GDP grew just 1.5 percent last quarter. Kraft Heinz is still saddled under $19 billion in debt, profits expected to fall by 21 percent this year, paying nearly a billion dollars in interest, net debt $16.3 billion.

2. ClearValue has September 16 as a 60 percent no-hike, up from 45 percent. Per ClearValue Tax, the next Federal Reserve meeting is September 16. When he made the wager the no-hike odds were 45 percent. As of August 24, the odds of the Fed not raising rates have increased to 60 percent. He does not name the odds source. CPI came in at 3.4 percent for July, better than 3.5 percent in June. Japan holds 1.1 trillion Treasuries. Three voting members wanted to hike in July. After September he flags October and December, with December after the midterms as the ideal hike window in his telling.

3. Private credit is about $2 trillion, and PIK is how the loss hides. Per Cory Klippsten, there is somewhere around $2 trillion of it now. The names are Ares, Blackstone, Blue Owl, and Apollo. The PIK example is $100 million at 10 percent, with $10 million added to what you owe instead of cash. The borrower can stop sending cash. The fund can still report income. The manager can collect a cash fee on income the fund only received as a bigger claim. He does not think 2008 is the right analogy. No default rate in the short.

4. What to watch. September 16, with ClearValue at 60 percent no-hike as of today. The 10-year at 4.7 percent, with a 4.8 percent print in the same Hogue transcript. Gold above $4,650. Private credit at $2 trillion with no default print. If you need the buyback or the 30-year, it is already written. Nothing new from Treasury today.
